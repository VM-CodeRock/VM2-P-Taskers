Executive Summary
Key Insight
The GSA EOA Handbook is the administration's formal playbook for eliminating non-value-add federal work, optimizing essential processes, and automating repetitive tasks. Published June 3, 2026, it operationalizes five executive orders from Trump's second term and gives agencies a step-by-step methodology they are effectively required to follow. For Changeis, this is simultaneously a threat (incumbent scope may be re-scoped or eliminated) and a major opportunity (agencies will need experienced consultants to execute EOA projects).
  • What it is: A 40-page practical playbook (PDF, GSA.gov) developed from lessons learned at GSA, NASA, and the Department of Education during the first Trump term. It defines a four-phase methodology — Opportunity Assessment, Solution Planning & Design, Implementing & Sustaining, and an EOA Toolkit — for agencies to systematically eliminate, optimize, and automate federal work.
  • Why it matters now: The handbook directly responds to five executive orders (14158, 14179, 14210, 14219, 14222) mandating workforce reduction and operational efficiency. Agencies with shrinking headcount now have a formal framework they are expected to apply. GSA's own Million Hour Challenge targets shifting one million hours from low- to high-value work in FY26–FY27.
  • For Changeis BD: EOA creates a new category of consulting demand — agencies need facilitation, process mapping, RPA deployment, and change management. Changeis's existing FAA work (CLMRS, ATEPS-II) overlaps heavily with E, O, and A criteria; both are at risk of scope re-evaluation and both are platforms from which to expand.
  • Threat vector: The "Elimination" pillar explicitly targets duplicative reporting, redundant administrative reviews, and legacy system maintenance — all areas where Changeis has incumbent work. Any task orders perceived as duplicative could be de-scoped before option periods are exercised.
  • Timing context: The handbook lands in a dense policy week: EO 14409 (AI/cybersecurity, June 2) and CISA BOD 26-04 (risk-based patching, June 10) both create related demand for AI-enabled security and automation services. NITAAC sunset (October 29, 2026) simultaneously pushes IT procurement toward GSA vehicles — directly benefiting Changeis's GSA positioning.
GSA EOA Framework Breakdown

The handbook defines EOA through three interconnected lenses applied sequentially. Every federal process or program is assessed against all three categories in a structured four-phase methodology. Source: Federal EOA Playbook v1 (GSA, June 3, 2026).

Pillar 1

Eliminate

  • Remove policies, procedures, and requirements that create unnecessary workload
  • Target: duplicative reports, redundant multi-level reviews, reports no longer used by customers
  • Requires stakeholder facilitation sessions + policy documentation review
  • Must achieve go/no-go consensus before elimination proceeds
  • GSA example: eliminated financial reports no longer needed + second-level reviews that didn't catch discrepancies
  • Key metric: annualized hours of workload eliminated per year
Pillar 2

Optimize

  • Standardize, simplify, and streamline within and across business units
  • Target: duplicative processes, inconsistent procedures, bottlenecked handoffs
  • Tools: value stream mapping, lean, root cause analysis, agile management
  • Process reengineering for mission-critical functions that cannot be eliminated
  • GSA example: 45 process standardization projects in 6 months reduced FTE requirements
  • Key metric: cycle time reduction, error rate improvement, throughput increase
Pillar 3

Automate

  • Deploy emerging technologies to replace manual, repetitive, rules-based work
  • Technologies: RPA, AI/ML, automated dashboards, agentic workflows
  • Must follow Elimination and Optimization — automate only what is already streamlined
  • GSA example: 190+ RPA applications deployed, 2M+ hours saved FY19–25
  • Key metric: automation rate (% of tasks fully automated), cost avoidance
  • CISA BOD 26-04 compliance creates an accelerant for automated patch/vuln reporting

The Four-Phase Implementation Methodology:

~4 weeks

Key Insight
The methodology explicitly borrows from Lean, Root Cause Analysis, and Agile — all capabilities Changeis claims. The handbook is designed to be executed with external facilitation help: agencies need contractors to run value stream mapping sessions, facilitate stakeholder coalitions, and deploy automation. This is not a DIY guide; it's a methodology requiring practitioner support.

Proven GSA Results (FY2019–2025): GSA's own EOA program eliminated 115,000+ hours of annual workload and saved 125,000+ hours through optimization. The enterprise automation program deployed 190+ RPA applications and saved over 2 million cumulative hours. Staff in the OCFO went from 450 to 343 FTEs (a 24% reduction) while Federal Employee Viewpoint scores rose from 66% to 88%. These numbers will be cited by COs and program offices to justify EOA mandates on existing contracts.

Incumbent Contract Analysis — CLMRS & ATEPS-II
Key Insight
Both incumbent FAA contracts contain work that maps to all three EOA pillars — but they face different risk profiles. CLMRS is fundamentally an Optimize/Automate effort and should benefit from EOA alignment. ATEPS-II's administrative support scope is Eliminate-risk and requires proactive repositioning.

CLMRS — $62M FAA Supply Chain Transformation (Changeis announcement, May 2022)

  • EOA alignment — Optimize: Replacing legacy COBOL-based LIS with modern LCSS; re-engineering MSP, FSI, PMM, CDLS supply chain processes. This is core EOA Optimization work — process reengineering and standardization.
  • EOA alignment — Automate: CMA system modernization through software coding/customization is directly aligned with EOA Automate (replacing manual, rules-based work with scalable automation). The Agile/SAFe methodology matches EOA's recommended deployment approach.
  • Risk — Eliminate: If FAA conducts an EOA review of its logistics operations, some COBOL-era manual processes currently supported by the contract may be flagged for elimination rather than modernization, potentially shrinking scope.
  • Opportunity: Position the entire CLMRS contract as an EOA implementation vehicle. Request a joint EOA metrics review with the program office to formally document hours of annual workload automated through LCSS deployment.

ATEPS-II SDB1 — $114.7M FAA Air Traffic Engineering & Program Support (GovCon Wire, December 2024)

  • EOA alignment — at risk (Eliminate): "Business management support," "administrative assistance," and "other support assignments" at ATO PMO directorates are exactly the function types EOA's Eliminate pillar targets. Redundant reporting, second-level reviews, and duplicative coordination are all targeted for removal under the handbook's methodology.
  • EOA alignment — Optimize: Program management support for AJM-1 through AJM-4 directorates involves workflow coordination across multiple directorates — a prime candidate for process standardization and streamlining.
  • EOA alignment — Automate: CISA BOD 26-04 requires FAA (as a federal civilian agency) to automate CDM vulnerability reporting within 60–180 days. Changeis, as ATEPS-II incumbent, can propose automation of compliance dashboards and KEV tracking as a new task order.
  • Contract horizon: Performance period runs through December 1, 2034 — 8+ years remaining. Long enough to see multiple EOA cycles. Changeis must ensure the contract's administrative functions survive the first EOA assessment, which likely occurs in FY26–FY27 based on GSA's Million Hour Challenge timeline.
Risk
The combination of (1) DOGE contract reviews already completed, (2) the EOA Handbook now published as a government-wide tool, and (3) FAA's known budget pressures means that ATO PMO leadership will almost certainly apply EOA criteria to their support contractor portfolio in FY27 planning. Changeis should request a program review meeting now — before the next budget cycle — to get ahead of any scope modifications.
