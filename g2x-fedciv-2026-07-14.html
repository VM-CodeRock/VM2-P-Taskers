USDOT · DHS · GSA · DOI · Treasury · Commerce · VA
Monday, July 14, 2026 — coverage window: past 24-48 hours
1 Agency News, Awards & Policy Shifts
  • Commerce / BIS

    Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security formally published its rule reclassifying the United Arab Emirates from Country Groups D:3/D:4 into the preferential A:5 tier, taking effect July 14, easing licensing for AI chips, satellites, and dual-use hardware bound for G42, Core42, and MGX (CNBC, Ontime+).

  • GSA

    GSA announced FY27 City Pair Program contract awards to eight air carriers, including first-time small-business awardee Key Lime Air, projecting $2.02 billion in annual government travel savings beginning October 1 (GSA newsroom).

  • GSA / Polaris

    GSA issued the notice to proceed for the Polaris SDVOSB Pool phase II on July 7, clearing 17 additional companies for task-order eligibility under the GWAC (GSA Polaris page).

  • GSA / OMB

    The public comment window closed July 13-14 on OMB's proposed "Uniform Grants Regulation," a government-wide rewrite of 2 CFR Part 200 that would require political-appointee pre-issuance review of discretionary awards, expand agency termination-for-convenience authority, and mandate E-Verify and Treasury Do Not Pay checks; dockets were filed in parallel by DHS, DOI, DOT, VA, and 12 other agencies (Granted AI, Wiley Rein).

  • USDOT / FAA

    Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy announced FAA grants totaling $1.776 billion for airport runway rehabilitation, safety upgrades, and terminal improvements nationwide (FAA newsroom).

  • USDOT / FAA

    FAA proposed a rule to replace the 1973 blanket ban on civil supersonic overland flight with a noise-based certification standard, targeting mid-2027 finalization; USDOT separately extended for one year its non-enforcement policy on the flight-number-change refund rule for airlines (Deep Arrival, The Regulatory Review).

  • DHS

    A federal judge ordered DHS to restore state government access to federal citizenship data used for voter-roll screening in Florida, Ohio, Iowa, and Indiana (The Regulatory Review).

  • DHS

    DHS signed a Memorandum of Cooperation with Caricom's IMPACS agency establishing a biometric data-sharing partnership for regional border security (Jamaica Observer).

  • DOI

    Interior finalized a rule removing the regulatory definition of "harm" under the Endangered Species Act, eliminating the principal basis for treating habitat modification alone as unlawful "take" of protected species (AP News).

  • DOI / BLM

    BLM's public comment period on proposed changes to federal grazing regulations closed July 13, with a final recommendation still pending (Deseret News).

  • Treasury

    OFAC revoked General License X and issued GL X1, terminating the June 21 authorization for Iranian crude and petrochemical sales and setting a 10-day wind-down through July 17 (National Law Review).

  • VA

    VA announced its disability compensation and pension claims backlog fell below 70,000 for the first time since February 2020, a 74% reduction since January 2025, alongside centralized payroll processing for all 430,000-plus VA employees (DRG News, VA newsroom).

2 Bid Protests & GAO/OIG Activity
  • GAO / VA

    GAO sustained a protest finding VA's Veterans Health Administration illegally excluded contractor past-performance records older than three years from a $2.4M acquisition-support-services evaluation, despite RFQ language permitting reduced-weight consideration of older data (GovConFeed).

  • GAO / DHS-USCIS

    Echelon Services withdrew its GAO protest of a $39M USCIS Quality, Security, Testing, Automation and Governance (QSTAG) services award on July 9, closing the challenge without a merits decision (Orange Slices).

  • GAO / NASA SEWP VI

    NASA cleared the final protest obstacle on its roughly $60B SEWP VI GWAC after GAO denied the last of 12 challenges; the sole remaining action is a reconsideration request GAO is likely to deny (Orange Slices).

  • Oversight / CIGIE

    House Oversight Chairman James Comer sent CIGIE a letter demanding documents by July 15, warning the committee may move to strip the Council's authority to investigate misconduct within inspector general offices after a GAO finding that the IG system is failing to police itself (The Investigative Journal).
