Headline 1 — OpenAI / IPO Filing

OpenAI Confirms S-1 Filed with SEC This Morning — 900M Weekly Users, $25B Revenue, NYSE Listing Targets September at $852B–$1T

Sources: Instagram / OpenAI · AI to ROI · Euronews · May 23, 2026

OpenAI filed its S-1 prospectus with the Securities and Exchange Commission on the morning of May 23, 2026, targeting a New York Stock Exchange listing in September at a valuation between $852 billion and $1 trillion. The S-1 discloses 900 million weekly active ChatGPT users, $25 billion in annualized revenue, and enterprise contracts with "the largest companies and governments on earth." Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are lead underwriters; Latham & Watkins is advising the banks; Cooley and Wachtell Lipton are advising OpenAI. An Elon Musk lawsuit alleging OpenAI abandoned its nonprofit mission was dismissed by a jury on May 19, removing the last major legal obstacle to listing. CFO Sarah Friar has cautioned company leaders that internal systems may require additional preparation time, leaving open the possibility of a slip from September to October.

The S-1 signals that OpenAI's roadmap — including autonomous AI agents, research systems, and hardware — will be disclosed to public markets for the first time, creating pricing and competitive transparency that has not previously existed. The filing comes three days after SpaceX's public S-1 and ahead of Anthropic's projected October IPO target, compressing the three-way AI public-markets race into a six-month window. Prediction markets place the probability of OpenAI listing before Anthropic at 85%.

Federal procurement officers should anticipate that contract terms negotiated with OpenAI pre-IPO may be renegotiated post-listing as the company optimizes for public market metrics. Changeis should also track how the S-1's enterprise contract disclosures define the competitive landscape for federal AI prime contractors — the disclosed government deal scope will be the first hard data on OpenAI's federal revenue concentration.

Lead Story — White House / Executive Order

Trump Kills AI Executive Order After Direct Calls from Musk, Zuckerberg & Sacks — White House Says Revised Version Coming "Early Next Week"

Sources: Politico · Build Fast with AI · Yahoo News / Politico · May 22, 2026

President Trump formally withdrew the draft AI executive order on Thursday, May 22, hours before a scheduled signing ceremony and with AI lab executives reportedly already en route to the White House. Axios reported the proximate cause: Elon Musk, Mark Zuckerberg, and AI czar David Sacks each called Trump directly between Wednesday night and Thursday morning. Sacks argued the voluntary 90-day pre-launch review framework could slow industry pace, jeopardize the U.S. edge over China, and risk evolving from voluntary to mandatory. Trump told reporters he had "many" concerns and did not want to "authorize anything until it is properly finalized," adding: "I felt it was hindering the industry."

White House officials and two industry representatives indicated a revised executive order will emerge, led by National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross. One industry representative expected "more clarity early next week." The collapsed order had included NSA involvement in classified frontier model testing and addressed oversight of Anthropic's Mythos-class models specifically. The CISA/NIST pre-release evaluation agreements already signed with Google DeepMind, Microsoft, and xAI remain in effect and represent the working federal AI oversight mechanism in the interim. (Politico)

Why it matters to Changeis The EO collapse confirms that near-term federal AI governance will be defined by bilateral NIST/CISA evaluation agreements rather than a statutory or executive framework. Federal agencies that built compliance or procurement planning around an anticipated EO must now revert to the existing NIST AI RMF and agency-specific ATO pathways. For Changeis clients in national security AI work, the survival of NSA involvement language in the draft EO suggests cybersecurity model evaluation will remain a live policy priority even without a signed order. Monitoring the revised EO draft — expected within days — is a critical action item this weekend.

Anthropic Project Glasswing: 10,000+ Critical Vulnerabilities Found in Month One — wolfSSL CVE Affects Billions of Devices

  • Scale in one month: Approximately 50 partners used Claude Mythos Preview as part of Project Glasswing. Most partners individually found hundreds of critical- or high-severity vulnerabilities each; collectively, more than ten thousand were identified. (Anthropic)
  • Cloudflare results: Cloudflare found 2,000 bugs across critical-path systems, including 400 high- or critical-severity bugs, with a false positive rate Cloudflare's team judged better than human testers.
  • Open-source scanning: Anthropic scanned more than 1,000 open-source projects. Of 1,752 high- or critical-rated vulnerabilities assessed by independent security firms, 90.6% (1,587) were valid true positives and 62.4% (1,094) were confirmed high- or critical-severity. At current rates, Mythos Preview is on track to surface nearly 3,900 high- or critical-severity vulnerabilities in open-source code alone.
  • wolfSSL CVE-2026-5194: Mythos Preview detected a vulnerability in wolfSSL, an open-source cryptography library used by billions of devices worldwide, and constructed a working exploit capable of forging certificates for bank and email provider domains. The vulnerability has been patched; full technical analysis will be published in coming weeks.
  • Bottleneck is now human patching capacity: Anthropic states the constraint is no longer finding vulnerabilities but triaging, designing, and deploying patches. Average patch time for a high- or critical-severity Mythos-found bug is two weeks. Of 530 disclosed high/critical bugs, only 75 are patched and 827 confirmed vulnerabilities are still awaiting disclosure.
  • Fraud prevention: Mythos Preview helped detect and prevent a fraudulent $1.5 million wire transfer at a Glasswing partner bank after a threat actor compromised a customer's email account and made spoof phone calls.
  • Claude Security in public beta: Claude Opus 4.7 patched over 2,100 vulnerabilities in three weeks during the beta period. Available now for Claude Enterprise customers.
Supply Chain Alert — GitHub / TeamPCP

GitHub Confirms 3,800 Internal Repos Stolen via Poisoned VS Code Extension — OpenAI, Mistral, European Commission Also Hit

Sources: Build Fast with AI · May 20, 2026

GitHub confirmed on May 20 that approximately 3,800 internal repositories were exfiltrated by threat actor TeamPCP (also tracked as UNC6780 by Google Threat Intelligence Group). The attack vector was a trojanized version of the Nx Console VS Code extension — 2.2 million installs, verified publisher status — which was live in the Visual Studio Marketplace for exactly 18 minutes (12:30-12:48 PM UTC, May 18) before removal. The malicious version targeted 1Password vaults, Anthropic Claude Code configurations, npm tokens, GitHub tokens, and AWS credentials. GitHub CISO Alexis Wales stated there is no evidence of customer data exposure from outside internal repositories.

The same TeamPCP group launched its initial campaign on May 11, compromising TanStack's router ecosystem and spreading across 170+ npm packages and two PyPI packages (CVE-2026-45321, CVSS 9.6). Confirmed victims include OpenAI — which had two employee devices compromised, triggered certificate rotation, and is revoking its macOS app-signing certificate on June 12 — as well as Mistral AI (one developer device, $25,000 Monero extortion demand for alleged 5 GB source code leak) and the European Commission's public website. Trend Micro tracked at least seven confirmed TeamPCP waves in 2026.

Why it matters to Changeis An 18-minute supply chain compromise affecting 2.2 million-install developer tooling is a material threshold event for any organization using VS Code extensions in development pipelines — including federal IT shops under FedRAMP and FISMA requirements. Changeis clients running Claude Code or GitHub-integrated CI/CD environments should audit VS Code extension inventories and verify 1Password vault integrity. The OpenAI macOS certificate revocation on June 12 requires active application reinstallation by enterprise ChatGPT users. The European Commission compromise signals that TeamPCP is not limiting targets to commercial entities.

Research Milestone — OpenAI / Mathematics

OpenAI Model Autonomously Disproves 80-Year-Old Erdos Conjecture with 125-Page Proof — Fields Medalist Calls It "a Milestone in AI Mathematics"

Sources: Build Fast with AI · May 20, 2026

OpenAI announced on May 20 that one of its internal general-purpose reasoning models autonomously disproved the Erdős unit distance conjecture — a central problem in discrete geometry first posed by Paul Erdős in 1946. The model produced a 125-page proof establishing an infinite family of planar point configurations that produce significantly more unit-distance pairs than square-grid arrangements. The proof used algebraic number theory, specifically the Golod-Shafarevich criterion, connecting geometry to infinite class field towers. The result was confirmed in a companion paper published the same day. Fields medalist Tim Gowers called it "a milestone in AI mathematics." Princeton mathematician Will Sawin quantified the improvement: the best configurations now scale as n^(1+delta), with delta >= 0.014. Noga Alon called it "an outstanding achievement." The model was not trained specifically for the problem, did not retrieve an existing solution, and was not guided step-by-step by humans.

Why it matters to Changeis An unsupervised AI proof of a problem that stumped elite mathematicians for 80 years establishes a new benchmark for autonomous scientific reasoning capability — one that will directly inform how DoD, DARPA, NIH, and national laboratories evaluate AI for open-ended research tasks. Federal agencies currently scoping AI pilot programs in scientific research, signals intelligence analysis, or intelligence synthesis should treat this result as evidence that autonomous reasoning capability has crossed a threshold relevant to their use cases.
