Decision Detail — Cavan Solutions (Controlling 2026 Precedent)
CONTROLLING PRECEDENT 2026 WL 1284037 (O.D.R.A. 2026)  ·  SETIS Program  ·  Published: May 15, 2026 (NLR / JD Supra)
Protest of Cavan Solutions, Inc.
Protester: Cavan Solutions, Inc.  |  Intervenor/Awardee: Advanced Sciences & Technology, LLC (AS&T)  |  Forum: FAA ODRA
Moot + No-Protest Clause Struck

BID PROTEST SYSTEMS ENGINEERING / IT OCI — FORMER FAA EMPLOYEES SETIS IDIQ ATEPS-II ANALOGOUS

Background

Cavan Solutions and AS&T both hold contracts under FAA's Systems Engineering and Technical Innovative Solutions (SETIS) IDIQ — structurally similar to Changeis's ATEPS-II vehicle. After the FAA product team awarded a task order valued at approximately $23.8 million to AS&T, Cavan filed an initial and supplemental protest alleging organizational conflicts of interest: former FAA employees had become AS&T employees and allegedly leveraged insider knowledge to influence the competition.

Corrective Action — Core Protest Relief

Following investigation, the contracting officer concluded there was "ample evidence" of an "unmitigable, unavoidable OCI" that could not be waived. The product team moved to exclude AS&T from the competition, terminate its task order, and re-award to Cavan. This corrective action rendered the protest's core OCI claims substantially moot. The ODRA denied Cavan's additional request for enhanced remedies (IG referral, suspension proceedings, default termination), reasoning that the product team's good-faith corrective action was adequate and that the product team was better positioned to assess further remedial measures.

Precedent-Setting Holding — No-Protest Clause Unenforceable

The case's most consequential ruling addressed SETIS Clause G.8.3, which declared task order award decisions "final and conclusive" and non-protestable (except where task order value exceeded $25M or involved scope/period/ceiling). The ODRA struck down Clause G.8.3 on three independent statutory grounds:

(a) 49 U.S.C. §40110(d)(4): The FAA's organic bid protest statute mandates that protests shall be adjudicated by the administrator through ODRA. A contractual clause stripping ODRA of jurisdiction directly contravenes this mandatory language.

(b) FASA inapplicability to FAA: DOD and civilian agencies can restrict task order protests under 10 U.S.C. §3406(f) and 41 U.S.C. §4106(f). But Congress expressly exempted the FAA from FASA via 49 U.S.C. §40110(d)(2) — the FAA cannot use a statutory exception Congress declined to extend to it.

(c) 5 U.S.C. §575(a)(3): "Final and conclusive" clauses without judicial review function as arbitration agreements, violating the federal prohibition on requiring arbitration consent as a contract condition.

Transparency Ruling

The ODRA rejected the product team's request to suppress the public version of its prior interlocutory order, citing DOT guidance encouraging publication of formal adjudication decisions. The ODRA noted that FAA's insulation from GAO protest review makes public transparency especially important to demonstrate impartiality.

Changeis Implication — ATEPS-II SETIS and ATEPS-II are structurally analogous FAA IDIQ programs. If ATEPS-II task order solicitations contain a Clause G.8.3 equivalent — making TO awards "final and conclusive" or non-protestable below certain thresholds — that clause is now unenforceable per Cavan Solutions. Changeis has unlimited right to protest any ATEPS-II TO award it was excluded from. Competitors have the same right to protest Changeis-won TOs. BD and Legal must audit all pending ATEPS-II task order RFPs for such clauses and ensure proposal quality is defensible on the record. Target completion: June 13, 2026.
