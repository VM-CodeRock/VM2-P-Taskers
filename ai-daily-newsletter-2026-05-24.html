Top Story — Google / I/O 2026

Google I/O 2026: Gemini 3.5 Flash to GA, Gemini Spark Always-On Agent, Antigravity 2.0 Dev Suite — AGI "a Few Years Away," Demis Says

Sources: The Prompt Factory · Unrot Weekly · Tech Bytes May 24 · May 19–24, 2026

Google's I/O 2026 keynote was the most product-dense AI event in the company's history. Gemini 3.5 Flash reached general availability on May 19, priced at $1.50 per million input tokens and $9 per million output tokens with a 5-million-token context window and 40% speed improvement over Gemini 3.0 Flash; it outperforms Gemini 3.1 Pro on coding and agentic benchmarks and runs 12x faster inside Antigravity than comparable frontier models. Google Gemini Spark — a $100/month always-on personal AI agent that runs on Google Cloud even when the user's device is closed — launches next week for US subscribers; it works across Gmail, Sheets, Docs, Drive, and Calendar, and gains MCP support within weeks for third-party integrations including Canva, Instacart, and OpenTable. Gemini Omni ships natively into YouTube Shorts, treating text, image, audio, and video as first-class inputs in a single model. Google Antigravity 2.0 — a full agentic dev suite with desktop app, CLI, and SDK — ships at a $100/month tier. Google removed all daily prompt limits for AI Ultra subscribers (now $100/month, down from $250/month). Demis Hassabis told the keynote audience that AGI is "a few years away," while the Gemini app reports 900 million monthly active users processing 9.7 trillion tokens every month.

At the same I/O, Google confirmed Android XR smart glasses for autumn priced at $600–$900 in partnership with Samsung, Warby Parker, and Gentle Monster. Search now builds bespoke mini-apps powered by Antigravity. Google also added its latest Gemini model to the GenAI.mil classified platform, where users have already built more than 100,000 AI agents according to Defense One; separately, eight AI firms — not including Anthropic — were cleared to provide tools for classified Pentagon networks in a diversification move intended to prevent vendor lock-in.

Why it matters to Changeis Google's unified pivot — same model, all modalities, every surface, every price tier — is the most aggressive platform-consolidation play in the AI market. Federal agencies that have been evaluating Gemini for sensitive use cases will find that the same model powering consumer Google Search now powers GenAI.mil. For Changeis BD: (1) The GenAI.mil penetration and 100,000+ agent count signals that DoD has moved well past pilot phase — proposal responses should assume existing AI infrastructure, not greenfield; (2) The exclusion of Anthropic from the new classified-network approved list (eight firms cleared, Anthropic not among them) is a material procurement signal for clients relying on Claude in government environments; (3) MCP's adoption by both Google Spark and Anthropic Claude Managed Agents cements it as the de facto agent-to-tool protocol — Changeis should standardize integration architectures around MCP now.
Model & Product Updates
  • Anthropic / Compute

    Anthropic Signs $40B GPU Deal with xAI's Colossus 1 — $1.25B/Month for 300 MW, 220K H100/H200/GB200 GPUs in Memphis

    Anthropic committed $1.25 billion per month — up to $40 billion over four years — for the entire 300 MW output of xAI's Colossus 1 facility near Memphis, Tennessee, gaining access to approximately 220,000 H100, H200, and GB200 GPUs. The deal was made possible because Grok demand has slipped and Colossus was underutilized. Colossus 1's underlying real estate was simultaneously acquired by a SpaceX subsidiary from Phoenix Investors for $185 million, consolidating the site fully under Elon Musk's corporate umbrella. Anthropic's total compute spend now approaches $1.5 billion per month. (The Prompt Factory · Yahoo Finance)

    The Anthropic–xAI compute arrangement is one of the largest infrastructure partnerships in AI history and directly shapes Anthropic's ability to serve enterprise contracts. For federal procurement: Anthropic's compute dependency on SpaceX/xAI infrastructure introduces a supply-chain relationship that acquisition officers should flag under FAR conflict-of-interest provisions when Anthropic tools are specified in government solicitations.
  • Anthropic / Claude Updates

    Claude Managed Agents Ships Self-Hosted Sandboxes (Public Beta) and MCP Tunnels (Research Preview) at Code with Claude London

    Anthropic shipped two new Claude Managed Agents capabilities at the Code with Claude London event: self-hosted sandboxes in public beta (agent orchestration stays on Anthropic infrastructure while code, filesystem, and network egress run inside the customer's perimeter), and MCP tunnels in research preview. The UK AI Security Institute confirmed Claude Mythos as the first model to solve both of its cyber ranges end-to-end. An updated Glasswing report noted that approximately 50 partners using Claude Mythos Preview found more than 10,000 high- and critical-severity vulnerabilities in a month, with Cloudflare finding 2,000 bugs at a false-positive rate better than human testers. (The Prompt Factory)

    Self-hosted sandboxes for Claude Managed Agents directly address the most common blocker for federal AI deployment: data residency and network segmentation requirements. Changeis should evaluate this capability against FedRAMP and IL4/IL5 requirements for clients exploring Claude-based agentic workflows.
  • OpenAI / Enterprise

    OpenAI Launches Guaranteed Capacity SKU and Partners with Dell to Deploy Codex On-Premises for Banks, Insurers, and Defense

    OpenAI launched Guaranteed Capacity, a new product that sells reserved compute as a paid SKU through the enterprise team, allowing organizations to lock in long-term compute at discounted rates. Separately, OpenAI and Dell Technologies announced a partnership to deploy Codex in on-premises and hybrid enterprise environments, with defense buyers, banks, and insurers as primary targets. GPT-5.5 Instant is quietly being made the default in ChatGPT. OpenAI also posted a $445,000 safety researcher role focused on AI self-improvement, and OpenAI Codex now supports "Locked Use" on macOS — controlling the device even when the screen is locked for long-running developer tasks. (AI Weekly · The Prompt Factory)

    The Dell partnership brings OpenAI Codex to on-premises environments — a direct play for defense and IC customers with air-gap requirements. Changeis should assess this as a competing offering to Anthropic's self-hosted sandboxes for customers requiring disconnected AI coding environments.
  • Microsoft / Enterprise

    Microsoft Cancels Claude Code Licenses for Windows/Teams/Outlook/Surface Engineers — Shifts to GitHub Copilot CLI by End of June

    Microsoft has begun canceling many internal Claude Code licenses in its Experiences + Devices division, affecting engineers working on Windows, Teams, Outlook, and Surface products, with most access ending by June 30, 2026. The reason cited is token consumption costs reaching hundreds to thousands of dollars per developer per month. Teams are being redirected to GitHub Copilot CLI as the primary alternative; Microsoft says it continues to offer Anthropic models through Microsoft Foundry but prioritizes tools aligned with Azure and GitHub infrastructure. (LinkedIn)

    Microsoft's internal migration from Claude Code to Copilot is a real-world cost management signal for any large organization running agentic AI coding tools. Enterprise budgets for AI coding assistants at scale are not trivial — this decision will be cited in competitive procurement conversations as evidence that open-market AI tool consolidation is underway.

Anthropic on Track to Close $30B Round at $900B Valuation — Surpasses OpenAI in Private Market Capitalization

Sources: Unrot Weekly · Agentic AI Hype · Bloomberg/FT (confirmed week of May 26)

Bloomberg and the Financial Times confirmed Anthropic is on track to close a $30 billion funding round at a $900 billion valuation — surpassing OpenAI's $852 billion private market valuation to become the highest-valued private AI company in the world. The round is co-led by Sequoia Capital, Dragoneer Investment Group, Altimeter Capital, and Greenoaks Capital Partners, each contributing approximately $2 billion. Peter Thiel's Founders Fund and General Catalyst are also participating. The valuation is anchored in part by a $45 billion compute contract with SpaceX. Anthropic's Q2 2026 revenue is projected at $10.9 billion (up 130% from $4.8 billion in Q1), with its first-ever quarterly operating profit of $559 million projected for Q2. Andrej Karpathy, co-founder of OpenAI and former head of Tesla Autopilot, joined Anthropic's pretraining team under Nick Joseph on May 19. (Unrot Weekly)

Why it matters to Changeis Anthropic's valuation surpassing OpenAI's — while simultaneously posting its first operating profit and signing a SpaceX compute deal — signals that the second-mover AI lab strategy (safety-first, enterprise-focused) is now validated by capital markets. For federal BD: Anthropic's improving financial position increases the likelihood of long-term contract stability; the SpaceX infrastructure dependency remains a procurement risk flag for classified engagements.

Policy Lead — White House / Executive Order Aftermath

"Who Killed the AI Order?" — Musk Denies, Trump Says He "Hated Regulation"; Collapse Leaves No Federal AI Governance Plan in Place

Sources: The Jakarta Post · The Guardian · May 23–24, 2026

Friday brought a fierce blame-assignment battle over the last-minute collapse of Trump's planned AI executive order. The order would have given the federal government up to 90 days of access to the most powerful AI models before public release and would have established a coordinated response to AI-enabled threats to critical infrastructure including banks, hospitals, and power grids. Elon Musk denied on X that he killed the order, writing: "This is false. I still don't know what was in that executive order and the president only spoke to me after declining to sign." However, reports from multiple outlets cited Musk, Zuckerberg, and Sacks as having contacted Trump in the preceding days. The Guardian reported that Microsoft and Google had privately agreed to a voluntary, non-binding arrangement for pre-release model evaluations — agreements that may now be moot without the executive framework to enforce them. The collapse leaves the administration with no formal plan for managing security risks from frontier AI systems and no timeline for producing one.

Why it matters to Changeis Federal agencies that built AI acquisition and compliance planning around the anticipated executive framework must now revert entirely to agency-specific AI use policies and the NIST AI RMF. The 90-day pre-release review mechanism — had it passed — would have created formal federal agency access to model evaluations, potentially generating a procurement pathway for AI governance services. Changeis BD teams should monitor for agency-level policy issuances that attempt to fill the void, particularly from CISA, NSA, and NIST in the coming weeks.
  • California / AI Workforce

    California Governor Newsom Signs AI Workforce Executive Order N-6-26 — 90-Day Labor Impact Review, WARN Act Overhaul, EDD AI Dashboard

    California Governor Gavin Newsom signed Executive Order N-6-26 on May 21, directing state agencies to study and respond to AI's workforce impacts. Key provisions: the Labor and Workforce Development Agency must deliver a review of AI labor market impacts within 90 days; EDD must launch a public dashboard tracking AI's employment effects using UI data within 90 days; the state WARN Act must be reviewed and updated within 180 days to address AI-driven workforce disruptions; and recommendations for AI-labor collective bargaining practices are due by October 15, 2026. California has existing AI MOUs with NVIDIA, Adobe, Google, IBM, and Microsoft through CSU and California Community Colleges. (California Governor's Office)

    California's EO N-6-26 is a preview of likely federal legislative language on AI and labor. The WARN Act amendment directive is particularly significant — if adopted federally, it would impose disclosure obligations on organizations using AI to drive workforce reductions. Changeis clients in regulated industries should begin tracking state WARN Act reform proposals as early indicators of federal compliance requirements.
  • EU AI Act

    EU AI Act Strict Enforcement Begins — High-Risk System Audits Now Mandatory, Fines Up to 7% of Global Revenue

    The European Union began strict enforcement of the AI Act, with mandatory audits for high-risk AI systems and fines up to 7% of global annual revenue for non-compliance. Any company serving EU users is affected regardless of headquarters location. The enforcement scope triggers particularly for AI systems in critical infrastructure, employment screening, financial services, law enforcement, and border management. (Tech Bytes)

    US-headquartered firms operating in EU markets — including many federal contractors with NATO and allied-nation programs — face immediate compliance obligations under the AI Act's high-risk classification. Changeis clients with EU program presence should audit current AI deployments against the high-risk category definitions.

    The top Democrat on the House Intelligence Committee stated publicly that "it would be insane" for U.S. spy agencies not to have early access to frontier AI models, and argued that the Commerce Department should have a role in AI policy — framing the killed executive order's pre-release review provision as a national security necessity rather than a regulatory burden. The statement directly contradicts the tech industry's argument that pre-release reviews would harm competitiveness. (Defense One)

Research Notable
  • Open Source / Architecture

    SubQ Launches First Commercial Subquadratic LLM — 12M-Token Context on $29M Seed; ZAYA1-8B Ships on AMD Hardware

    Subquadratic Inc. launched SubQ, the first commercial LLM using a subquadratic sparse attention architecture, with a 12 million-token context window and $29 million in seed funding. The architecture scales compute linearly rather than quadratically with context length, enabling dramatically lower inference costs at long context. Separately, Zyphra dropped ZAYA1-8B under Apache 2.0, trained from scratch entirely on AMD Instinct hardware — the first frontier model trained without Nvidia GPUs. (Intellectia · Agentic AI Hype)

    Subquadratic architectures are potentially transformative for government use cases involving very long documents — contracts, legislation, intelligence reports. If SubQ's cost-performance curve holds at scale, it could enable full-document AI analysis workflows that are currently cost-prohibitive on transformer models.
