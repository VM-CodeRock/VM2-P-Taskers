Legion LegalTech · DC Court · EAR Authority
Legion LegalTech v. Commerce Remains Active Post-Restoration; Mayer Brown Confirms Novel EAR Jurisdiction; IEEPA Informational-Materials Exclusion Challenge Continues; Framework Durability Unresolved

Legion LegalTech, Corp. v. United States (1:26-cv-02225, D.D.C., filed June 23, 2026) remains active as of July 1. The Fable 5 restoration resolves Legion's immediate access injury — the company is an Anthropic customer with employees in Canada who lost access June 12 — but the court filing's broader statutory challenges continue. The Clearinghouse case profile confirms Legion seeks: (1) a declaratory judgment that the June 12 BIS directive is unlawful and void; (2) vacatur of the directive; (3) preliminary and permanent injunctive relief; and (4) a stay. The core legal question — whether Commerce's BIS has authority under ECRA Section 4817(b)(1) and EAR Section 744.22(b) to treat access to a hosted AI inference API as an "export, reexport, or in-country transfer" — is now before the court regardless of the restoration. Mayer Brown's June 30 legal analysis described this as "novel" — no prior case has tested whether a software-as-a-service AI API access grant constitutes a "deemed export" or an item "subject to the EAR." (Clearinghouse, Jul 1; Mayer Brown, Jun 30)

The IEEPA challenge adds a second statutory argument: to the extent BIS's authority rests on IEEPA rather than ECRA/EAR, the June 12 directive violates IEEPA's Section 1702(b)(3) exclusion of informational materials from presidential authority, was issued without the national emergency declaration required by Section 1701(a), and claims "vast economic and political significance" that IEEPA does not clearly confer. The APA arbitrary-and-capricious argument points to OpenAI's GPT-5.5 having an identical code-review-and-vulnerability-identification capability that was not restricted, and the government's failure to consider less-restrictive alternatives. A court ruling on the merits will determine whether the managed-release regime — now extended to GPT-5.6 Sol under the voluntary White House EO framework — has durable legal authority or remains vulnerable to challenge by any commercial entity that loses API access.

What This Means For Changeis

(1) The Fable 5 restoration does not moot the case; track the docket for mootness briefing and any government motion to dismiss, which would force the court to rule on whether the legal challenge survives; (2) If the court rules on the merits and upholds EAR authority over AI API access, the managed-release framework becomes durable law — the "cleared partner" model for Mythos 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol becomes permanent and Changeis's Annex A relationships have long-term value; (3) If the court vacates or narrows BIS's authority, future frontier model restrictions will require different legal hooks — prepare messaging for clients on both scenarios; (4) Include a one-sentence Legion LegalTech status note in any federal proposal that references model access as a delivery dependency, noting that the legal framework governing access is subject to pending court review.
