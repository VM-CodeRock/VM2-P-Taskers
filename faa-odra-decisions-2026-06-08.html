Case Detail — Protest of Cavan Solutions (2026 WL 1284037)

Background & Parties

Cavan Solutions and Advanced Sciences & Technology, LLC (AS&T) both held IDIQ contracts under the FAA's Systems Engineering and Technical Innovative Solutions (SETIS) program. The FAA product team awarded a task order valued at approximately $23.8 million (operational traffic management support for FAA System Operations / ATO) to AS&T. Cavan filed an initial and supplemental protest alleging organizational conflicts of interest (OCI) tied to former FAA employees who had joined AS&T.

Outcome — OCI / Corrective Action

Following investigation, the contracting officer found "ample evidence" of an "unmitigable, unavoidable OCI." The FAA excluded AS&T, terminated its task order, and awarded to Cavan — providing complete relief on the protest's core claims. The protest was rendered largely moot. Cavan's request for enhanced remedies (IG referral, suspension/debarment of AS&T) was denied; the ODRA credited the product team's good faith and voluntary corrective action.

Landmark Holding: No-Protest Clause Unenforceable

The ODRA's most consequential ruling struck down SETIS contract clause G.8.3, which purported to make task order awards "final and conclusive" and non-protestable below $25M. The ODRA found the clause void on three independent grounds:

  • 49 U.S.C. § 40110(d)(4) — FAA's organic bid protest statute mandates ODRA adjudication; a contractual clause cannot override it.
  • FASA inapplicable to FAA — The task order protest bars at 10 U.S.C. § 3406(f) and 41 U.S.C. § 4106(f) do not apply; Congress expressly exempted FAA via 49 U.S.C. § 40110(d)(2).
  • 5 U.S.C. § 575(a)(3) — A "final and conclusive" clause functions as an arbitration agreement, barred by federal statute.

The ODRA also criticized a previously non-public 2016 administrator memorandum (approved by then-Admin. Michael Huerta) that had endorsed the no-protest clause, finding it misled the administrator by omitting key statutory context. The ODRA concluded: "The Government of the United States does not work in this manner."

Transparency Ruling

The ODRA rejected the FAA product team's request to suppress the public version of the order, citing recent DOT guidance encouraging publication of formal adjudication decisions. Given FAA's insulation from GAO protest review, the ODRA stressed that transparency is essential to demonstrate proceedings are "free from abuse of discretion."

Changeis Relevance Analysis
ATEPS-II / Changeis Flags — Review Required

Changeis holds a $114.7M ATEPS-II contract (Award ID: 693KA925D00004, awarded December 2024, running through December 1, 2034). The Cavan Solutions ruling's no-protest logic applies beyond SETIS to all FAA IDIQs. Changeis counsel and contracts team should confirm whether ATEPS-II contains any analogous "final and conclusive" or no-protest clause in task order provisions.

Flag A: ATEPS-II No-Protest Clause Risk

Status: ACTION REQUIRED — Review Contract Language
The Cavan Solutions holding voids no-protest clauses across FAA IDIQ vehicles. ATEPS-II task orders issued under Award 693KA925D00004 may contain SETIS-style "final and conclusive" language in task order solicitations. If such language exists, it is now unenforceable under the ODRA's three-prong analysis. This creates both risk (any competitor can protest ATEPS-II task order awards to Changeis regardless of any no-protest clause) and opportunity (Changeis can protest task order awards it believes were improperly evaluated). Changeis contracts/legal team should audit ATEPS-II task order clauses immediately.

Flag B: OCI Vigilance — Former Government Employee Hiring

Status: MONITOR — Post-Award OCI Risk Elevated
The AS&T disqualification in Cavan Solutions stemmed entirely from former FAA employees joining AS&T and being involved in evaluating/performing a related task order. The ODRA found this an "unmitigable, unavoidable OCI." Changeis, which provides business management support to FAA's ATO PMO directorates (AJM-1 through AJM-4), should ensure that any former FAA officials hired into its workforce are properly OCI-screened against active ATEPS-II task orders and any open task order competitions. The contracting officer cooperating voluntarily in Cavan Solutions was a mitigating factor — Changeis should maintain proactive OCI mitigation documentation.

A review of the FAA ODRA public case register and available legal databases identifies no ODRA protest or contract dispute filed by or against Changeis Inc. in the June 1–8, 2026 window or currently pending. Flag D: SETIS / NextGen / AIT / TIM Contract Landscape

Status: MONITOR — SETIS OCI Precedent Relevant
Cavan Solutions and AS&T both operate in FAA NAS modernization / NextGen space. The SETIS program overlaps with FAA ATO PMO work covered by ATEPS-II. As Changeis provides program management support to FAA's ATO PMO (AJM directorates covering Integrated Services, Air Traffic Systems, Enterprise Services, Surveillance Services), task order competitions under ATEPS-II may attract protests from competitors citing OCI or evaluation deficiencies, now that the no-protest clause barrier is eliminated. Changeis should track SETIS competitive activity as a bellwether for ATEPS-II protest risk.

Trends & Observations
  • No-protest clauses are now a litigation target. The Cavan Solutions ruling signals that any FAA contractor who previously declined to protest a task order award due to a no-protest clause now has a viable path to ODRA. Expect a surge in post-award protests under SETIS, ATEPS-II, PSS, and other large FAA IDIQ vehicles in the 12–18 months following this ruling. See National Law Review (May 15, 2026).
  • FASA inapplicability creates unique FAA protest landscape. Unlike DOD and civilian agencies, FAA cannot limit ODRA jurisdiction via FASA-derived task order protest bars. This makes FAA procurement more contestable than other federal agencies at the task order level.
  • Transparency push increases case visibility. The ODRA's insistence on publishing decisions (even over agency objection) means future ODRA precedents will be more accessible and cited more frequently, increasing the importance of monitoring this docket.
  • OCI remains the primary protest vector. Both the Cavan Solutions case and ODRA-25-965 (Science Applications International, March 2025) involved ATEPS-related competitive actions. Competitor OCI challenges are the highest-probability protest risk for Changeis given its deep FAA PMO relationships.
  • ODRA publication lag is significant. The formal public docket (faa.gov/RecentCases) shows only cases through ODRA-25-965 (March 2025). The Cavan Solutions case was issued in early 2026 but reached the legal press only in May 2026. Changeis should monitor legal publications (JD Supra, National Law Review, SmallGovCon) as a leading indicator ahead of the official docket.
