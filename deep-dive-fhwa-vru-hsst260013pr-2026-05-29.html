6. FHWA Office of Safety Background

VRU Safety Program Overview

The FHWA Office of Safety (HSST) has elevated Vulnerable Road User safety as a top national priority under the Safe System Approach. In 2024, there were 7,080 pedestrian fatalities (down 3.9% from 2023) and 1,103 bicyclist fatalities (down 6% from 2023), yet injuries increased for both groups — together accounting for over 21% of all U.S. traffic fatalities. The persistence of VRU fatalities on arterial roads designed for motor vehicle throughput has driven a structural shift in FHWA policy toward the Safe System Approach, which accepts human fallibility and designs roads to prevent fatal crashes.

BIL / IIJA Statutory Foundation

  • 23 U.S.C. 148(l) — VRU Safety Assessments: The Bipartisan Infrastructure Law (BIL/IIJA, 2021) required every state to complete an initial VRU Safety Assessment by November 15, 2023 as part of the Highway Safety Improvement Program (HSIP). All 50 states completed theirs. These assessments identify high-risk areas, quantify VRU fatalities and serious injuries, and develop programs of projects to address identified risks.
  • VRU Special Rule (23 U.S.C. 148(g)(3)): If VRU fatalities represent 15% or more of a state's total crash fatalities, that state must obligate at least 15% of its HSIP apportionment for VRU highway safety improvement projects the following fiscal year.
  • Safe Streets and Roads for All (SS4A): $5B over 5 years (FY22–FY26) for competitive grants to local and tribal governments to prevent roadway fatalities. As of December 2025, $3.9B awarded to 2,000+ communities. Reauthorization proposed at $5B FY27–FY31.
  • 23 U.S.C. 503, 109, 217: FHWA statutory authorities cited in the SOW for deploying safety technologies, ensuring countermeasures in Federal-aid projects, and integrating bicycle/pedestrian facilities.

FHWA Safety IDIQ Structure (2026)

In April 2026, FHWA awarded a new $48M multiple-award Safety IDIQ (RFP 693JJ325R000004) to 7 holders: Guidehouse, ICF, Kittelson, Macrosys, VHB, WERIS, and WSP. This IDIQ is the vehicle for HSST260013PR. All 7 IDIQ holders are eligible to compete for this task order, creating robust technical competition.

ICF's Prior FHWA Safety Work

ICF has held FHWA transportation IDIQs since at least 2016. In 2017, FHWA awarded ICF nearly $6M in task orders under a five-year transportation solutions IDIQ for technical support, outreach, and program services for the Office of Operations. ICF's federal transportation practice also includes the DOT Transportation Planning IDIQ (693JJ321D000017) and a recent $23M DOT Discretionary Grants Lifecycle Management System contract (December 2025).
