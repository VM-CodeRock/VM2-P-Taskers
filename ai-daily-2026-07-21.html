Anthropic / Claude
Reversal
Fable 5 Metered Billing Lasted Less Than 24 Hours; Anthropic Restores Permanent Access July 21

After Claude Fable 5's free-access promotion expired July 19 at 11:59:59 PM PT and metered, credits-based billing took effect July 20 at 11:59 PM PT, Anthropic reversed course within a day: as of July 21, Fable 5 is now a permanent (if capped) feature of Max ($100/month) and Team Premium plans, limited to 50% of each plan's weekly usage allowance, while Pro subscribers keep access via prepaid usage credits sweetened by a one-time $100 grant. The reversal caps an extension cycle that ran June 22 → July 7 → July 12 → July 19 → credits July 20 → permanent inclusion July 21, and Anthropic also extended Claude Code's 50% weekly rate-limit boost through August 19 as part of the same announcement, alongside confirmation that Sonnet 5's introductory pricing ($2/$10 per million tokens) steps up to $3/$15 per million on August 31 (AI Tools Recap).

Court Ruling
Federal Judge Grants Final Approval to Anthropic's $1.5B Copyright Settlement

Judge Araceli Martinez-Olguin of the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California signed off July 20 on final approval of Anthropic's $1.5 billion class-action settlement with authors and publishers who alleged the company illegally downloaded and stored millions of pirated books to train Claude, rejecting objections that the payout was too small. The settlement — believed to be the largest in U.S. copyright law history — will pay approximately $3,000 per work across an estimated 500,000 works, with initial payments to class members expected around August 10. Because Anthropic settled rather than appealed, the underlying legal question of whether AI training on pirated books constitutes fair use will not become binding appellate precedent (TechCrunch, The Hindu).

Compute Deal
Meta-Anthropic $10B Compute Lease Talks Remain Early-Stage as of July 20–21

Continued coverage of the Meta-Anthropic compute lease discussions first reported by the New York Times on July 17 shows no material progress in the 48-hour window: TradingView and other outlets characterize the talks as still preliminary, with neither company confirming terms, and analysts continue to frame the potential $10 billion, two-year arrangement as part of Meta's broader pivot toward a "Meta Compute" cloud-rental business rather than a signed deal (TradingView).

OpenAI
Litigation
Apple v. OpenAI Trade-Secret Suit Remains Active Two Weeks In; No New Escalation Reported in 48h Window

Apple's July 10 trade-secret lawsuit against OpenAI — alleging a scheme "at every level" of the company to misappropriate Apple hardware and design secrets via former employees Tang Tan and Chang Liu — continues to anchor the OpenAI litigation narrative, with legal-hold letters previously sent to roughly 40 former Apple employees now at OpenAI remaining the most recent escalation as of this window. OpenAI has said it is "not aware of any evidence" the claims have merit; the case continues to be cited by market commentators as a headwind on OpenAI's IPO timeline expectations (CNBC, TechCrunch).

Model Lifecycle
GPT-5.4 Retirement Set for July 23 as GPT-5.6 Sol/Terra/Luna Rollout Continues

OpenAI's model release notes confirm GPT-5.4 retires July 23, 2026, two days inside this brief's forward-looking horizon, continuing the company's post-GPT-5.6 model-lifecycle cleanup. GPT-5.6 Sol, Terra, and Luna remain generally available following their July 9 public rollout, with Sol continuing to serve as the flagship reasoning/coding tier at $5/$30 per million input/output tokens and GPT-5.6 now the default engine across Microsoft 365 Copilot surfaces (AI Business Weekly).

Perplexity
Federal Offer
Perplexity Enterprise Pro for Government Remains Open at $0.25/Agency; No New Announcements in 48h Window

Perplexity's federal offering — automatic zero-data-usage protection for all identified U.S. government requests plus Perplexity Enterprise Pro for Government at a nominal $0.25 per agency for the first 15 months under GSA's OneGov Multiple Award Schedule — remains unchanged and open for adoption; no new Perplexity product announcements were logged in the July 19–21 window (Perplexity, GSA). Separately, Perplexity's standard Enterprise Pro tier continues to be listed at $40/seat/month ($400/seat/year), with volume discounts available for 250+ seats, education, nonprofit, and government customers (Coworker AI).

Google DeepMind / xAI / Meta

Gemini 3.5 Pro Remains Unshipped; Stopgap Gemini 3.6 Flash Reportedly Under Consideration

Google's Gemini 3.5 Pro has now missed at least three reported target dates (June, then July 17), and as of July 21 the model page still lists it as "coming soon" with no model card, API pricing, or benchmark disclosure. Build Fast with AI's July 20 roundup reports Google is exploring a stopgap Gemini 3.6 Flash release to put a product in market while engineering continues on the delayed Pro build, following earlier reporting that four senior Gemini researchers left for Anthropic amid the slip (Build Fast with AI).

China / Kimi K3 Aftermath
Capacity Constraint
Moonshot Pauses New Kimi Subscriptions as K3 Demand Saturates GPU Capacity

Moonshot AI temporarily stopped accepting new paid Kimi subscriptions on July 20 after demand for Kimi K3 pushed available GPU capacity to its limit over a 48-hour period, per reporting from The Hans India; existing subscribers retain uninterrupted access, and new sign-ups are expected to reopen gradually as additional capacity comes online. Moonshot simultaneously split its paid offering into two specialized plans — a standard Kimi Membership for web, app, and workplace use, and a separate Kimi Code Membership for programming-focused workflows — a structural change intended to allocate compute more predictably. The episode is an early operational signal for enterprises evaluating K3: assess rate limits, subscription availability, and fallback-model routing before committing production workloads (WindowsForum).

Open-Weight Countdown
Kimi K3 Open-Weights Deadline Holds at July 27; Still "Open in Intent, Closed in Practice"

Moonshot's commitment to release K3's full 2.8-trillion-parameter weights under a Modified MIT license by July 27 remains unchanged as of July 21, with no public checkpoint, repository, or license file yet available; the model remains accessible only via Kimi.com, Kimi Work, Kimi Code, and the API at $3/M input (cache miss), $0.30/M input (cache hit), and $15/M output tokens. Commentary continues to flag possible Congressional scrutiny of Chinese-model distillation claims, with Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's distillation allegations against K3 (reported July 19 by Sina) still circulating alongside China's rebuttal as the open-weight release approaches (Digital Applied, Economic Times/Bloomberg).

White House
Gold Eagle Clearinghouse Draws "Muted" Industry Support One Week After Launch

Bloomberg Law's July 17 reporting that industry response to the White House's "Gold Eagle" AI-cybersecurity clearinghouse has been muted continues to frame the program's status as of this window: governance mechanics, participation criteria beyond Anthropic's Mythos-derived findings, and a formal operational timeline remain undefined even as the White House maintains the program is "already receiving vulnerability reports, checking scans, and sending repair guidance." The clearinghouse, run through Treasury with CISA and DHS involvement and built on the VINCE platform in partnership with Carnegie Mellon's Software Engineering Institute, continues to represent an open thread on how much control it will assert over lab partner-approval decisions, including Anthropic's Project Glasswing and OpenAI's Daybreak rosters (Bloomberg Law, Cybersecurity Dive).
