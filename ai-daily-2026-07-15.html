The Model Context Protocol's largest-ever specification revision entered its release candidate phase, moving to a stateless core and hardened OAuth/OIDC authorization ahead of a July 28 final release, alongside NSA guidance formally establishing MCP authorization expectations for federal deployments (MCP Blog, DeepInspect).

Anthropic / Claude
Federal Signal
CISA's Attack Surface Evaluation Team Confirmed Using Claude Mythos on Government Code

Reuters reported — and Forbes independently corroborated via a CISA employee — that members of CISA's Attack Surface Evaluation team have been using Anthropic's Claude Mythos model to scan government code repositories hosted on GitHub, uncovering a "significant number" of vulnerabilities that could otherwise be exploited by foreign intelligence services or cybercriminals. Notably, CISA was not among the roughly 50 organizations in Anthropic's initial Mythos access group, and it remains unclear how the agency obtained authorization; neither Anthropic nor CISA has responded to requests for clarification. Separately, the NSA has reportedly been using Mythos since at least April, with six Anthropic engineers previously embedded at the agency to customize the model for offensive cyber operations against foreign adversaries (Reuters, Forbes).

Product Launch
Anthropic Launches Free "Claude for Teachers" for US K-12 Educators

Anthropic introduced Claude for Teachers on July 14, giving verified US K-12 educators a full year of free premium Claude access — including Claude Cowork and Claude Code — plus an open-source library of teaching skills mapped to academic standards in all 50 states, developed with Learning Commons and piloted with teachers including those at Brooklyn's Prospect Schools. The program integrates with nine K-12 tools including ASSISTments, Canva Education, and MagicSchool, uses a FERPA-aligned Data Processing Addendum, and will pilot in Detroit Public Schools; registration is open through June 30, 2027, with school/district-level offerings planned next (The Verge). Edtech company Stride's stock fell as much as 7.7% intraday, closing down roughly 5.6%, on concerns the free offering undercuts its differentiated-instruction business (FuTu News).

International Expansion
Anthropic Commits CA$10M to Canadian AI Research, Extends Startup Credits

Anthropic announced on July 14 it will direct CA$10 million to eight Canadian institutions — including Amii, Mila, the Vector Institute, CHEO, CAMH, and the University of Toronto's Data Sciences Institute — for responsible-AI, safety, and health research, without disclosing the split across categories. Amii, Mila, and Vector will also join the Anthropic for Startups program this summer, extending at least $5,000 USD in API credits to hundreds of affiliated Canadian startups. Anthropic separately disclosed, using only its own internal usage data, that Canada ranks eighth globally in Claude.ai usage and exceeds the company's predicted per-capita adoption rate by more than four times (AI Insiders).

Cross-Border Scrutiny
China's MIIT Orders Uninstall of Claude Code Over Tracking Mechanism; Canada's OSFI Flags Cyber Risk

China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology issued a binding national directive to uninstall Claude Code versions 2.1.91 through 2.1.196, citing a built-in "prompt steganography" mechanism — since quietly removed by Anthropic — that reportedly transmitted user location and identity data to remote servers without consent; Alibaba has internally banned staff from using the tool (Cryptopolitan). Separately, Canada's Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions warned banks and insurers that advanced models like Claude Mythos could shorten the window available to detect, contain, and remediate cyberattacks, urging faster vulnerability-response practices — a warning that echoes similar cautions previously issued by Japan and the UK (CNBC Indonesia / Reuters).

Talent War
Andrej Karpathy, Tom Blomfield Reportedly Join Anthropic

Anthropic has reportedly added Andrej Karpathy, the influential former Tesla AI director and OpenAI founding member, along with Monzo co-founder and former CEO Tom Blomfield, who is joining Anthropic's AI compute team. The hires extend an aggressive 2026 recruiting run that previously brought Nobel laureate John Jumper over from Google DeepMind, reinforcing Anthropic's position as a top destination for marquee AI talent amid an intensifying industry-wide hiring race (Build Fast with AI).

OpenAI
Litigation
OpenAI Formally Rebuts Apple Trade-Secret Lawsuit

OpenAI issued its first substantive comment on the merits of Apple's trade-secret suit on July 14, stating: "While we take these allegations seriously, we're not aware of any evidence that this complaint has merit," and adding, "We believe in fair competition and allowing people the freedom to work wherever they choose." The statement, first shared by Bloomberg's Ed Ludlow, follows Apple's July 11 complaint alleging that OpenAI employees — including Chief Hardware Officer Tang Tan, a 24-year Apple veteran — engaged in a coordinated effort to obtain confidential Apple IP for OpenAI's hardware ambitions. Bloomberg separately reported OpenAI is developing a screen-free, mobile "humanlike AI companion" smart speaker with input from several former Apple iPhone and Mac engineers (TechCrunch).

Litigation
NYT and Publishers Seek Sanctions Against OpenAI in Copyright Suit

The New York Times and a group of publishers filed a motion seeking sanctions against OpenAI, alleging the company withheld training-data evidence in the ongoing copyright litigation over whether OpenAI illegally used their journalism to train its models — adding a new procedural front to one of the industry's highest-profile IP disputes (Build Fast with AI).

Safety Grade
Future of Life Institute's 2026 AI Safety Index: Best Grade Is a C+

The Future of Life Institute released its 2026 AI Safety Index grading nine frontier labs, with Anthropic earning the field's top mark at C+, OpenAI and Google DeepMind both at C, Meta at D+, and xAI, DeepSeek, and Mistral receiving effectively failing grades. Reviewers specifically flagged labs for "moving the goalposts" on prior safety-pause commitments even as capabilities and regulatory scrutiny both intensify — a sobering counterpoint to the frontier labs' rapid model-release cadence this month (Build Fast with AI).

Perplexity
Model Launch
Perplexity's Sonar Frontier Claims Near-Opus Quality at a Third of the Cost

Perplexity released Sonar Frontier, a model post-trained from Zhipu AI's open-weight GLM 5.2, claiming 96% of Claude Opus 4.8's complex-reasoning performance and 92% of its code-generation quality on internal evaluations, priced at $4.5/$18 per million input/output tokens versus Opus 4.8's $15/$60 — a partial reversal of Perplexity's earlier bet on training proprietary Sonar models from scratch. CEO Aravind Srinivas framed the move bluntly: "peak quality belongs to Anthropic and OpenAI. Our fight is search and answer quality." Sonar Frontier is now included in the $20/month Perplexity Pro tier at no added cost (AI Navigate).

Litigation
Perplexity Founders Must Turn Over Personal Emails in Publisher IP Suit

A federal judge ordered Perplexity to search founders Johnny Ho and Denis Yarats' personal Gmail accounts for records relevant to News Corp's Dow Jones and New York Post copyright suit against the company, after evidence showed both founders used personal email for company business. The order adds discovery pressure to Perplexity's ongoing content-licensing disputes with major publishers (Bloomberg Law).

Product Update
Comet Browser Continues Iterating Post Worldwide Launch

Following last week's global free release, Comet for iOS shipped version 26.27.0 with new tab-management controls (auto-close inactive tabs after a day/week/month) and address-bar polish, continuing Perplexity's recent pattern of incremental browser refinements — reader mode, customizable ad blocking, and tab muting — layered on top of the broader Comet Plus publisher program and Background Assistants rollout (PiunikaWeb).

Google DeepMind / xAI / Meta
Model Launch
Gemini 3.5 Pro's July 17 Target Remains Unconfirmed by Google

As of July 13, no model card, pricing page, or public API listing for Gemini 3.5 Pro exists — Google's own model page still describes it as "coming soon," and the current public API lists only gemini-3.5-flash and gemini-3.1-pro-preview. Third-party reporting continues to circulate a July 17 general-availability target with a 2-million-token context window, a Deep Think reasoning layer gated behind the $250/month Ultra tier, and roughly $1.25/$10 per-million-token API pricing, but every specific figure traces back to unnamed sources rather than an official Google announcement. The model has already slipped five-plus weeks past its original Google I/O-era commitment (Tech Times).

Coding / Benchmark
Grok 4.5 Claims Long-Horizon Terminal-Bench Lead; Musk Directs Tesla Staff to Adopt It

Elon Musk said on X that Grok 4.5 claimed the top spot on the Long-Horizon Terminal-Bench, a benchmark for complex, extended agentic tasks, while separately directing Tesla staff in a memo to switch to Grok "when possible" given its lower token costs — even as Musk concedes internally that Grok is not yet the strongest model available. The directive follows Tesla capping employee spending on third-party AI tools (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google) at $200/week while pointedly exempting xAI's Grok. Independent multi-domain benchmarks still place Grok 4.5 behind several OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google models overall, with its coding score the lowest on one widely cited leaderboard (Electrek, Basenor).

Infrastructure
Meta's Louisiana Data Center Investment Hits $50 Billion; Google Caps Meta's Gemini Access

Meta's data center buildout in Louisiana has reached a cumulative $50 billion investment amid the company's broader AI infrastructure push (CNBC). In a sign of strained AI-compute supply chains, Google has reportedly capped Meta's Gemini API usage because it cannot meet Meta's growing demand for cloud computing capacity — notable given Meta has grown increasingly reliant on Gemini for internal workloads even as it builds out its own Muse Spark model family and custom "Iris" AI chip (The Verge).

Federal AI / Policy
FedRAMP
FedRAMP 20x Continuous-Authorization Model Gains Momentum Across Vendors

GSA's modernized FedRAMP 20x pathway — which replaces annual point-in-time audits with continuous, machine-readable validation against Key Security Indicators — continues to draw new tooling and authorizations. C1Secure launched SmartRAMP 20x on the ServiceNow platform to help cloud providers reach the FedRAMP Marketplace faster and cheaper, while Anthropic's Claude Code and Claude Cowork entered public beta inside a FedRAMP High-authorized "Claude for Government Desktop" spanning all three federal branches (Yahoo Finance / PR Newswire, Enterprise DNA). Separately, the FCC reactivated a previously cancelled $1 pilot contract for "Anthropic Claude for Government Federal," an 85-day FedRAMP High-compliant subscription that had been cancelled in March under then-administrator Brendan Carr (All-Source Intelligence).

Executive Order
EO 14409 Deadlines Advance: Frontier-Model Benchmarking Framework Due August 1

Implementation of Executive Order 14409, "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security," continues on schedule: the classified benchmarking process to define a "covered frontier model" based on cyber capabilities, plus a voluntary framework for AI companies to disclose models pre-release, are both due August 1, 2026. The order already triggered a federal AI cybersecurity clearinghouse (NSA/CISA/Treasury) and Binding Operational Directives for civilian systems by its July 2 deadline (Congressional Research Service).

Enforcement
Agentic AI Vulnerability Wave Prompts CISA, ECB, ESRB Action

More than twenty agentic AI vulnerabilities landed across MCP servers, coding assistants, and memory/gateway frameworks this past week, several rated CVSS 9.8-10.0. CISA's actively-exploited Langflow flaw (CVE-2026-55255, CVSS 9.9) remains in the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog after Sysdig confirmed in-the-wild credential harvesting since June 25; separately, the ECB ordered eurozone banks to file AI-enabled cyber action plans by October 31, and the ESRB upgraded systemic AI cyber risk to "severe." Illinois became the third and strictest US state to mandate independent frontier-AI safety audits via SB 315, notably with both OpenAI and Anthropic publicly endorsing the bill (LinkedIn / Gerry Chng security brief).

Cybersecurity
White House Launches "Gold Eagle" Vulnerability-Sharing Center

The White House launched a "Gold Eagle" cybersecurity center intended to securely share software-flaw and vulnerability information before AI model releases, enabling developers to fix issues faster; some critics warn the initiative could evolve into de facto government-led AI regulation, drawing comparisons to the temporary export-control episode that suspended Anthropic's Claude Mythos earlier this year (Chosun Ilbo).

Agent Tooling & MCP
Protocol Update
MCP's Largest-Ever Spec Revision Enters Release Candidate; Final Ships July 28

The Model Context Protocol project published its 2026-07-28 release candidate — the largest revision since launch — introducing a stateless core that removes the initialize handshake and session IDs, new extensions (server-rendered UIs via MCP Apps, long-running work via Tasks), and six authorization SEPs that align MCP more closely with OAuth 2.0 and OpenID Connect, including mandatory issuer validation per RFC 9207 to close a mix-up-attack class relevant to MCP's single-client, many-server pattern. Roots, Sampling, and Logging are formally deprecated with a 12-month minimum runway; the ten-week validation window runs through the final release on July 28, 2026 (Model Context Protocol Blog).

Federal Guidance
NSA Issues Joint Guidance Setting Federal MCP Authorization Expectations

A joint NSA cybersecurity information sheet on Model Context Protocol security design now sets explicit expectations for federal deployments: authenticate callers to MCP servers, authorize individual tool invocations rather than whole connections, and treat protocol transports as security-relevant configuration. Trackers note this moves MCP authorization "from a good idea to a documented federal expectation," with enterprise adoption typically following federal AI guidance on a roughly two-quarter lag (DeepInspect).

Enterprise
Citrix, Varonis Extend Governance and Security Coverage to Agent Traffic

Citrix added MCP Gateway functionality to NetScaler, letting enterprises centrally authenticate, rate-limit, and allow/block-list agent traffic to backend MCP servers (Citrix). Varonis separately extended its Atlas AI security coverage to Claude Code and Claude Cowork, building on its existing Claude Compliance API integration — part of a broader enterprise trend toward treating agentic AI traffic as a distinct, auditable security surface rather than ordinary application traffic (Business Insider).
