Anthropic Raises $65B at a $965B Valuation — and Ships Claude Opus 4.8 the Same Day

Source: Anthropic (May 28, 2026) · ABC7 Bay Area

Anthropic closed a $65 billion Series H round on May 28, vaulting to a $965 billion post-money valuation — now ahead of OpenAI's $852B March 2026 mark. The round was led by Altimeter Capital, Dragoneer, Greenoaks, and Sequoia Capital, with co-leads including Capital Group, Coatue, D1 Capital Partners, GIC, ICONIQ, and XN. Blackstone, Brookfield, Fidelity, General Catalyst, Temasek, and sovereign wealth fund MGX also participated. The raise includes $15B of previously committed hyperscaler investments, including $5B from Amazon.

Anthropic disclosed $47B in annualized revenue — a figure that stunned the market. The company simultaneously shipped Claude Opus 4.8, its most capable generally available model, with no price increase from Opus 4.7 ($5/M input, $25/M output). Key Opus 4.8 additions include a new "Fast mode" research preview, mid-conversation system messages, and a lowered prompt-cache minimum. Opus 4.8 is now the default model in Claude Code on Max, Team Premium, and Enterprise plans.

Proceeds will fund safety and interpretability research, expanded compute (Amazon: 5 GW new capacity; Google/Broadcom: 5 GW next-gen TPU; SpaceX GPU access), and global product expansion. Anthropic is opening new offices in Milan and Seoul.

Why It Matters for Federal Contractors Anthropic's trajectory — $47B ARR, near-trillion-dollar valuation — confirms Claude is now a tier-1 enterprise platform alongside Microsoft/OpenAI and Google. As KPMG's 276K-employee global rollout (reported yesterday) illustrates, Big Four and defense primes are standardizing on Claude. Federal contractors positioning AI modernization proposals should expect Claude to appear in agency AI RFPs the same way M365 Copilot does. Anthropic's compute agreements with AWS and Google Cloud also mean Claude is contractually accessible via existing federal cloud vehicles (GovCloud, FedRAMP-authorized deployments).
Other Notable Items
  • Federal Contracting · DoD Technology

    Pentagon Awards Microsoft $9.69B Core Enterprises Technology Agreement BD

    The Department of Defense awarded Microsoft a five-year, $9.69 billion contract — the Core Enterprises Technology Agreement (CETA) — to consolidate M365 subscriptions, cloud computing, software assurance, and on-premises licensing across the Pentagon, intelligence community, and U.S. Coast Guard. The deal eliminates years of "license sprawl" where agencies independently purchased overlapping software. DoD officials framed CETA as part of a broader AI modernization strategy, and the contract is expected to accelerate Microsoft Copilot and Azure AI adoption across defense agencies. A separate Pentagon deal with eight AI firms (Google, Microsoft, OpenAI, Nvidia, and others) was struck May 4, 2026.

    BD angle: CETA establishes Microsoft as the DoD's default cloud/AI delivery layer — contractors building AI solutions for defense agencies should plan integrations against M365 Copilot and Azure AI Foundry as baseline infrastructure. Source: The American Bazaar
  • AI Models · Enterprise Agentic

    Google I/O 2026 Ships Gemini 3.5 Flash, Gemini Omni, and "Spark" Autonomous Agent

    Google completed its I/O 2026 release cascade this week: Gemini 3.5 Flash is now generally available via Google AI Studio, Vertex AI, and the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, delivering "frontier performance for agents and coding." Gemini Omni Flash — capable of generating high-quality video from any input combination (images, audio, text) — is rolling out to all Google AI subscribers. Most significantly, Gemini Spark, a 24/7 autonomous personal AI agent integrated across Gmail, Docs, Calendar, and local desktop files, is entering Beta for Ultra subscribers. Google also expanded SynthID AI-watermarking to 100B+ images/videos and launched a new AI content detection API on Google Cloud for enterprise use.

    The launch of Spark — a cloud-based agent that keeps working after you close your laptop — is the clearest signal yet that ambient agentic AI is moving from concept to product. Source: Google Blog · Google Research
  • Federal AI Policy · White House

    White House AI Executive Order Remains Shelved; Congress and NSA Step into the Vacuum BD

    President Trump abruptly delayed an AI executive order on May 21 — just hours before signing — after David Sacks and Silicon Valley allies warned that even a voluntary pre-release model-testing framework would impede innovation. The order would have allowed the government up to 90 days to review frontier AI models before public release. The White House remains divided: one faction supports a Treasury-led AI cybersecurity clearinghouse as an alternative. Meanwhile, Congress is scheduling hearings on frontier AI cybersecurity threats (anticipated next week), and the NSA has already published MCP security guidance (U/OO/6030316-26). A separate post-quantum cryptography EO is also in preparation for covered federal contractors.

    For govcon: The absence of a federal AI governance framework creates both risk and opportunity — agencies will fill the void with their own AI policies and acquisition language. Expect divergent AI clauses across agency solicitations through at least Q3 2026. JD Supra is tracking an "AI Clauses in Federal Contracts" seminar for June 10. Source: International Business Times · JD Supra
  • AI Security · Enterprise Risk

    54% of Enterprises Have Suffered AI-Related Security Incidents; Only 26% Trust Their Architecture

    Check Point Software's 2026 Cloud Security Report: Securing the AI Transformation — based on 1,042 cybersecurity and IT professionals — found a stark 51-point gap between AI adoption and security readiness. 54% of organizations reported at least one AI-related security incident (shadow AI usage, AI-generated phishing, deepfakes, data leakage); only 26% believe their current architecture can effectively enforce AI governance policies. Sixty-four percent already have AI agents in pilot or production; 12% have granted agents privileged access to core systems. The report recommends five actions: build an AI asset inventory, govern external AI access, embed runtime controls, centralize AI policy accountability, and consolidate toward hybrid mesh security architectures.

    The 51-point adoption/readiness gap is the key number for federal contractors advising agencies on AI risk management. Source: Cybersecurity Insiders
