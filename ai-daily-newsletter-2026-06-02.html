Top Story — Funding / M&A

Anthropic Confidentially Files IPO Prospectus With SEC — $47B Revenue Run Rate, $965B Valuation

On June 1, Anthropic officially confirmed it has submitted a draft Form S-1 to the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, setting the stage for what could become the largest AI company IPO in history. The filing positions Anthropic ahead of OpenAI, which has not yet filed its own confidential prospectus. CNBC reported that Anthropic’s revenue run rate surged to $47 billion annualized as of May 2026 — up from $10 billion in full-year 2025 — driven by enterprise subscriptions to Claude Code and Claude for Government. The company projects $10.9 billion in Q2 2026 revenue, placing it on track for its first quarterly operating profit ever. TechCrunch noted that the filing comes less than a week after Anthropic closed a $65 billion Series H at a $965 billion pre-money valuation — a staggering $47 billion increase from its March 2025 round.

The confidential filing triggers a 15-day clock before Anthropic must deliver its public prospectus to investors ahead of a roadshow. SpaceX, whose IPO process is tracking just weeks ahead, filed confidentially on April 1 and published its public S-1 on May 20. If Anthropic follows a similar timeline, a roadshow and public debut could occur as early as late June or July 2026. The company is not bound by any specific timeline and has explicitly noted that “the timing of the proposed initial public offering will be influenced by market dynamics.” Anthropic’s official announcement under Rule 135 of the Securities Act provides the minimum statutory disclosure.

The IPO follows a quarter of contradictions for Anthropic: it simultaneously lost its $200M Pentagon contract (designated a “supply chain risk”) while raising the largest private AI round in history and growing revenue nearly 5x year-over-year. For public market investors, this dynamic raises the central question the S-1 will need to answer: how dependent is Anthropic’s growth on government and regulated-sector customers, and how does its Pentagon exclusion affect that thesis? The Next Web reported that in Q1 2026 alone, AI startups raised $255.5 billion globally — surpassing all of 2025 — with Anthropic’s $30.6B raise among three deals accounting for 67% of that capital.

For Changeis BD: the S-1 process will produce the most detailed public disclosure of Anthropic’s federal revenue, government customer concentration, and contract risk factors ever released. The prospectus will quantify exactly how much of Anthropic’s growth comes from government-facing contracts (including OneGov and Claude for Government), which directly maps to Changeis’s market sizing for AI advisory services. Changeis should designate a partner to read the S-1 the day it drops and brief leadership within 24 hours. Additionally, the IPO process creates a business development window: agencies currently using Anthropic products under OneGov licenses should be engaged now on transition risk planning, governance posture, and multi-vendor strategy before post-IPO pricing and policy changes take effect.

Model & Product Launches

Microsoft Build 2026: Windows Agent Framework, MAI-Thinking-1, Azure Agent Mesh, and Windows Agent Store Go Live

Microsoft’s Build 2026 keynote (June 2, San Francisco) delivered the most pre-disclosed agenda in Build history — and still produced substantial material. The confirmed announcement stack includes: Windows Agent Framework APIs embedded in the OS shell, enabling any developer to build agents that operate natively within Windows 11; MAI-Thinking-1, Microsoft’s first proprietary reasoning model (not distilled from another AI), aimed at enterprise use cases requiring verifiable chain-of-thought; MAI-Voice-2 and MAI-Image-2.5 multimodal models; WSL 3 with paravirtualized GPU and NPU access for near-native Linux AI performance on Windows; Azure Agent Mesh for federated agent execution across Windows 365 and Azure Arc (GA Q4 2026); and the Windows Agent Store with an 85% developer revenue share, with Adobe and Zoom as launch partners. The NVIDIA RTX Spark superchip (announced at Computex Taipei June 1) — 1 petaflop, 128GB unified memory, ARM-based — will power a new generation of Surface, Dell, HP, Lenovo, and ASUS AI PCs this fall, co-engineered with Microsoft for on-device agent security via the NVIDIA OpenShell runtime.

Source: AI Tools Recap · The Verge · Yahoo Finance / NVIDIA
Federal / Defense AI Contracts

VA Issues Enterprise AI Interface RFI — No Incumbent, $47B Workforce Potential, June 9 Deadline

The Department of Veterans Affairs posted an RFI on May 30 (updated June 2) seeking vendors for a next-generation AI user interface and API layer to serve its entire 95,000-user workforce. FedScoop confirmed there is no incumbent contractor for this effort, making it a clean competitive opportunity. The scope covers the full AI interaction spectrum — assistive, collaborative, and agentic — across compute, foundation models, data integration, orchestration, and user-facing surfaces, with governance embedded at every layer. The VA is actively piloting tools from Anthropic (Claude for Government), Google (Gemini for Government), and OpenAI (ChatGPT Enterprise FedRAMP). VA GPT already has 95,000 sign-ups and 5,500 daily users. The agency is seeking usage-based pricing for commercially available frontier models and may embed AI engineers and data scientists directly within VA operations. Responses due June 9.

Source: FedScoop

Air Force ATA-AI Solicitation Seeks Next-Gen AI Target Tracking; C4I Research BAA Open Through September

The Air Force Research Laboratory issued two AI-focused solicitations this week. The Advanced Tracking Architecture Using AI (ATA-AI) Advanced Research Announcement seeks innovative technologies integrating AI, machine learning, and machine inferencing within high-performance computing environments for next-generation target tracking — response deadline September 2, 2026, for FY27 consideration. Separately, AFRL’s Foundations of Trusted Systems Broad Agency Announcement solicits white papers for trusted hardware and software supporting C4I and cyber information processing systems, with a response window open through September 30, 2026. Both solicitations target early-stage R&D with a path toward program of record integration, making them relevant for Changeis or its teaming partners with SBIR/STTR vehicles or BAA-eligible research capabilities.

Source: ExecutiveGov
Policy / Regulation

EU AI Act Enforcement Body Launched — Scientific Panel and Advisory Forum Appointed; High-Risk Rules Delayed 16 Months

The European Commission has appointed a Scientific Panel and an Advisory Forum to support enforcement of the EU AI Act, with both bodies serving two-year terms and advising the Commission’s AI Office and national authorities. The European Commission confirmed the Scientific Panel will focus on general-purpose AI (GPAI) models, systemic risks, model classification, and evaluation methodologies. Separately, EU legislators reached a provisional agreement on May 7 to delay enforcement of high-risk AI rules by 16 months, pushing the effective date past August 2, 2026 — a reprieve for organizations deploying AI in Annex III high-risk categories. DLA Piper notes the delay is contingent on formal endorsement, and affected organizations should use the extra time to build compliance infrastructure rather than defer it.

Source: European Commission · DLA Piper

Colorado Moves to Repeal AI Antidiscrimination Law; Connecticut Enacts Comprehensive AI Transparency Framework

The state AI regulation landscape shifted significantly in the past 24 hours. Colorado lawmakers are advancing legislation to repeal the state’s first-in-the-nation AI antidiscrimination law — which mandated bias audits and risk impact assessments — and replace it with a streamlined transparency-and-notice framework, reducing compliance burden on employers and AI developers. By contrast, Connecticut enacted one of the most comprehensive AI laws in the country, requiring employers and deployers to notify users of AI use, conduct impact assessments, and provide redress mechanisms for automated decisions. Per Fisher Phillips via JD Supra, the diverging state-level approaches underscore an increasingly fragmented AI compliance landscape that creates advisory demand for multi-state operating federal contractors. The Commerce Department’s expected late June guidance on “onerous” state AI laws may attempt to pre-empt some of these frameworks.

Source: Fisher Phillips / JD Supra
Security & Risk

GlassWorm C2 Dismantled; GREYVIBE APT Uses LLMs Operationally Against Ukraine; AI-Assisted Cryptojacking Campaign Active

Three notable AI-adjacent security developments emerged in the past 24 hours. First, CrowdStrike, Google, and the Shadowserver Foundation simultaneously took down all four command-and-control channels of the GlassWorm operation on May 26 — a Russian-origin malware campaign that used trojanized VS Code extensions published on the Microsoft VS Code Marketplace and Open VSX to compromise developer environments and deliver malicious npm and Python packages. Infected endpoints are now beaconing to a benign sinkhole IP. Organizations should scan for connections to 164.92.88[.]210. Second, security firm WithSecure documented GREYVIBE, a previously unknown Russian state-aligned APT that uses LLMs “operationally rather than experimentally” across its attacks on Ukrainian government and military networks — the first documented case of a state actor fully integrating LLMs into an ongoing kinetic intelligence campaign. Third, a new campaign is abusing AI chatbot recommendations to redirect users searching for popular tools to cryptojacking malware downloads, with attackers also deploying ScreenConnect for persistent remote access. Per The Hacker News, AI is now compressing the time from vulnerability disclosure to active exploitation — India’s CERT-In has issued 12-hour patch guidance for actively exploited internet-facing flaws.

Source: The Hacker News Weekly Recap · Check Point Research

FTC Antitrust Probe of Microsoft Azure / Copilot Deepens — Six-Plus Competitors Subpoenaed

The Federal Trade Commission has sent civil investigative demands to at least half a dozen Microsoft competitors as part of an intensifying antitrust investigation into whether Azure cloud services and Copilot AI features are being illegally bundled. TechBuzz.AI / The Verge report the agency is examining whether Microsoft leverages its dominant position in Office 365, Windows Server, and Active Directory to favor Azure over Google Cloud and Oracle, and whether Copilot bundling effectively forecloses standalone AI competitors. The probe extends to Microsoft’s OpenAI partnership structure — regulators are scrutinizing whether the arrangement blurs lines between supplier, investor, distributor, and platform owner in ways that evade standard merger review. For federal IT buyers, the near-term practical impact is likely tougher questions around Microsoft licensing portability, bundled discounts, and multi-cloud negotiating power — areas where Changeis clients regularly seek advisory guidance. The investigation represents the opening salvo of what could become the defining AI antitrust proceeding of the decade.

Source: TechBuzz.AI · Windows Forum
Developer Tools & Research

OpenAI Retires GPT-4.5 June 27, o3 in August; GitHub Copilot AI Credits Launch; Gogs Zero-Day RCE Unpatched

Three developer-layer developments merit tracking. OpenAI announced the retirement of GPT-4.5 on June 27, 2026, and plans to retire o3 in August — consolidating its model lineup around GPT-5.5 and o4 Pro. Per OpenAI release notes, enterprise customers on GPT-4.5 API should migrate now. GitHub Copilot AI credits launched this week, allowing developers to trade unused subscription compute — an early sign that AI infrastructure pricing is beginning to exhibit marketplace dynamics. Most urgently: Rapid7 disclosed a critical zero-day remote code execution vulnerability in Gogs, a widely used self-hosted Git service, exploitable by any authenticated attacker via a pull request with a malicious branch name — and on default-configured instances (open registration enabled), an unauthenticated attacker can create an account and immediately exploit the flaw to achieve RCE as the Gogs server process user, enabling full repository access, credential dumping, and supply chain compromise. No patch has been released as of June 1. Organizations running Gogs should disable open registration and restrict repository access immediately. This vulnerability has direct relevance for federal contractors operating self-hosted source control in development environments. The Hacker News rates it among the week’s top threats alongside the PAN-OS GlobalProtect authentication bypass (CVE-2026-0257) now under active exploitation.

Source: The Hacker News · AI Tools Recap · OpenAI Release Notes (via X)
