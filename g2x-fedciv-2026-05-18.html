Industry News & Policy
Fixed-Price EO Implementation: OMB Guidance Due June 14 — FAR Deviations Already in Use
President Trump's April 30 EO "Promoting Efficiency, Accountability, and Performance in Federal Contracting" establishes fixed-price as the default procurement type. The 45-day OMB implementation guidance deadline falls June 14, 2026. FAR Council must propose regulatory amendments within 120 days (by August 28). Critically: agencies are instructed to use FAR deviations "to the maximum extent practicable" immediately — meaning solicitations this summer will already reflect FFP requirements before formal FAR changes are published. Non-FFP contracts above $10M (most civilian agencies), $25M (DHS), or $35M (NASA) require written agency-head approval. Agency heads have until late July to review and restructure the 10 largest non-FFP contracts. R&D and pre-production development are exempt. (White House EO, Gordon Rees May 2026 Legal Update)
GSA AI Safeguarding Clause (GSAR 552.239-7001) — Final Rule Expected This Summer
GSA's proposed GSAR 552.239-7001 "Basic Safeguarding of Artificial Intelligence Systems" clause would apply to any MAS contract involving AI capabilities. Core requirements: (1) disclose all AI systems used in performance within 30 days of award; (2) use only American AI systems — no foreign-developed or foreign-controlled AI; (3) no use of government data to train or improve commercial AI models; (4) government retains ownership of custom AI developments; (5) report security incidents within 72 hours; (6) AI systems may not refuse outputs based on provider's discretionary use policies. Public comment period closed April 3. Final rule expected summer 2026. Contractors using Copilot, Claude, GPT, or similar AI tools in contract performance should assess compliance posture immediately. (Fox Rothschild, Parker Poe May 2026)
FAA Releases Bold 2026–2028 Air Traffic Controller Workforce Plan — Modernization Procurement Signals
On May 15, FAA released its 2026–2028 Air Traffic Controller Workforce Plan targeting 12,563 certified professional controllers (currently ~11,000 deployed). Hiring targets: 2,200 FY26, 2,300 FY27, 2,400 FY28. The plan commits $6B+ of $12.5B allocated under Trump-backed legislation. Technology modernization pillars include: full digital network replacement (analog infrastructure out), AI/ML tools for NAS simulation and traffic management, expanded simulator-based training (up to 27% faster certification), and advanced scheduling tools. Each pillar generates adjacent contracting requirements for systems engineering, program management, and IT modernization support — directly accessible via eFAST, PSS, and SETIS vehicles. (FAA.gov)
NIST AI Cybersecurity Guidelines — Summer 2026 Release; Federal Contractors Must Prepare
NIST is finalizing cybersecurity guidance specifically for AI-driven threats across multiple system types. The guidelines are expected to be released summer 2026 and will become a reference framework for federal AI procurement. Per Nextgov/FCW reporting (May 17), the guidance addresses AI model risk assessments, threat modeling for AI-enabled systems, and human oversight requirements. Contractors whose platforms include AI components — relevant to NAICS 541511/541512/541519 work — should monitor NIST's website for the draft release and begin internal compliance gap assessments ahead of expected agency adoption. (Nextgov/FCW May 17, 2026)
Rule of Two Codification Bill (H.R. 2804) — House Small Business Committee Markup May 20
The House Small Business Committee scheduled a full committee markup of H.R. 2804 (Protecting Small Business Competitions Act, Rep. Velazquez D-NY) for May 20, 2026. The bill would codify the Rule of Two into statute, making competitive set-asides a legal requirement rather than a regulatory preference. This would insulate SB set-aside requirements from future administrative rollback and require agencies to hold more contracts as competitive set-asides rather than sole-sourcing. Net effect: more bid opportunities for competitive SB/SDVOSB/HUBZone/8(a) firms across OASIS+, STARS III, and other vehicles. (JD Supra / PilieroMazza May 14)
DHS Intelligence Reform Bills Advancing — OIA Restructuring Affects CBP/CISA Contractor Pipeline
The House Homeland Security Committee approved a slate of DHS intelligence reform bills the week of May 18. Measures include modernizing terror alert systems, expanding local threat support, and standardizing training under the Office of Intelligence and Analysis (OIA). The Trump administration is simultaneously seeking to restructure OIA. Combined with prior CISA budget cuts (−$707M proposed FY27) and the May 22 DHS appropriations expiration, Changeis should expect continued process friction at CISA, TSA, and OIA-adjacent programs. Contracts that rely on CISA funding should be monitored for scope contraction or re-scoping into CBP/ICE/USCG mission areas. (Nextgov/FCW May 2026)
CICA Stay Override Clarified — Life Science Logistics v. United States (Fed. Cir. 2026)
The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit clarified that a contractor challenging a CICA stay override need only show the override was arbitrary and capricious under the APA — not satisfy the traditional injunction framework. This lowers the bar for protesters challenging agency decisions to override automatic bid protest stays. Practical effect: agencies will face greater scrutiny when attempting to continue contract performance during an active protest. Contractors on competitive vehicles like T4NG2, OASIS+, and STARS III should understand this protection when considering filing a protest. (Gordon Rees May 2026)
