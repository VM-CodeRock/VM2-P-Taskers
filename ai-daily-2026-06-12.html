Federal AI Policy & Procurement
CISA BOD 26-04 — Risk-Based Vulnerability Management

CISA issued Binding Operational Directive 26-04 on June 10, 2026, representing the most aggressive federal patch timeline in history. The directive replaces BOD 19-02 and BOD 22-01 with a 16-tier risk matrix driven by four binary factors: public exposure, KEV catalog presence, exploit automatability, and degree of attacker control. Highest-tier vulnerabilities (KEV-listed, public-facing, automatable, total-control) must be remediated within three calendar days with mandatory forensic triage. Lower-risk flaws can be deferred to next scheduled upgrade. Per GovCIO Media, the phased rollout requires immediate policy updates, 60-day process alignment (by ~Aug 9), and 180-day full compliance (by Dec 7, 2026). Acting Director Nick Andersen confirmed this is a direct response to AI-accelerated adversarial exploitation.

EO 14409 — AI Cybersecurity (July 2 Deadline Approaching)

The June 2 EO "Promoting Advanced AI Innovation and Security" imposed a 30-day window for CISA to issue BODs on AI-enabled cyber defense — BOD 26-04 partially fulfills this. Per JD Supra, a separate 30-day deadline requires Treasury, NSA, and CISA to stand up an AI Cybersecurity Clearinghouse (deadline ~July 2, 2026). The 60-day window (by ~August 1) requires a classified benchmarking framework for frontier AI model capability assessment. Frontier AI developers may voluntarily provide 30-day pre-release model access to government.

NSPM-12 — Cloud Security for National Security Systems

President Trump signed NSPM-12 establishing cybersecurity governance for NSS cloud environments. Within 90 days, the CNSS must report on cloud provisioning baselines for Secret, TS Collateral, TS/SCI, and SAP levels. Within 120 days, CSPs hosting NSS must submit secure configuration baselines. The memorandum explicitly coordinates with the NSPM-11 computing roadmap.

NSPM-11 — Defense AI Multi-Vendor Mandate (Tracking)

Signed June 5, NSPM-11 requires national security agencies to hold contracts with multiple AI vendors within 120 days (deadline: October 3, 2026). Per AI Magazine, the Secretary of War has 90 days to update DOD Directive 3000.09 on autonomous weapons (deadline Sept 3). TEVV methodology standards are due October 3. The directive bars single-vendor lock-in following the DoW/Anthropic contract cancellation episode.

GSA AI Directive — Supersedes CIO 2185.1B

GSA published an updated internal AI directive on June 12 (Accelerating Responsible Use of AI at GSA), superseding CIO 2185.1B. Key requirements: AI governance boards, public AI strategy publication, Chief AI Officer empowerment, lifecycle risk assessments, AI-ready data infrastructure planning, and preference for American-made AI solutions. Aligns with OMB M-25-21, M-25-22, and M-26-04.

NITAAC Sunset — October 29, 2026 Confirmed

NIH's contracting arm NITAAC formally notified prime contractors that all GWACs (CIO-SP3, CIO-SP3 Small Business, CIO-CS) sunset October 29, 2026 as the last day for new task order awards. Per FedScoop, performance on existing orders can extend through December 31, 2028. New orders placed after June 8 cannot extend past that date. Functions transfer to GSA. GSA confirmed readiness to absorb NITAAC workload.

Legal & Regulatory
Amazon v. Perplexity — Ninth Circuit Oral Arguments (June 11)

The Ninth Circuit heard oral arguments on June 11, 2026 in Seattle in Amazon.com Services LLC v. Perplexity AI, Inc. — case no. 26-1444. This is the first federal appellate test of whether the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA) applies to an AI agent that acts under explicit user authorization via the user's own credentials. Per ChatForest (updated June 12), the panel received Perplexity's arguments with "notable skepticism" per Bloomberg Law. No ruling was issued; written decisions on preliminary injunction appeals typically arrive within several months.

The core dispute: Amazon contends its cease-and-desist plus technical countermeasures revoked authorization, and Perplexity's update to defeat those measures constitutes CFAA unauthorized access. Perplexity argues that under Van Buren v. United States (SCOTUS 2021), it is the user — not Perplexity — who accesses Amazon's computers; Comet is merely the user's technological delegate. Per Truth on the Market, Amazon's case rests on three key facts: Amazon deployed a technical block targeting Comet; Perplexity updated within 24 hours to defeat it; and Comet spoofed Chrome's user-agent string. The ACLU filed an amicus brief warning of chilling effects on journalism and research tools.

Four possible outcomes remain open: affirm injunction (Amazon wins, Comet blocked); reverse (Perplexity wins on Van Buren); remand (insufficient district court analysis); or narrow ruling on California Penal Code §502 or First Amendment grounds. The ruling will bind all nine Western states and likely influence agentic AI access law nationally.

NYT v. OpenAI — Motion to Dismiss Denied

A New York district judge denied OpenAI's motion to dismiss copyright infringement claims brought by the New York Times and Daily News publishers, per Global Legal Post. Judge Stein found plaintiffs "plausibly alleged" end-user infringement and that OpenAI knew or had reason to know. OpenAI succeeded in dismissing some DMCA claims (where outputs were not "close to identical" to originals), but copyright infringement, contributory infringement, and trademark dilution (Daily News) proceed. The case is now consolidated in SDNY.

EU AI Act — Transparency Code & August 2 Deadline

The European Commission published a voluntary Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content on June 10, 2026. Per ComplexDiscovery, the hard deadline for AI watermarking obligations under Article 50 of the AI Act is August 2, 2026, with a four-month grace period for systems already on market (compliance by December 2, 2026). High-risk AI system obligations under Articles 8-17 apply from December 2, 2027 (standalone) and August 2, 2028 (product-embedded).

New EO — Classified AI Model Evaluations

A June 2, 2026 executive order shifts AI model evaluations for frontier models to a classified framework run by Treasury, NSA, and CISA, per KuCoin News. The Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) has ceased public benchmark publishing. Companies must allow 30-day government review before public release (voluntary). Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google DeepMind are covered.
