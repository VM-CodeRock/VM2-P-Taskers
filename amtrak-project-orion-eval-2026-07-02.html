Scope — 4 Primary Tasks

Task 1 — Virtual Data Room (VDR): Establish and host a secure US-based VDR. Provide indexing, search, administration, user support, and access control. FRA and its legal advisors must have secure external access. Transition VDR data to Amtrak-owned platform upon engagement completion.
Task 2 — Contract & Asset Inventory: Screen, organize, and index an estimated 30,000–35,000 contracts and assets across 10 categories (financing/debt, grants, operating agreements, vendor contracts, capex, real estate, equipment, labor, regulatory approvals, other). Produce a searchable, sortable Excel Inventory & Tracker Matrix.
Task 3 — Transferability Analysis: For a subset, analyze each contract/asset for transfer restrictions, consent requirements, notification obligations, affiliate assignment provisions, and financial consequences. Classify each: Freely Assignable / Assignable with Notice / Assignable with Consent / Non-Assignable. 14-field Transferability Matrix per Exhibit 1.
Task 4 — Risk Identification & Prioritization: Flag High-Risk items per 8-criteria framework (service disruption, termination risk, financial exposure, grant/federal interest impact, consent uncertainty, regulatory constraints, real property, implementation complexity). Provide mitigation pathways, escalation recommendations, real estate cost assessment, and executive summary.

Competitive Landscape & Procurement Context

Amtrak Procurement Rules

Amtrak (National Railroad Passenger Corporation) is a federally-chartered corporation established under 49 U.S.C. § 24301. It is not subject to the Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR). Amtrak uses its own procurement rules, generally mirroring commercial best practices. This means:

  • No small business set-aside requirements; no SBA socioeconomic program applicability
  • No SAM.gov registration required (Amtrak is subject to FOIA, not FAR procurement)
  • Competition is commercial-style; best-value selection with negotiation is standard
  • No GSA schedule or other federal contract vehicle provides any competitive advantage
  • North Highland's "contract vehicle" reference likely means an existing Amtrak vendor relationship, not a federal IDIQ
  • The SOW explicitly states "No Obligation to Award" — highly flexible procurement process

Red Flags & Dealbreakers

Red Flag 1 — Scope Mismatch: This is fundamentally a legal due diligence engagement. The core deliverable (Transferability Matrix) requires analysis of assignment clauses, consent standards, default provisions, and legal risk — work requiring attorneys or contract law specialists. Changeis does not have this capability in-house. Attempting to prime this engagement without legal advisory capability would likely result in a non-competitive proposal on Evaluation Criterion #2 (Relevant Experience).
Red Flag 2 — Cybersecurity Infrastructure Gap: The SOW requires US-only data hosting in dedicated, isolated environments with FIPS 140-2 Level 2 encryption, SIEM integration, SOC2/ISO 27001 documentation, and work conducted on Amtrak-managed Citrix VDI. Meeting these requirements independently would require significant infrastructure investment or a proven technology partner. This cannot be addressed in the proposal without a named, credentialed VDR platform partner.
Yellow Flag — Questions Deadline Passed: Changeis was not engaged during the Q&A phase (June 24 deadline). Competitors who submitted questions likely have clearer scope understanding and informal relationship-building advantage with the procurement team. This disadvantage cannot be fully overcome.
Yellow Flag — Brian Pickerall's Internal Skepticism: The SVP leading BD explicitly called this "a stretch." This accurately reflects the capability gap. Any pursuit must be firmly scoped to a sub-contractor role with defined, bounded deliverables rather than leading. Pursuing as prime would be a misallocation of proposal resources.
