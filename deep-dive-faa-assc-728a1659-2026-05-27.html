2. ASSC Decoded — What Is Being Procured

"ASSC" stands for Acquisition Support Services Contract — this is the FAA's vehicle to provide embedded acquisition workforce augmentation to its Acquisition and Contracting (AAQ) organization. It is not an aviation safety information-sharing system; it is a professional services staffing/advisory contract for contracting professionals inside FAA's procurement shop.

Core Services (from Draft PWS)

DisciplineOPM Series Equiv.GS Equiv.Description
Contract Specialist1102 (Contracting)GS-5 to GS-14Solicitation development, source selection support, evaluation panel support, award documentation, modifications, closeout

Contract Administrator0343 (Mgmt Analyst)GS-5 to GS-14COR support, deliverable review, performance monitoring, contract file maintenance, invoice processing, correspondence tracking
Critical Constraint — No Inherently Governmental Functions: The PWS explicitly prohibits contractor personnel from obligating appropriated funds or executing contractual agreements on behalf of FAA. All procurement decisions remain with FAA contracting officers. This constraint is directly relevant to OCI — Changeis personnel would be supporting FAA COs, not making final decisions.

6. OCI Analysis — FAR 9.5 / FAA AMS T3.1.7

Regulatory Framework Note: The FAA is NOT subject to the FAR. FAA acquisitions are governed by the Acquisition Management System (AMS). However, FAA's AMS OCI guidance (T3.1.7) is explicitly modeled on FAR Subpart 9.5 and uses identical three-category taxonomy. All FAR 9.5 analysis below maps directly to FAA AMS T3.1.7 provisions. Key AMS clauses: 3.1.7-1 (Exclusion from Future Contracts), 3.1.7-2 (OCI), 3.1.7-4 (OCI Mitigation Plan), 3.1.7-5 (Disclosure of Conflicts), 3.1.7-6 (Disclosure of Certain Employee Relationships). See FAA OCI Guidance (Dec 2021) ↗
MEDIUM RISK Type 1: Unequal Access to Information (AMS T3.1.7 / FAR 9.505-4)

Definition: A contractor gains access to nonpublic information as part of performing an FAA contract, providing a competitive advantage in a later competition for another FAA contract.

Applicable Changeis Contracts:

  • FAA CLMRS (active) — Changeis has access to FAA Logistics Center acquisition strategies, procurement plans, IGCE data, and AMS milestone documentation for supply chain programs. If AAQ-700 (Aeronautical Center) or AAQ-900 (which handles some logistics-adjacent work) receives orders under ASSC, Changeis' CLMRS team may have seen nonpublic acquisition data that could inform its ASSC bid.
  • FAA eFAST AHR (693KA9-23-F-00043) — Changeis performed acquisition support for FAA's Office of Accountability and Strategic Business Management. This work included reviewing procurement plans, supporting source selection, and advising on acquisition strategies within AAQ-adjacent organizations. Personnel from this contract may have had exposure to AAQ workload forecasting, acquisition pipeline, and internal resource gaps — exactly the information FAA would use to size the ASSC requirement.
  • FAA ATAPS II (693KA9-24-F-00096) — Changeis supports 8 ATO directorates. While ATO (Air Traffic Organization) is distinct from AAQ, the ATAPS II work includes programmatic and financial planning that touches AAQ processes when ATO programs go through the AMS investment lifecycle.

Specific Concern: During the CLMRS and eFAST AHR performance periods, Changeis personnel may have seen: (a) internal FAA documents about acquisition workforce capacity, (b) AAQ staffing plans or resource gap analyses that directly informed the ASSC market survey (6973GH-25-SS-00002), or (c) IGCEs for individual AAQ division contracts that are being consolidated under ASSC. This nonpublic information could give Changeis an unfair pricing advantage.

FAA AMS Trigger: AMS T3.1.7 states: "If the requirements of the FAA procurement anticipate the successful vendor may have access to nonpublic information, all vendors should be required to submit and negotiate an acceptable mitigation plan." The ASSC solicitation, when issued, should include this requirement.

Risk Assessment: MEDIUM. The information access is indirect — CLMRS is supply chain/logistics, not AAQ contracting-staff augmentation. eFAST AHR is the most direct concern. However, the work was performed for different offices (AHR = Office of Accountability, not AAQ). Changeis should affirmatively disclose and certify that no nonpublic acquisition information was retained or used.
HIGH RISK Type 2: Impaired Objectivity (AMS T3.1.7 / FAR 9.505-3) — PRIMARY CONCERN

Definition: A contractor is placed in a position of providing assessment and evaluation findings over itself (or a related entity), where the contractor's financial or other business relationship to the entity being evaluated could undermine impartial advice to FAA.

This is the most serious OCI vector for Changeis on ASSC. Here is the specific chain of concern:

  1. Changeis is currently performing acquisition support work for FAA — specifically ATAPS II ($103M), CLMRS ($62M active), and eFAST AHR. These contracts are active FAA procurements.
  2. ASSC tasks include source selection evaluation support — Contract Specialist (1102) personnel under ASSC would support FAA COs in evaluating proposals, developing source selection criteria, and providing "technical assistance in the evaluation of proposals received in response to the solicitation."
  3. The ASSC contractor could be positioned to support evaluations that involve Changeis itself — if any of the AAQ divisions issues a recompete or new task order under eFAST, SETIS, OASIS+, or a standalone vehicle for the type of work Changeis performs (IT/management consulting, acquisition support, program management), and if Changeis bids on that procurement, then the ASSC contract specialist supporting that AAQ division's source selection could potentially be a Changeis employee.
  4. ATAPS II recompete exposure — ATAPS II is a 5-year contract (expires ~2029). If the ASSC is awarded in late 2027, ASSC contract specialists supporting ATO acquisitions could be positioned near the ATAPS II recompete or follow-on task orders. Changeis ASSC personnel supporting AJT or AJG could conceivably see or influence procurement documents for work Changeis itself would bid on.

Applicable Changeis Contracts Creating the Concern:

  • ATAPS II (693KA9-24-F-00096) — $103M, currently active. ASSC personnel in AAQ-300 or AAQ-400 could support source selection for any ATO-facing follow-on work Changeis bids on.
  • CLMRS (active) — $62M, currently active. ASSC personnel in AAQ-700 (Aeronautical Center, which manages the CLMRS contract vehicle) could be positioned to see CLMRS recompete planning documentation.
  • FAA OASIS+ Task Orders — If Changeis holds OASIS+ task orders through FAA, ASSC personnel in any AAQ division that awards OASIS+ orders could be supporting FAA COs who evaluate proposals on vehicles Changeis is positioned on.
  • FAA APEX (previously bid) — Changeis invested in the FAA APEX bid. If APEX-related follow-on work is acquired through AAQ-supported processes, ASSC personnel could inadvertently support evaluations related to programs Changeis has knowledge of from proposal preparation.

FAA AMS Requirement: "If the successful vendor may be in a position to provide evaluations and assessments of itself or corporate siblings, or other entity with which it has a significant financial relationship, the affected contractor should provide a mitigation plan that includes recusal by the vendor from the affected contract work. Such recusal might include divestiture of the work to a third party vendor."

Risk Assessment: HIGH — but MITIGABLE. This is a real, structural conflict. However, FAA AMS expressly provides for mitigation rather than automatic exclusion. The ODRA (FAA's Office of Dispute Resolution for Acquisition) has confirmed that the Contracting Officer has sole authority to waive unmitigated OCIs (see ODRA Case D20_00872). The path forward is a proactive, detailed OCI Mitigation Plan submitted with the proposal.
LOW RISK Type 3: Biased Ground Rules (AMS T3.1.7 / FAR 9.505-1, 9.505-2)

Definition: A contractor, in the course of performance, establishes important ground rules (specifications, evaluation criteria, standards) that could favor its own capabilities in a later competition.

Analysis: This risk is LOW for Changeis on ASSC. The biased ground rules concern applies when a contractor helps write the statement of work or evaluation criteria for a competition. In this case:

  • The ASSC PWS has already been drafted by FAA internally (draft PWS released with the market survey).
  • Changeis is not involved in writing the ASSC solicitation documents.
  • Changeis' market survey feedback (which FAA is soliciting) may influence pricing structure, but FAA's stated policy is that feedback will be publicly posted and sanitized of company identification — all vendors are on equal footing.
  • The only residual risk is if Changeis' CLMRS or eFAST AHR work involved helping FAA plan or document the consolidation strategy that led to the ASSC. This is unlikely but should be affirmatively reviewed.

Specific Contracts to Review for Biased Ground Rules:

  • eFAST AHR (693KA9-23-F-00043): Review task order deliverables to confirm no Changeis work product contributed to the ASSC market survey, PWS, or acquisition strategy documentation.
  • CLMRS: Confirm no Changeis deliverables in AMS acquisition planning phases touched AAQ-700 consolidation planning.
Risk Assessment: LOW. No evidence that Changeis contributed to ASSC requirements development. FAA's market survey process was open to all vendors. Recommend a one-paragraph certification in the OCI plan confirming no involvement in ASSC PWS preparation.
OCI Verdict: MITIGABLE

Dominant Risk: Impaired Objectivity (High) — Changeis ASSC personnel could support source selection or evaluation activities that involve Changeis' own active contracts (ATAPS II, CLMRS) or bids on FAA work.

Secondary Risk: Unequal Access to Information (Medium) — eFAST AHR performance may have included exposure to AAQ-adjacent acquisition planning data.

Tertiary Risk: Biased Ground Rules (Low) — No indication Changeis influenced ASSC requirements development.

ODRA Precedent: OCI is Waivable Under FAA AMS

The FAA's ODRA confirmed in Case D20_00872 (CSRA, 2020) that the FAA Contracting Officer has sole authority to waive unmitigated OCIs when "in the agency's best interest to award a contract notwithstanding a conflict of interest" (AMS Clause 3.1.7(e)). This means even if the FAA CO finds Changeis' mitigation plan insufficient, an OCI waiver is legally available — it is not an automatic disqualification. Prior ODRA decisions also show that in protest contexts, impaired objectivity is mitigable via recusal (see D17_00814 — CSRA protest).
