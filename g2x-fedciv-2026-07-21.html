2 FedCiv Roundup — Past 24–48 Hours (July 19–21, 2026)

Curated bullets from GAO, Treasury/IRS watchdog reporting, GSA, DOI, USDOT, and legal/policy trackers. Sources linked inline.

  • OMB Uniform Grants Regulation — post-comment status: The 45-day comment period on OMB's rewrite of 2 CFR Part 200 (Uniform Guidance) closed July 13, 2026 with more than 341,000 public comments logged; Sen. Susan Collins (R-ME), chair of the Senate Appropriations Committee, formally asked OMB Director Russell Vought to extend the window by 45 days and withdraw key provisions, but OMB has signaled it will not extend and is targeting an October 1, 2026 effective date for FY2027 awards (University of Washington Federal Relations).
  • Uniform Guidance rewrite — substantive risk flagged: Federal News Network reports the proposed rule would disallow fixed-price/fixed-amount grants entirely, a notable divergence from the government's broader shift toward outcome-based contracting, and adds a pre-issuance political review step for discretionary grants (Federal News Network).
  • GAO bid protest roundup, week of July 13: GAO issued six decisions with zero sustains. Notable: Sea Box, Inc. (B-424332) denied — GAO upheld DLA's Rule of Two analysis leaving small-business set-aside CLINs unrestricted; Absolute Strategic Technologies (B-424321.2 et al.) denied in part/dismissed in part on cost realism vs. price realism grounds; Pacific Ohana Laulima (B-424314 et al.) denied, with GAO reaffirming adjectival ratings alone don't guarantee advancement in two-phase competitions (GAO Protest Roundup, Charles Veigel).
  • GAO/DHS jurisdiction ruling: GAO dismissed a protest challenging a DHS sole-source task order for border barrier construction, holding that procurements conducted under an IIRIRA waiver fall within the exclusive jurisdiction of federal district courts — not GAO (Morgan Lewis GovCon Update).
  • Treasury/IRS — TIGTA data security alert: A new Treasury Inspector General for Tax Administration report found 14 contractor employees made 1,375 unauthorized entries into restricted Zero Paper Initiative facilities, an unsecured loading dock with no guard controls at one site, and 128 of 269 (48%) system vulnerabilities unresolved past IRS deadlines — including 20 critical vulnerabilities open an average of 223 days versus a 30-day requirement (Forbes).
  • GSA leadership moves: GSA Administrator Ed Forst appointed Michael Gelber as acting commissioner of the Public Buildings Service, shifting Andrew Heller into a newly created dual-deputy-commissioner structure; GSA also named Larry Allen to lead the Southeast region and Robert Ortiz (ex-Cushman & Wakefield) as PBS Western Region administrator (Federal News Network).
  • GSA Emerging Technology Showcase (July 30): GSA will take its internal "moonshot" tech-efficiency effort governmentwide at a July 30 showcase at DOI's Yates Auditorium, live-streamed to a virtual audience expected in the tens of thousands, spotlighting AI, automation, and low/no-code modernization wins (Nextgov/FCW, GSA).
  • GSA GAO audit exposure: A July 13 GAO report identified 11 unresolved priority recommendations for GSA spanning federal real property management, shared services, and award oversight — GSA closed three of eight original items over the past year but picked up six new ones in June 2026 (Legis1).
  • DOI $484M Cisco enterprise IDIQ: Interior's Interior Business Center is running a total small-business set-aside for an enterprise-wide Cisco IDIQ covering roughly 2,400 CONUS/OCONUS locations — $149M software licensing (single award) plus $335M hardware (up to nine awards, two reserved for Indian Small Business Economic Enterprises). Proposals are due July 31, 2026; ordering period begins September 30, 2026 (Sweetspot / SAM.gov).
  • USDOT/FAA — air traffic control modernization update: USDOT reports FAA has replaced one-third of all legacy copper wiring, installed 148 new radio systems, and deployed surface awareness systems across 44 towers under the $12.5B air traffic control overhaul funded by the One Big Beautiful Bill; the department is also surging air traffic controller hiring, up 20% year-over-year (USDOT).
  • Civilian contract award activity (July 17–20): Leidos secured an $862M TSA logistics contract, the largest single award in a 23-contract, $3.06B civilian-dominated 24-hour window (22 of 23 awards civilian); FocalPoint won a $44M Treasury Asset Forfeiture Fund support contract; Cadmus Group booked a $39M DOT FRA realty support contract; Kentro (IT Concepts) was awarded an $85M SSA task order (Gunpowder Alerts, GovBrew).
  • DOI policy actions: The Fish and Wildlife Service finalized additional Endangered Species Act regulation reforms (July 17), and Interior/Commerce jointly rescinded the prior regulatory "harm" definition under the ESA (July 10) — both relevant to DOI's energy-permitting and natural-resources contracting pipeline (U.S. Department of the Interior).
