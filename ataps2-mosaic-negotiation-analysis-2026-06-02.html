Situation Summary

Changeis holds the prime contract for ATAPS II under the FAA eFAST vehicle. Mosaic ATM (CEO Chris Brinton) has been performing as a subcontractor via an Authorization to Proceed (ATP), which expired June 2, 2026. A formal subcontract was not executed before the ATP expiration. Two clauses are in dispute: Clause 20 (IP/Inventions) and Clause 29 (Non-Solicitation of Client). Both disputes stem from Mosaic's position that their work under ATAPS II is operationally entangled with work they perform under other FAA contracts on different vehicles.

Clause 20 — IP/Inventions: Analysis
Changeis Position: LEGALLY SOUND
Mosaic Request: COMMERCIALLY UNDERSTANDABLE BUT LEGALLY PROBLEMATIC
Changeis Position Assessment

Strong. FAA eFAST (and most federal IT contracts) flows down Government data rights requirements. The cited clauses — AMS 3.5-13 (Rights in Data – General), AMS 3.5-10 (Patent Rights – Ownership by Contractor), and AMS 3.5-12 (Patent Rights – Ownership by Government) — require the prime to hold clear, passable rights to all deliverables. Joint ownership between prime and sub creates a legal ambiguity: under joint ownership, either party can independently license or use the jointly-owned IP without the other's consent (unless contractually restricted). This directly undercuts the Government's ability to get clean rights through the prime. Changeis's refusal of joint ownership is not just a commercial preference — it is almost certainly a compliance requirement under the prime contract.

Mosaic Position Assessment

Mosaic's argument is commercially intuitive ("joint ownership doesn't take away your rights") but legally imprecise in a federal contracting context. Federal data rights frameworks depend on a clear chain from performer to prime to Government — joint ownership breaks that chain. However, Mosaic's underlying concern is legitimate: they are investing their core systems, data science, and flight data expertise into this work, and they do not want to permanently surrender all rights to work product that overlaps with their independent R&D.

Root cause of the impasse: Mosaic conflates two distinct categories of IP — (1) newly-created work product under ATAPS II (where Changeis must hold exclusive rights by prime contract obligation) and (2) their pre-existing background IP and independently-developed capabilities. The draft Clause 20 already addresses the second category: it includes a standard "Prior Works" carve-out where Mosaic retains its background IP and licenses it to Changeis for use under the contract.

Recommended Resolution — Clause 20

The Changeis position is substantively correct. The path to resolution is clarifying the distinction between "Deliverables IP" (must belong exclusively to Changeis per AMS flow-downs) and "Background/Methods IP" (Mosaic retains ownership; licenses to Changeis). Specifically:

  • Make the "Prior Works" carve-out broader and more explicit — name Mosaic's specific platform/toolsets if possible (e.g., FMDS, AirportViewer-related data infrastructure)
  • Add a sentence confirming Mosaic may continue to develop, commercialize, and use its own data science methods, even if similar methods are used in ATAPS II deliverables — as long as the specific ATAPS II deliverables themselves belong to Changeis/Government
  • Do NOT concede joint ownership of deliverables — this is a prime contract compliance issue, not just a negotiating preference
Clause 29 — Non-Solicitation: Analysis
Changeis Position (Clarified): REASONABLE
Mosaic Demand (Full Removal): OVERREACHING
Changeis Position Assessment

The clarified Clause 29 language Ara offered is well-calibrated. It narrows the restriction to soliciting the specific ATAPS II scope directly (i.e., preventing Mosaic from going around Changeis to pick up the same SOW on a different vehicle), while explicitly carving out all other FAA work. This is standard non-circumvention language and is a legitimate prime contractor protection. The 12-month duration is industry-standard. The explicit carve-out for other vehicles addresses Mosaic's stated concern almost entirely.

Mosaic Position Assessment

Mosaic's demand for full removal is an overcorrection. Their stated reason (work spans multiple FAA contracts) is addressed by the carve-out language already offered. The actual concern may be that Chris fears even the clarified language could be interpreted broadly enough to block future FAA opportunities. That concern is addressable through tighter scoping — not through full removal. Full removal would leave Changeis with zero protection against Mosaic using ATAPS II access to disintermediate them on the exact same work — which is an unreasonable ask of any prime contractor.

Recommended Resolution — Clause 29

Hold the substance of Clause 29 with the clarified language already offered. If Mosaic needs additional comfort, offer to:

  • Reference the specific contract number (ATAPS II Work Order 11-B) rather than using "Client" generally — making the restriction scope crystal clear
  • Reduce the post-term duration from 12 months to 6 months if needed as a compromise signal
  • Add a specific list of what is not covered: "For the avoidance of doubt, this Section does not restrict Mosaic from competing for or performing: (a) ATAPS I renewals under separate vehicles, (b) any work under NextGen or other FAA programs not under eFAST Work Order 11-B, (c) any independently-initiated FAA research or data science work"
  • Do NOT remove Clause 29 entirely — this is a core prime contractor protection

  1. Hold exclusive ownership of ATAPS II deliverables (non-negotiable per prime flow-downs)
  2. Offer a more explicit and named "Prior Works" carve-out that specifically acknowledges Mosaic's FMDS toolsets, data science methods, and infrastructure as their own property

  1. Keep the substance of Clause 29 with Ara's clarified language

  3. Offer to reduce post-term restriction from 12 to 6 months as a gesture
  4. Add a specific affirmative list of what Mosaic can freely do (other FAA vehicles, NextGen, independent R&D)

Fallback position (if Mosaic remains intractable):

  • Formally document that Mosaic elected to stop work unilaterally pending contract execution; ensure Changeis is not exposed for breach
  • Begin parallel sourcing of alternate subcontractor capability for the FMDS analytical roles — even if only as negotiating leverage and contingency planning
  • Consider a time-limited bridge agreement (Letter Subcontract or Subcontract Work Order) that locks in the essential terms (payment, scope, IP fundamentals) and defers remaining negotiation, to get Mosaic staff back on the project
Bottom Line

Changeis's legal and contractual positions on both clauses are sound. The dispute is resolvable — Mosaic's underlying concerns (protecting their background IP, maintaining freedom to operate with FAA) are addressable without conceding on the substance. The main risk is a prolonged standoff that disrupts FAA deliverables. A direct call to resolve the specific language — not the principles — is the fastest path to closure.
