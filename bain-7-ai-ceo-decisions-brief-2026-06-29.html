Bain & Company: 7 AI Decisions No CEO Can Delegate

Source: Turn Artificial Intelligence into Proprietary Intelligence — Bain & Company

Briefed for: Varun Malhotra, CEO — Changeis Inc. — Federal IT & AI/Automation Firm (~70 employees, growth stage)
Executive Summary
  • Bain surveyed 100 CEOs and found a structural divide: companies pulling ahead are not running better pilots — they are building proprietary intelligence through unique data, encoded workflows, and learning architectures that compound over time.
  • Seven decisions separate AI transformation leaders from laggards: Posture, Domain Focus, Proprietary Data, Technology Architecture, Operating Model, Learning System, and Governance — none can be delegated below the CEO level.
  • Roughly 85% of CEOs are using AI primarily for near-term cost reduction and productivity. The 15% who treat it as competitive reinvestment are building moats today that will be structurally expensive to catch up to in three to five years.
  • The core competitive asset is not the AI model (which commoditizes quickly) but the orchestration layer tying proprietary data, workflows, and agents together — this must be built internally and cannot be purchased from any vendor.
  • For Changeis, this framework is highly actionable: federal contracting, MAPS, ATEPS-II, and FAA relationships represent both the domain bets and the proprietary data foundations that, if built deliberately now, become a defensible moat within 12–24 months.

Detailed Breakdown: The 7 Decisions
1
Posture
This is a strategic choice, not a technology decision.

Bain Framing

  • AI transformation posture is a board-level bet, not an IT roadmap.
  • Waiting is a choice — and increasingly the wrong one. The advantage compounds from day one.
  • Confusing pilots and use cases with strategic commitment is the most common failure mode.
  • You cannot buy the orchestration layer. It must be built. This requires engineering talent and culture most large organizations have allowed to atrophy.
  • Declaring AI leadership while funding it for in-year ROI is a strategic statement, not a strategic commitment.

Why It Matters

  • Every quarter of delay allows competitors to widen data moats, deepen software capability, and accumulate organizational learning that becomes structurally expensive to close.
  • AI is not like cloud computing — late adoption cannot catch up easily by spending more later.
  • The gap is not primarily a technology gap but a software development and organizational culture gap.

Changeis Context

  • Changeis has signaled an AI/automation posture publicly and is executing on MAPS and AI tooling — this is above-average for a 70-person federal contractor.
  • Risk: pilots may still dominate the portfolio. The posture decision means committing multiyear capital to a specific strategic position Varun narrates personally.
  • FAA, ATEPS-II, and adjacent federal relationships are the moat. The posture decision is: what kind of AI company does Changeis intend to be by 2029?
2
Domain Focus
Only the CEO can unlock the constraints that matter.

Bain Framing

  • Spreading AI across too many use cases is the single biggest execution mistake. Three to five concentrated bets beats broad coverage every time.
  • Right domains sit at: competitive advantage intersection + data richness + end-to-end reinvention potential.
  • 85% of CEOs prioritize near-term cost reduction. Leaders focus on competitive reinvention.
  • Only the CEO can break job definitions, silo boundaries, process ownership, and incentive structures that bottom-up teams work around.

Why It Matters

  • Diffused talent and attention produces incremental improvements, not competitive advantage.
  • Transformations that reshape competitive position require CEO sponsorship to remove structural constraints no middle manager has authority to remove.
  • Domain bets require multiyear capital committed before P&L proof — only board-level commitment enables this.

Changeis Context

  • Candidate domains: (1) aviation safety data workflows (FAA/ATEPS-II), (2) contract intelligence (SAM/GSA/eBuy automation), (3) proposal and BD automation.
  • MAPS is a domain candidate if the underlying data is proprietary enough. Verify: does MAPS generate proprietary operational signal or is it generic?
  • Varun must personally sponsor the workflow redesign decisions for whichever 2–3 domains are chosen — especially breaking Changeis's own process constraints.
3
Proprietary Data
A key part of your competitive moat.

Bain Framing

  • Models commoditize. Proprietary data is the durable differentiator — what competitors cannot purchase.
  • Most enterprise data is not AI-ready: fragmented, inconsistently defined, missing a semantic layer.
  • Building the semantic layer (shared business vocabulary for agents to reason in business terms) is the foundational work that determines whether AI scales or stalls.
  • Data strategy must be anchored in domain bets, not built as generic infrastructure.
  • In an agentic world, governance must be embedded in systems in real time — not periodic audits.

Why It Matters

  • Companies building data moats today will be structurally harder to compete against in 3–5 years.
  • Generic data modernization programs are expensive, slow, and usually disconnected from business outcomes.
  • Live operational signal continuously replenished is the renewable competitive asset.

Changeis Context

  • Key proprietary data assets to identify and protect: FAA operational records, ATEPS-II incident/inspection data, historical contract outcomes, proposal win/loss patterns.
  • Semantic layer work: define consistent terminology across contracts, safety reports, and workforce data so agents can reason across systems.
  • Governance: as Changeis deploys agents on federal data, real-time access controls and lineage tracking are not optional — they are a compliance requirement and a moat.
4
Technology Architecture
Own enterprise orchestration.

Bain Framing

  • The most important architecture decision is not which model to use — models are obsolete within months.
  • Own the enterprise orchestration layer: where agents, tools, and skills are managed as first-class enterprise assets with governed access.
  • Ceding orchestration to a single vendor hands them architectural control of your AI estate and limits you to their roadmap.
  • Agentic systems are probabilistic and stateful; they drift, fail in cascades, and are vulnerable to adversarial injection. Traditional governance is not fit for this.
  • The code is disposable; the semantic layer, registries, and engineering muscle are the durable assets.

Why It Matters

  • Build now even if today's implementation will be obsolete in months — early builders accumulate the durable assets (semantic layer, registries, engineering muscle) that latecomers must pay a premium to acquire.
  • Without shared infrastructure, every agent team duplicates foundational work; with a governed platform, assets are reusable across domains.

Changeis Context

  • Changeis is an AI tooling firm — the orchestration layer is a client deliverable AND an internal asset. Building it well is doubly strategic.
  • Action: define which parts of the AI stack are owned internally vs. vendor-sourced. Ensure the orchestration of federal workflow agents is NOT fully vendor-locked.
  • ATEPS-II and MAPS agents should run through an internally governed orchestration layer with full audit trail capability — critical for federal oversight requirements.
  • Reassess current SaaS stack: which vendors' value propositions are being eroded by AI-native alternatives?
5
Operating Model
Design for speed, then build the culture to sustain it.

Bain Framing

  • Strategy and architecture mean nothing without an operating model built to deliver at pace.
  • Workflow redesign and workforce transformation must happen simultaneously — not sequentially.
  • AI-native software development is the first function to fully transform: organizations making this shift report 5–10x output from the same engineer headcount.
  • Democratize access — tinkerers outside tech roles discover use cases central teams would not design.
  • CEOs and direct reports must personally build and experiment inside tools, not just supervise.

Why It Matters

  • Speed is a strategic asset. Organizations compounding learning faster pull ahead of those executing well but slowly.
  • Old habits take hold if systems, incentives, and role definitions are not changed simultaneously with workflow redesign.
  • End state: every businessperson builds working prototypes in low-code/no-code. Digital acumen as common as financial literacy.

Changeis Context

  • At 70 employees, Changeis has the speed advantage of a small firm. Ship AI workflow improvements every 2–3 weeks, not quarters.
  • Hiring priority: identify internal tinkerers; give them sandbox access and a clear path to propose scalable use cases.
  • Workforce: as MAPS and ATEPS-II workflows are redesigned, be explicit with affected staff about reskilling pathways and role redefinitions — social contract clarity is a CEO responsibility.
  • Varun should personally spend time weekly inside AI tools used by the delivery team — not just as oversight but as building personal fluency.
6
The Learning System
Design the transformation to compound.

Bain Framing

  • Most AI programs are designed to deliver value but not to learn. The most durable competitive advantage comes from building learning into the program's architecture.
  • Every agent workflow needs: (1) per-agent feedback loops and (2) a shared context and memory layer so the next agent can draw on what prior agents learned.
  • Agents improve not because the model changed, but because the system around them gets smarter with every interaction.
  • Social visibility of AI work accelerates organizational learning — work done in isolated tools is invisible to the system.

Why It Matters

  • Early movers compound learning advantages faster than any previous technology wave. The position gap and the capability gap grow simultaneously.
  • Memory layer design is as important as the semantic layer: what gets written, curated, who can read it, how stale knowledge is retired.
  • The binding constraint shifts to human judgment: posing good problems, setting constraints, judging which proposals are worth keeping.

Changeis Context

  • Every MAPS workflow deployment and every ATEPS-II agent interaction should be instrumented with feedback loops from Day 1.
  • Build a shared institutional memory layer across Changeis's AI projects: what worked in FAA workflows should inform what is tried in next federal contract.
  • Make AI work visible internally: shared channels, weekly learning readouts, not just project delivery reports. This is how a 70-person firm compounds at speed.
7
Governance
Embed accountability, align funding, and move at speed.

Bain Framing

  • Run two parallel governance motions: one to run the business (existing), one to change the business (new). Existing governance is almost perfectly wrong for AI transformation.
  • Token costs are the new unit economics of AI operations. Must be tracked by workflow, domain, and business outcome — not absorbed into IT project budgets.
  • Funding model must shift from project-by-project ROI to portfolio management aligned to strategic posture.
  • The CEO must own AI risk. Not monitor it. Own it. A named individual at the top who understands the risk profile, has set the risk appetite, and is accountable for outcomes.

Why It Matters

  • As agents make consequential decisions autonomously, accountability cannot be delegated to a compliance team or abstracted into a black box.
  • Retrofitting accountability after an agent causes harm is both ethically and strategically more costly than building it in from the start.
  • Token costs can become material and invisible without deliberate tracking — especially as agent estates scale.

Changeis Context

  • Federal contracting context elevates governance urgency: government clients require clear human accountability for AI-driven decisions in regulated environments.
  • Establish a bi-weekly AI transformation governance cadence separate from Changeis's operating governance. Varun chairs it personally.
  • Track token costs per contract/program from the first agent deployment. This is a client billing and cost-management necessity, not optional.
  • Varun formally owns Changeis's AI risk posture. Document the risk appetite and communicate it to the team and to key federal client contacts.
