1. Federal AI Policy & Acquisition
Export Controls · Anthropic · Fable 5 Day 13
Fable 5 Ban Day 13: Anthropic Accuses Alibaba of 28.8M-Exchange Distillation Attack; China's 360 Security Announces Mythos-Matching Tools; Export Ban Holds

Day thirteen of the Fable 5 / Mythos 5 export ban was dominated by two escalatory Chinese responses to the ongoing US controls. First, Anthropic formally accused Alibaba of executing the largest-known distillation attack on any US AI lab: a letter sent by Anthropic to Senators Tim Scott (R-SC) and Elizabeth Warren (D-MA), and White House officials, alleged that operators linked to Alibaba's Qwen AI lab used approximately 25,000 fraudulent accounts to conduct 28.8 million interactions with Claude between April 22 and June 5, 2026, specifically targeting Claude's software engineering and agentic reasoning capabilities. Alibaba has not commented. The campaign was described as an attempt to extract US AI capabilities for Chinese competitors at a fraction of the training cost — what Anthropic calls "adversarial distillation." Alibaba's US-listed ADR fell nearly 3% on the news. (Bloomberg, Jun 24; Business Times, Jun 25; Reuters, Jun 24)

Second, Chinese cybersecurity firm 360 Security Technology publicly claimed to have developed tools matching Anthropic's Mythos capabilities, framing the US model as "a strategic cyber capability that China could not afford to lack" and positioning its domestic tools as a countermeasure. This is the most aggressive public Chinese government-linked response to the export ban to date, and it undercuts the US government's stated rationale that restricting Fable 5 and Mythos 5 prevents adversaries from acquiring the capability. The Legion LegalTech lawsuit continues in DC federal court; no preliminary injunction ruling has been issued. The export ban remains in full force for all non-US users. (Reuters, Jun 25)

Anthropic's letter asked Congress for three specific policy responses: (1) antitrust clarity to allow US AI labs to share distillation intelligence with each other without antitrust exposure; (2) new federal penalties for foreign firms using distillation to extract US AI capabilities; (3) continued and strengthened chip export controls. Note that Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google have already begun sharing distillation intelligence bilaterally, per the Business Times. The Handy AI timeline confirmed that Fable 5 free credits ended June 23; active paid subscribers continue at $10/M input tokens and $50/M output tokens. (Handy AI, Jun 24; Devdiscourse, Jun 25)

What This Means For Changeis

The Anthropic–Alibaba distillation disclosure and China's Mythos-matching claim have five direct BD implications. (1) Anthropic's request for antitrust clarity on intelligence sharing is the precursor to a new federal AI information-sharing program — Changeis should watch for a DOJ or FTC guidance document as the first procurement signal; (2) the 360 Security Mythos-matching claim, if credible, erodes the government's primary argument for the Fable 5 ban — this makes a partial or full restoration more likely on a shorter timeline, supporting a pre-July 17 resolution scenario; (3) the adversarial distillation framing gives federal AI proposals a new risk category: Changeis proposals deploying API-accessible models should include API access controls, rate limiting, and anomaly detection language to address distillation risk in federal environments; (4) Anthropic's call for chip export controls and AI capability extraction penalties is the clearest signal yet that the current export control regime will expand — Changeis BD pipeline for AI procurement should include a regulatory change monitor for new model export control rules in Q3 2026; (5) the Samsung reversal (lifting its three-year ChatGPT ban) is a direct parallel to federal agency AI adoption — expect FISMA agencies that have been slow to adopt AI tools to accelerate internal authorization pathways in H2 2026.

Quantum · Federal · Trump EO
Trump Signs Two Quantum Computing Executive Orders: 2028 Quantum Computer Target, Post-Quantum Cryptography Migration Mandate

President Trump signed two executive orders on quantum computing on June 23, per Mayer Brown and Insurance Journal reporting. The first directs the DOE and DARPA to build a large-scale quantum computer capable of powering "a new era of scientific discovery" by 2028, building on the DOE's "Quantum Genesis" initiative. The second mandates accelerated post-quantum cryptography (PQC) migration across all federal agencies to protect government systems from quantum decryption threats — directly citing China's quantum computing advances as the driver. Trump set a 2028 target, which analysts call aggressive but achievable for targeted scientific applications. (Mayer Brown, Jun 24; Insurance Journal, Jun 24)

The PQC migration mandate creates an immediate federal contracting opportunity: agencies must now comply with NIST post-quantum standards (ML-KEM, ML-DSA, SLH-DSA) on an accelerated timeline. The DOE Quantum Genesis initiative was announced alongside the EO signing. Quantum stocks surged: Infleqtion (INFQ) climbed 12% on the news. The backdrop is China's LineShine supercomputer topping the TOP500 — though experts note LineShine's CPU-only architecture is not optimized for AI workloads, ranking 4th on AI benchmarks. (Yahoo Finance, Jun 24; WSJ, Jun 24)

What This Means For Changeis

The quantum EOs are high-priority BD signals for two Changeis practice areas. (1) The PQC migration mandate creates an immediate, agency-wide compliance requirement — every federal agency's CIO/CISO is now accountable for a PQC migration plan; Changeis should draft a PQC readiness assessment capability statement this week, positioning it as an add-on to existing FISMA compliance work; (2) the 2028 quantum computer target positions DOE and DARPA as primary quantum program offices — Changeis should identify DARPA quantum program officers and DOE national lab contacts for Q3 2026 outreach; (3) the China-LineShine backdrop will make PQC urgency a recurring theme in federal IT budget discussions — include quantum threat language in Changeis cybersecurity proposals for the rest of FY2026.

2. Model Releases & Lab News
OpenAI · Jalapeño · Broadcom · Chip
OpenAI Unveils "Jalapeño" — First Custom AI Inference Chip Built With Broadcom; Targets LLM Cost and Efficiency at Scale

OpenAI revealed its first custom-built AI inference chip, named "Jalapeño," designed in partnership with Broadcom, on June 24. The chip is optimized for LLM inference at scale — specifically the serving workloads that dominate OpenAI's compute cost — rather than training. OpenAI confirmed initial availability will be through its own API and infrastructure, not sold externally. The chip represents OpenAI's first step toward a full-stack AI hardware strategy, reducing dependence on Nvidia for inference operations (though Nvidia remains essential for training). Broadcom stock rose approximately 3% on the news. (OpenAI, Jun 24; Reuters, Jun 24; CNBC, Jun 24; TechCrunch, Jun 24)

The Jalapeño chip follows the custom ASIC path taken by Google (TPUs) and Amazon (Trainium/Inferentia), both of which built inference chips after years of GPU dependence. OpenAI's inference costs are the dominant driver of COGS at current scale — Jalapeño is the clearest signal yet that OpenAI's infrastructure strategy is moving from cloud dependence toward vertical integration. The WSJ reported the chip is built to "improve efficiency and lower costs" — implying the inference cost per API call is a target. For federal customers, a cost-reduced OpenAI API would widen the competitive gap between GPT models and alternatives. (WSJ, Jun 24; Kursol, Jun 25)

What This Means For Changeis

The Jalapeño chip has three near-term federal BD implications. (1) A more cost-efficient OpenAI inference stack improves the TCO case for GPT-5.5/5.6 in federal proposals — Changeis proposals with AI inference cost as a line item should include a note that OpenAI's custom chip deployment is expected to reduce per-token inference costs, improving proposal cost competitiveness; (2) the chip confirms OpenAI's transition from API reseller to infrastructure owner — for federal ATO processes, this changes the supply chain disclosure; (3) Broadcom's stock gain signals market conviction that custom inference ASICs are the future — Changeis should track Broadcom's federal defense/IC relationships, as future versions of Jalapeño may be targeted at NIST-certified federal compute environments.

Qualcomm · Modular · AI Software
Qualcomm Acquires AI Software Firm Modular for $3.9B; Brings MAX Language and MLIR Stack Into Qualcomm AI Portfolio

Qualcomm announced a definitive agreement to acquire Modular Inc. for $3.9 billion in stock (approximately 19.2M shares), per WSJ, Bloomberg, and Qualcomm's investor relations release. Modular is the creator of the MAX programming language, built on MLIR (Multi-Level Intermediate Representation), which provides a unified software stack for AI inference across CPUs, GPUs, and specialized accelerators. Modular's Mojo language had significant developer traction in 2024–2025 as a Python-compatible AI systems language. The acquisition is Qualcomm's largest AI software move since its Neural Processing SDK. (Qualcomm IR, Jun 24; WSJ, Jun 24; Reuters, Jun 24)

The deal positions Qualcomm's on-device AI hardware (Snapdragon X Elite, Snapdragon 8 Gen 4) with a first-party software stack for local inference — directly competing with Apple Silicon + Core ML and Nvidia CUDA for the edge AI workload market. Enterprise customers running AI on-device for privacy or latency reasons now have a Qualcomm-native software path. For federal edge computing deployments (DHS CBP, DoD tactical edge), a Qualcomm-Modular stack could support air-gapped AI inference environments that cannot use cloud APIs. (Bloomberg, Jun 24)

Samsung · OpenAI · Enterprise Access
Samsung Grants Employees ChatGPT Enterprise and Codex Access After Three-Year AI Tool Ban; Industry Signal for Federal Agency AI Adoption

Samsung announced it is granting employees access to ChatGPT Enterprise and OpenAI Codex, three years after restricting AI tools following an internal data-security incident in 2023 when sensitive semiconductor code was leaked via ChatGPT. The announcement, per AI News, signals the broader enterprise tipping point from AI caution to structured deployment. (AI News, Jun 24)

3. Funding, Corporate & Market
China · LineShine · Supercomputer · TOP500
China's LineShine Tops TOP500 at 2.198 Exaflops — First Chinese #1 Since 2017; CPU-Only Architecture Ranks 4th on AI Benchmarks

China's LineShine supercomputer at the National Supercomputing Center in Shenzhen claimed the #1 position on the June 2026 TOP500 list with 2.198 exaflops — a 22% lead over the US El Capitan at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (1.809 exaflops). It is the first time a Chinese machine has topped the list since Sunway TaihuLight in 2017. LineShine uses domestically designed LX2 ARM-architecture CPUs (304 cores each, ARMv9, 1.55GHz), no Nvidia GPUs, 13.79 million total cores, 90 cabinets, and 42.2 megawatts of power. The achievement reflects China's domestic chip self-sufficiency ambitions. (WSJ, Jun 24; Al Jazeera, Jun 24; Economic Times India, Jun 24)

Critically, on the HPL-MxP benchmark (which simulates AI-relevant mixed-precision workloads), LineShine ranked 4th — behind El Capitan, Frontier (Oak Ridge), and Aurora (Argonne). CPU-to-mixed-precision uplift for LineShine was only 3.6x versus 15–20x for GPU-accelerated systems. Technology and policy experts quoted by Reuters and the Economic Times said the TOP500 result is a measure of Chinese computing ambition and chip self-sufficiency, not AI leadership. The US maintains the lead in AI-optimized compute at scale (Nvidia H100/H200/GB300 clusters). (Economic Times India, Jun 24; AP, Jun 24)

What This Means For Changeis

The LineShine story has two federal BD angles. (1) The media framing ("China beats US on supercomputers") will generate Congressional and agency anxiety about US compute leadership — Changeis should anticipate new federal HPC and AI compute modernization RFIs in Q3 FY2026, particularly at DOE national labs and DoD; (2) the CPU-only, domestically designed architecture of LineShine is actually a technology limitation story — Nvidia's GPU monopoly for AI training/inference remains intact, and the US chip export control regime is proven effective at preventing Chinese labs from building GPU-scale AI systems. This is a strong talking point for Changeis proposals emphasizing US AI capability advantages in competitive proposals against Chinese-origin AI tools.

(Reuters, Jun 25)

California · AI Pricing · Antitrust
California Drivers Sue Gas Stations Over AI-Coordinated Fuel Price Inflation; First AI Antitrust Class Action Under New California Algorithm Law

California drivers filed a class action lawsuit against major gas station operators alleging they used AI pricing tools to coordinate higher fuel prices — the first proposed class action testing new California laws aimed at algorithmic price-fixing, per Reuters and The Guardian. The lawsuit argues that AI-driven dynamic pricing tools enabled price coordination that would otherwise require explicit collusion, exploiting a legal grey area in existing antitrust law. The suit is a harbinger for federal AI antitrust action: if California courts accept the theory, it will trigger Congressional and FTC scrutiny of AI pricing tools across sectors. (AP / Reuters, Jun 25)
