2 FedCiv Roundup — Past 24 Hours (July 21–22, 2026)

Curated bullets from federal courts, DHS, GSA, Treasury, and legal/policy trackers. Sources linked inline.

  • OMB grant-termination authority dealt a setback in court: A federal judge denied the administration's motion to dismiss a lawsuit brought by 21 state attorneys general and three governors, ruling that OMB's grant-termination clause cannot be used to cancel existing awards simply by citing changed policy priorities — a decision that bears directly on OMB's parallel Uniform Grants Regulation rewrite, which remains on track for an October 1, 2026 effective date after drawing more than 341,000 public comments during its now-closed comment period (Ground News).
  • DHS rescinds 2022 public charge rule: DHS issued a final rule on July 20, 2026 rescinding the 2022 public charge regulation and returning to a case-by-case, totality-of-the-circumstances discretionary approach for immigration officers; the rule takes effect September 18, 2026 and will apply to new admissions and adjustment-of-status filings from that date (The National Law Review).
  • DHS immigration enforcement data release: After a three-month delay, DHS published detention data on July 20 showing ICE arrests at record levels under Secretary Markwayne Mullin, with average daily detention bookings up 32% since April; the release follows a difficult week for the agency after fatal ICE encounters this month renewed congressional and public scrutiny of enforcement tactics (USA Today).
  • GSA previews governmentwide role in Southwest DC redevelopment: Administrator Ed Forst told a Bisnow summit audience that GSA sees itself as a potential "first mover" shaping the roughly 250-acre Southwest DC federal corridor between the National Mall and the waterfront, following recent GSA sales of the Seventh and D Streets SW office building and the Liberty Loan building; Forst pointed to the historic Yards transfer as a possible model for the district (Bisnow).
  • GSA AI data-protection clause update: GSA revised its draft AI contract clause following a July 14 public listening session, narrowing scope to government data processed by large language models rather than AI systems broadly, adding exceptions for common commercial products, and introducing distinct flow-down obligations for LLM developers, system operators, integrators, and providers; GSA is still accepting written comments before finalizing the clause (Federal News Network).
  • Treasury intercepts nearly $100M in payments to deceased individuals: Treasury reported it stopped almost $100 million in taxpayer funds from being disbursed to deceased individuals, part of a broader payment-integrity push; separately, Treasury flagged certain Wall Street tax strategies as potentially abusive and is weighing new guidance (New York Post, Reuters).
  • DHS/DOI probationary-firing case advances to appeals court: Attorneys for former DHS and Interior probationary employees dismissed last year filed their opening brief at the federal court of appeals, challenging a Merit Systems Protection Board ruling that it lacked authority to review the legality of the terminations; the government has until September 25 to respond (Federal News Network).
  • DHS/DOJ drone rulemaking continues to move: Following the July 1–2 DHS/DOJ interim final rule implementing SAFER SKIES Act training, certification, and airspace-approval provisions, the FCC issued a July 17 public notice proposing to bar continued importation and marketing of certain previously authorized foreign-produced drone equipment — a coordinated national-security push relevant to DHS counter-UAS and USDOT airspace-integration contracting (Holland & Knight).
  • VA rural-veteran transportation grant and reauthorization push: VA awarded $710,550 to the North Dakota Department of Veterans Affairs on July 20 under the Highly Rural Transportation Grants program to fund free transportation for rural veterans; legislation to permanently reauthorize and expand the program, including new eligibility for Tribal and Veterans Service Organizations, is advancing through the Senate Veterans' Affairs Committee (Govly).
  • GSA Office of Small Business subcontracting webinar (Aug. 7): GSA's Office of Small Business will host a free virtual webinar on August 7, 2026 covering subcontracting plan requirements and strategies for small businesses seeking engagement with prime contractors — a near-term outreach and teaming touchpoint for FedCiv small-business partners (Govly).
  • GAO key-personnel disclosure doctrine reaffirmed: A new legal analysis of GAO bid-protest precedent confirms that while the FAR imposes no blanket duty to disclose a proposed key person's unavailability after proposal submission, GAO treats silence in the face of known unavailability as a potential material misrepresentation that can support a sustained protest — a live risk factor for proposal teams managing staffing changes during lengthy civilian evaluations (The National Law Review).
  • TSA privatized screening expands to Tampa: TSA security screening at Tampa International Airport will move to a private contractor under an expanded federal Screening Partnership Program, continuing a trend of civilian aviation-security outsourcing that follows the $862M Leidos TSA logistics award reported July 17–20 (CNN).
