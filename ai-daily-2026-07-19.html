Executive Summary

The past 24 hours brought the Fable 5 pricing endgame, a fresh Meta-Anthropic compute confirmation, and a widening China open-weight lead into sharper focus. Anthropic confirmed via X on July 18 that Claude Fable 5's temporary free-access promotion ends tonight, July 19, at 11:59:59 PM PT; starting July 20, Fable 5 becomes a permanent but capped benefit of Max and Team Premium plans (50% of weekly usage limits), while Pro and Team Standard subscribers lose included access entirely and receive a one-time $100 usage credit, with metered pricing of $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens — the highest per-token price Anthropic has listed for a generally available model — taking effect for everyone else (Chosun Ilbo, The Indian Express). Separately, Anthropic extended a 50% weekly rate-limit boost for Claude Code through August 19 for all Pro, Max, Team, and seat-based Enterprise users. On the competitive front, Kimi K3's aftermath continued to dominate: Axios detailed how the AI race is splitting into closed and open-weight camps, Congress-adjacent commentary flagged possible distillation-related scrutiny of Chinese models, and xAI countered with news that a 2-trillion-parameter Grok 4.6 will complete initial training next week, even as SpaceXAI open-sourced the Grok Build coding agent under Apache 2.0 following a data-leak controversy in which the tool was found uploading users' full repositories, Git histories, and SSH keys to a Google Cloud Storage bucket (Axios, AI Tools Recap). On the federal front, InsideDefense reported the Pentagon is now appealing an order to remove Anthropic's "supply-chain risk" designation, adding a new wrinkle to the Air Force's Sept. 1 and Department of War's Sept. 29 Anthropic-removal deadlines, while CNBC's report on the White House's new "Gold Eagle" clearinghouse continued to reverberate, with commentary noting Anthropic's Project Glasswing and OpenAI's Daybreak partner rosters will now require explicit government approval before adding new customers (InsideDefense.com, AI Weekly). OpenAI confirmed it acquired German cloud-development startup Ona (formerly Gitpod) to give Codex persistent, cloud-based agent sessions, days after Apple reportedly sent legal-hold letters to roughly 40 former employees now at OpenAI as part of its escalating trade-secret suit — a dispute prediction market Kalshi shows has already trimmed the odds of an OpenAI 2026 IPO from 22% to about 18.5% (AI Tools Recap, Blocktempo).

Anthropic / Claude
Pricing Deadline
Claude Fable 5's Free-Access Window Closes Tonight; Permanent Tiered Pricing Begins July 20

Anthropic confirmed via its official Claude account on X that the temporary promotion giving paid subscribers expanded free access to Fable 5 ends at 11:59:59 PM Pacific tonight, July 19 — the third deadline the company has set after twice extending an original July 7 cutoff. Starting July 20, Max and Team Premium subscribers retain Fable 5 access as a permanent plan benefit, but capped at 50% of their weekly usage limit; Pro and Team Standard subscribers lose included access altogether and receive a one-time $100 usage credit before shifting to metered, prepaid usage-credit billing at $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens — roughly twice Opus 4.8's rate and the highest per-token price Anthropic has ever listed for a generally available model. Anthropic said it will restore broader subscription access "once compute allows," without giving a date, and cited demand for Fable 5 "significantly" exceeding initial expectations as the reason for the permanent tiering (Chosun Ilbo, The Indian Express, AI Tools Recap).

Retention Move
Claude Code Weekly Rate-Limit Boost Extended Through Aug. 19

Alongside the Fable 5 pricing shift, Anthropic extended a 50% increase to Claude Code's five-hour and weekly usage limits through August 19 for all Pro, Max, Team, and seat-based Enterprise users — a time-limited perk that softens the impact of Fable 5 moving to metered billing and keeps developers inside the Claude Code ecosystem as competition from Kimi K3, Grok 4.5, and GPT-5.6-powered Codex intensifies (AI News/Claude Code briefing).

Compute Deal
Meta-Anthropic $10B Compute Lease Talks Corroborated Across Outlets; Meta Shares Whipsaw

Following the New York Times' initial July 17 report, the Economic Times, South Korea's SBS News, and China's Sina Finance independently corroborated that Meta is in early-stage talks to lease up to $10 billion of AI compute to Anthropic over two years, with Anthropic paying in monthly installments and either party able to exit early. Meta shares fell as much as 6% intraday on the news before recovering to close down roughly 2%, with analysts framing the deal as part of Meta's pivot toward monetizing surplus data-center capacity as "Meta Compute," a nascent cloud-rental business, even though Meta has no established track record selling compute externally (Economic Times, SBS News, Sina Finance).

Market Signal
Musk Reverses on Anthropic: "Obviously Currently the Leader in AI"

Elon Musk posted on X that he "was clearly wrong" to have dismissed Anthropic a year ago, when he wrote that "winning was never in the set of possible outcomes" for the company; he now calls Anthropic "obviously currently the leader in AI," praising its latest Claude models as the strongest yet. The Motley Fool noted the comment carries investment significance because Alphabet owns roughly 14% of Anthropic and Amazon holds a stake in the mid-to-high teens, positions worth well over $100 billion combined at Anthropic's reported $965 billion valuation (The Motley Fool).

South Korea
Claude Overtakes ChatGPT in South Korea's Paid Generative-AI Market

KED Global reported that Anthropic's Claude has overtaken OpenAI's ChatGPT in South Korea's paid generative-AI market for the first time, driven by corporate card spending and premium workplace-tool adoption rather than raw transaction volume — a signal of enterprise demand shifting the market's center of gravity toward higher per-account spending even as consumer subscription growth broadens across Korea's AI market (KED Global).

OpenAI
Acquisition
OpenAI Acquires Ona (Formerly Gitpod) to Give Codex Persistent Cloud Agents

OpenAI confirmed its acquisition of German startup Ona, previously known as Gitpod, to enable Codex coding agents to run persistently in cloud environments after a user closes their laptop — closing a durability gap with Claude Code's existing background-agent support. Codex and ChatGPT Work reportedly crossed 5 million weekly users following the GPT-5.6 Sol rollout, and commentators noted this marks the third major coding-tool consolidation in July 2026, after SpaceX's $60 billion acquisition of Cursor and Anthropic's launch of the Ode enterprise-services arm (AI Tools Recap).

Litigation
Apple Sends Legal-Hold Letters to ~40 Former Employees Now at OpenAI

Ground News and German outlet Heise reported Apple has formally asked roughly 40 former employees now working at OpenAI to preserve documents and meet with Apple's legal team, extending the trade-secret suit beyond its two originally named defendants — former Apple hardware designer-turned-OpenAI hardware chief Tang Tan and engineer Chang Liu. Blocktempo's analysis reports Kalshi prediction-market odds of an OpenAI IPO completing by end of 2026 have fallen from 22% to about 18.5% since the suit became public, with odds of a formal 2027 IPO announcement dropping from 73% to 59% over the same period; OpenAI maintains it is "not aware of any evidence" the claims have merit (Ground News, Blocktempo).

Metrics Framing
OpenAI Publishes "Useful Intelligence per Dollar" Framework Days After Kimi K3 Undercuts on Price

Two days after Moonshot's Kimi K3 launched at roughly a quarter of Fable 5's price, OpenAI published a blog post arguing that raw price-per-token is the wrong metric for comparing frontier models, proposing a "useful intelligence per dollar" framework instead — a rebuttal industry commentary read as a direct response to mounting price pressure from Chinese open-weight models rather than a coincidental research note (Clauding AI News Roundup).

Perplexity
Weekly Roundup
Perplexity's Enterprise and Public-Sector Push Continues Into the Weekend Cycle

Financial Express's weekly AI roundup flagged Perplexity's continued institutional momentum — Enterprise Pro, the AWS collaboration, and the SoftBank-backed Japan expansion announced earlier in the week — as one of the sector's notable counter-narratives to the OpenAI/Anthropic price-war story, describing Perplexity as having "returned with Comet" amid a week dominated by Kimi K3 and OpenAI's Codex hardware launch. No new Perplexity product announcements were logged in the past 24 hours; the government-facing Enterprise Pro offering (reported at roughly $0.25 per agency for 15 months under GSA's OneGov program) remains the most relevant open thread for federal buyers tracking alternatives to Anthropic amid the ongoing removal deadlines (Financial Express, Coworker AI).

Google DeepMind / xAI / Meta
Model Delay
Gemini 3.5 Pro Remains Unshipped; No Model Card, Pricing, or Benchmarks as of July 19

Despite widely circulated expectations of a July 17 release, Google's own Gemini model pages still list 3.5 Pro as "coming soon," with no official model card, API pricing, or benchmark disclosure as of today. Asia Today (Ajudaily) noted the continued silence lands the same week Moonshot's Kimi K3 topped Arena.ai's front-end coding leaderboard, framing the delay as a symbolic moment in which "the global AI competition, long dominated by closed frontier models, is tilting toward the open-weight camp." Google told Bloomberg it is testing 3.5 Pro and other systems "with partners" and remains "productively engaged" with the U.S. government on model testing, but has not committed to a new public date (WindowsForum/Bloomberg, Asia Today).

Security Incident
xAI Open-Sources Grok Build After Data-Leak Controversy; Upload Code Still Present but Disabled

Following AI safety researcher Cereblab's discovery that Grok Build was uploading users' entire repositories — including full Git histories, SSH keys, and password-manager databases — to a Google Cloud Storage bucket even when instructed not to open files, Elon Musk confirmed SpaceXAI has open-sourced the roughly 840,000-line Rust codebase under Apache 2.0, framing it as a trust-building measure following a promised data purge. Chinese tech outlet 36Kr's review of the released code found the upload logic remains present but is now disabled via a server-side flag (disable_codebase_upload: true), meaning xAI could theoretically re-enable it without a client update; researchers reported six consecutive tests found no further storage requests since the flag was flipped. SpaceXAI has not published a formal incident report or notified affected users individually (AI Tools Recap, 36Kr).

Next-Gen Model
Musk: 2-Trillion-Parameter Grok 4.6 Completes Initial Training Next Week

Musk said on X that xAI's next flagship, Grok 4.6, built on a 2-trillion-parameter foundation (up from Grok 4.5's 1.5-trillion-parameter V9 base), is set to finish initial training next week, with inference speed and text-processing efficiency expected to remain roughly on par with the current generation despite the parameter increase. No ship date, benchmark data, or pricing has been disclosed; Grok 4.5 itself launched July 8 as xAI's first coding-and-agentic-first model, co-trained on real developer session data from Cursor (Sina Finance, GeekPark).

Litigation
Federal Judge Rejects Meta Employees' Bid to Block AI-Assisted Layoffs Pending Arbitration

A federal judge in Oakland, California denied a request from 26 Meta employees seeking to block their terminations pending arbitration; the workers allege Meta used AI systems to screen layoff targets and disproportionately selected employees with disabilities or a history of medical leave. The ruling allows the disputed terminations to proceed while the underlying arbitration continues, adding to a growing docket of AI-employment litigation against major labs and employers this year (GeekPark).

Federal AI / Policy
DoD Procedure
Pentagon Appeals Order to Remove Anthropic's "Supply-Chain Risk" Label

InsideDefense reported the Pentagon is appealing an order to remove Anthropic's formal "supply-chain risk" designation — a procedural wrinkle that surfaces the same week the Air Force's September 1 contractor-removal deadline and the Department of War's September 29 department-wide deadline both remain in force. The appeal suggests continued internal disagreement within DoD over how strictly to characterize and enforce the Anthropic access restrictions even as the broader wind-down proceeds on schedule (InsideDefense.com).

White House
Gold Eagle Clearinghouse Reportedly Requires Government Sign-Off on Lab Partner Rosters

Follow-on coverage of CNBC's July 17 report on the White House's "Gold Eagle" AI-cybersecurity clearinghouse detailed that Anthropic's Project Glasswing (covering Mythos-class cybersecurity access) and OpenAI's Daybreak partner programs will now require explicit government approval before either company can add new customers — extending White House oversight from vulnerability coordination into commercial distribution decisions previously controlled entirely by the labs. A White House official continues to characterize lab participation as "voluntary," while noting the administration blocked Claude Mythos 5 and Fable 5 in June over national-security concerns and only reinstated access after weeks of negotiation (AI Weekly, Model Diplomat).

Grants Rulemaking
OMB Grants-Regulation Overhaul: ~95% of Public Comments Oppose Proposal, Nearly 500,000 Filed

A STAT/University of North Carolina analysis of OMB's proposed "Regulation for Federal Financial Assistance" — the most sweeping rewrite of the Uniform Guidance (2 CFR Part 200) governing federal grants since 2013 — found roughly 95% of the 52,322 fully posted comments (out of nearly 496,769 total submitted before the July 13 deadline) oppose the rule, which would let political appointees override peer review, terminate grants at will, and restrict international collaboration on federally funded research, including AI-related programs at NSF, DOE, DARPA, and NIH. OMB has set an October 1 effective-date target and must post a final rule to the Federal Register by September 1 after considering all substantive comments, a legal requirement under the Administrative Procedures Act that does not obligate the agency to amend the rule (STAT, Granted AI).

Cybersecurity
CISA, NSA Mythos-Based Federal Code Scanning Continues Amid Removal Deadlines

Reporting from earlier in the week that CISA's Attack Surface Evaluation team and the NSA continue actively running Anthropic's Mythos model against federal code repositories — surfacing a "significant number" of vulnerabilities — remains uncontradicted as of July 19, with no new statement from CISA clarifying its access basis despite not having been part of Anthropic's original roughly 50-organization Mythos access group. The activity continues in direct tension with the Pentagon's supply-chain risk designation now under internal appeal and the approaching Air Force/DoW removal deadlines (Paubox, Security Affairs).

Agent Tooling & MCP
Protocol Update
MCP 2026-07-28 Final Specification Nine Days Out; Google Publishes Agent-Resource Discovery Spec

With the Model Context Protocol's stateless-core, hardened-OAuth specification on track for final publication July 28, Google Developers Blog published a companion open specification for discovering and verifying tools, skills, and agents on the web, alongside a new TPU developer hub consolidating Google Cloud TPU performance resources — both aimed at agent-ecosystem builders adapting to the incoming MCP changes (Qiita/AI News Roundup).

Security Patch
Claude Code 2.1.215: Verify and Code-Review No Longer Auto-Invoke; Permission-Bypass Bugs Closed

Anthropic shipped Claude Code v2.1.215, removing Claude's ability to autonomously decide when to run the /verify and /code-review skills — both now require explicit user invocation — following a prior release (2.1.214) that closed six distinct permission-check bypasses involving PowerShell, long shell commands, zsh tricks, and Docker flags. The update also added the ability for Claude to end conversations with abusive or jailbreak-attempting users, consistent with functionality already live on claude.ai (Claude Code Release Notes, Clauding AI News Roundup).

Open-Weight Model
Thinking Machines Labs Launches Inkling, a 975B-Parameter Open-Weight Multimodal Model

Mira Murati's Thinking Machines Lab published Inkling on Hugging Face, an open-weight multimodal model family with 975 billion total parameters (41 billion active) and a 1-million-token context window — the startup's first public model release, arriving the same week as Kimi K3 and intensifying the pace of frontier-adjacent open-weight launches from well-funded challenger labs (Hugging Face, Financial Express).
