2 FedCiv Roundup — Past 24 Hours (July 23–24, 2026)

Curated bullets from federal courts, DHS/FEMA, GSA, Treasury, TIGTA, and legal/policy trackers. Sources linked inline.

  • 25 states and DC sue DHS/FEMA over disaster-grant election and immigration conditions: A coalition of 25 states and the District of Columbia filed suit July 23 in federal court in Rhode Island, challenging DHS/FEMA conditions on the Homeland Security Grant Program and Emergency Management Performance Grant Program that would require states to shift to hand-marked paper ballots, conduct manual post-election audits, verify voter citizenship, and cooperate with federal immigration enforcement or risk losing at least 20% of funding (roughly $148 million); the complaint argues the conditions violate the Administrative Procedure Act and the Constitution's spending clause — a new front in the same grant-termination authority fight underlying OMB's Uniform Grants Regulation rewrite (U.S. News/AP, Democracy Docket).
  • OMB Uniform Grants Regulation rewrite tracks toward October 1 effective date: New analysis details the scope of OMB's 2 CFR overhaul — the largest rewrite of federal grant rules since 2013 — which adds a discretionary "no longer effectuates program goals, agency priorities, or the national interest" termination ground modeled on FAR termination-for-convenience, a new 90-day suspension power, discretionary post-termination cost reimbursement, loss of formal appeal rights for discretionary terminations, and new E-Verify, Do Not Pay, and SAM.gov subaward-reporting mandates; the underlying proposal drew nearly 500,000 public comments before its comment period closed July 13 (Granted AI, The Conversation).
  • DHS public charge rule rescission moves toward September 18 effective date: Legal and immigration trackers confirm the DHS final rule rescinding the 2022 public charge regulation, published in the Federal Register, takes effect September 18, 2026 and will not apply to green-card applicants who filed before that date or to current permanent residents; officers will revert to a discretionary, totality-of-the-circumstances test (VisaVerge, Tampa Bay Times).
  • GSA reiterates governmentwide "first mover" role in Southwest DC redevelopment: GSA Administrator Ed Forst, speaking at Bisnow's Federal Office and Southwest DC Summit, said "the federal government has a real place to play here to be the first mover in terms of what can be brought to life" across the roughly 250-acre corridor between the National Mall and the Southwest waterfront, reinforcing GSA's active planning posture following recent property sales in the district (Three | E60 News).
  • TIGTA finds physical- and cyber-security gaps at IRS Zero Paper Initiative contractor sites: A new Treasury Inspector General for Tax Administration memorandum found 14 contractor employees accessed taxpayer-document areas without IRS authorization on 1,375 occasions in the past year at two Zero Paper Initiative sites, an unsecured perimeter fence and loading dock at one site with no guards controlling access, and 128 of 269 (48%) identified information-system vulnerabilities left unresolved on a timely basis, including unsupported software versions and unauthorized/unvalidated scanning tools; TIGTA disputed contractors' claim that their contract did not require guards, and IRS says it has taken or will take corrective action, with TIGTA planning further site visits (Accounting Today).
  • USDOT launches AI initiative under Genesis Mission: The Department of Transportation announced a new AI-driven initiative to modernize how the U.S. builds, operates, and maintains transportation infrastructure, joining a cross-agency effort with the Department of Energy and National Science Foundation under President Trump's Genesis Mission; Secretary Sean Duffy framed the effort around lowering costs and improving safety and efficiency, part of a $5 billion, 15-agency commitment announced by the White House this week (Future Transport-News, Washington Post).
  • NHTSA to develop new vehicle door-egress safety rule (USDOT): The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration said it will begin rulemaking to require a "robust and obvious door egress system" in all new motor vehicles, granting a petitioner's request following deadly incidents in which occupants became trapped inside vehicles with electronic door releases (Bloomberg).
  • Commerce Department: Space Commerce Certification moves forward; USPTO marks America's 250th: Commerce's Office of Space Commerce said it will publish a Federal Register "call for interest" in the coming weeks to begin certifying novel commercial space missions under its Space Commerce Certification proposal; separately, USPTO Director John Squires led a July 23 signing ceremony commemorating America's 250th anniversary with Deputy Secretary of Commerce Paul Dabbar and Secretary Howard Lutnick (Office of Space Commerce, USPTO).
  • GAO bid-protest precedent developments — prejudice standard and CPARS cutoffs: New practitioner analysis flags a GAO decision in Highlight Technologies, Inc., B-424060.2 (May 22, 2026), in which GAO found an agency's evaluation inconsistent with the solicitation yet denied the protest for lack of "convincing evidence" of prejudice, suggesting a possibly tightening prejudice standard; separately, in Veterans Mgmt. Servs., Inc., B-424182.2 (June 22, 2026), GAO sustained a protest after VA excluded CPARS data on contracts more than three years old, finding the agency's hard cutoff inconsistent with solicitation terms (Crowell & Moring, JD Supra).
  • USTR tariff announcement expected as Section 122 tariffs expire: The White House said USTR Jamieson Greer would announce next steps on tariff policy Thursday ahead of the midnight expiration of the administration's 10% global Section 122 tariffs, with replacement country-specific tariffs (Brazil, Canada) already rolling out under other statutory authorities — a trade-policy signal with downstream effects for Commerce and DHS/CBP enforcement posture (Inside U.S. Trade).
