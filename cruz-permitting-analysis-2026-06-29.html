Sen. Cruz — CLEAR Skies Act
Permitting Language Review

Senator Cruz's office has revised the permitting waiver legislation in response to AIC Board recommendations. Executive Summary

5 KEY POINTS
  • 1

    Scope is now explicitly limited to BNATCS. The redline removes the prior open-ended language ("any other action the Administrator determines is directly necessary") and replaces it with a closed enumerated list of six specific activity categories, each tied to the Brand New Air Traffic Control System. This directly addresses AIC's primary concern that the waiver authority was too broad.

  • 2

    Transparency and congressional oversight are added. The redline inserts a new Written Determination requirement (§40133(c)(3)) and a 15-day Report to Congress (§40133(c)(5)) for every waiver or preemption issued. Both are new provisions absent from the base draft — they represent the "transparency" accommodation AIC requested.

  • 3

    Waiver authority is expanded from Federal-only to all governmental tiers. The title change from "Authority to Waive Applicable Federal Requirements" to "Authority to Waive Applicable Legal Requirements" and new §40133(d) Federal Preemption language extend preemptive power to State, local, Tribal, and other governmental entities. This is a significant expansion of FAA authority.

  • 4

    Termination clause is strengthened but made more flexible. The fixed sunset of October 1, 2029 is replaced by a rolling expiration tied to the last appropriated funds, plus a carve-out for published waivers and committed funding already in the Federal Register. This extends the effective lifespan of the waiver authority beyond 2029 if appropriations continue.

  • 5

    Gene Hayman recommends AIC support. Based on AIC's stated priorities (BNATCS-only scope + transparency), the revised language appears to satisfy the Board's major objections. CEO Hayman has indicated support and requested a Yes/No vote promptly. The analysis below identifies residual risks but supports a conditional Yes vote.

Impact Assessment

4 DIMENSIONS

Regulatory

  • FAA waiver authority now extends to State, local, Tribal environmental reviews — NEPA, Clean Air Act, Clean Water Act, ESA, MBTA, and NHPA all waivable
  • Emergency waiver bypasses Federal Register pre-publication — compressed timelines for environmental review windows
  • Written determination requirement creates administrative record; reduces arbitrary waiver risk but adds administrative burden
  • Airspace redesign and flight procedures now subject to waiver only "if directly related to BNATCS" — limiting principle added
  • D.C. Circuit exclusive jurisdiction limits forum shopping; constitutional-only challenge bar is very high for environmental challengers

Business / Operational

  • Closed scope definition gives contractors clearer boundaries for which projects qualify for permitting acceleration
  • Cybersecurity hardening explicitly covered — IT and cybersecurity contractors can rely on waiver for BNATCS-related cyber work
  • Lease, easement, and real-property actions now explicitly covered — eases facility acquisition timelines
  • Rolling sunset means AIC members can plan for the long term without a hard 2029 cliff
  • Local permitting preemption (land use, zoning) removes major source of project delay for tower/facility construction

Legal

  • Extension of litigation window from 60 to 90 days increases exposure period; constitutional-only bar means APA challenges are precluded
  • Severability clause (§h) updated to standard "as applied" language — more defensible if challenged in court
  • Rule of construction updated: "subsection" corrected to "section" — clarifies supersession requires specific citation to §40133
  • Emergency waiver provision raises Due Process concerns if waivers take effect before Federal Register notice
  • Broad "other real-property action" language in §(a)(2)(B) may be challenged as exceeding statutory authority

Government Contracting

  • §(e)(2)(A) explicitly covers "contractors, grantees, airport sponsors, vendors, landlords, and State or local airport authorities" — Changeis and peers have direct waiver protection
  • Cybersecurity hardening and IT systems coverage (§a(2)(C)) opens new contract scope under BNATCS umbrella
  • New Limitation on Unrelated Projects (§f) protects against scope creep but also restricts contractors from expanding work under this authority beyond BNATCS
  • 15-day congressional reporting creates predictability but also public disclosure of each waiver project — competitors can see active FAA priorities
  • Rolling sunset extends bid window for BNATCS-related contracts beyond original 2029 timeline

Risk Flags

6 FLAGS
HIGH

Federalism / Constitutional Litigation Exposure. Extending preemption to State, local, and Tribal environmental laws will likely trigger immediate constitutional challenges from environmental groups and state attorneys general. While the D.C. Circuit exclusive-jurisdiction provision and constitutional-only challenge bar are designed to manage this, prolonged litigation could delay BNATCS projects even if the statute is ultimately upheld. The 90-day litigation window (extended from 60) increases the risk window.

HIGH

Emergency Waiver Without Prior Notice. §(c)(4)(B) allows the FAA Administrator to waive federal and state law with immediate effect, posting to the Federal Register within 10 business days after the fact. This creates a window where a waiver is legally operative but not yet publicly announced — raising Due Process and APA concerns that could be litigated even under the constitutional-only standard if a court finds the process itself unconstitutional.

MEDIUM

Open-Ended "Other Real-Property Action" Language. §(a)(2)(B) adds "or other real-property action" after listing specific property activities. This catch-all may be read broadly by FAA in implementation, expanding the authority beyond what AIC intended to endorse. If the FAA later uses this provision for commercial development adjacent to ATC facilities, AIC's support could be cited as endorsement of that use.

MEDIUM

Rolling Sunset Creates Open-Ended Commitment. By tying the expiration to "1 year after all funds have expired," the waiver authority has no fixed end date. Future Congresses could extend BNATCS appropriations indefinitely, effectively making the waiver permanent. AIC should consider whether it is comfortable with permanent (or indefinitely extended) preemption of environmental and planning law, even limited to BNATCS.

MEDIUM

Competitive Intelligence via Congressional Reports. The new 15-day Report to Congress requirement (§(c)(5)) makes every waiver — including the covered activity and project class — a matter of public congressional record. Competitors and adversaries can monitor FAA waiver filings to identify Changeis/AIC member project activities, timelines, and priorities.

LOW

Corrected CWA Citations May Affect Existing Analysis. The correction from 33 U.S.C. 1341/1343 to 1342/1344 is technically important — it now correctly covers NPDES (§402) and wetlands fill permits (§404) rather than state water quality certifications and thermal discharges. If AIC's prior review was based on the incorrect citations, the actual scope of the CWA waiver is slightly different than previously understood, though the practical effect on BNATCS projects is likely minimal.
