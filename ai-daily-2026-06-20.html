1. Federal AI Policy & Acquisition
GSA · GSAR · NEW RULE
GSA Publishes Revised GSAR LLM Clause — Comment Period Opens

On June 17, 2026, GSA published a materially revised draft of GSAR clause 552.239-7001, "Basic Safeguarding of Data within Large Language Model Artificial Intelligence Systems," in the Federal Register. The clause is significantly narrower than the March draft that alarmed industry, but its obligations are substantive. (Jones Walker Law, Jun 19) (JD Supra, Jun 19)

Scope: Applies when government data is actively processed by an LLM as part of contract performance. Embedded LLMs in word processors or navigation tools are excluded. Key obligations: (1) government owns all prompts, queries, responses, and derived data; (2) contractors may not use government data to train or improve AI models; (3) AI systems may not refuse outputs based on the contractor's "discretionary policies"; (4) a tiered supply chain framework — LLM Developers, System Operators, System Integrators, Service Providers — with tailored flow-down requirements at each tier.

Timeline: Comment period closes August 3, 2026. Public listening session at GWU Law / virtual: July 14, 11 AM–2 PM ET (registration closes July 3). GSA has signaled it intends to move quickly; a 60-day acceptance window after final rule adoption will leave little runway.

What This Means For Changeis

This is the most consequential federal AI acquisition development of 2026 for Changeis. As a federal IT services contractor, any contract where Changeis uses LLM-based tools to perform work — document review, analysis, code generation, chatbot delivery — is likely covered. Priority actions: (1) audit current and pipeline contracts for LLM use-case exposure; (2) review AI vendor commercial terms for conflicts with the "no training," "eyes-off," and "no refusals" requirements; (3) submit written comments before August 3 to shape final rule language, particularly around the no-refusals provision that is technically unimplementable with current commercial model guardrails; (4) register for the July 14 listening session. The DC Circuit dispute between Anthropic and DoD on the no-refusals requirement may resolve this tension — monitor closely.

NSPM-11 · DoD · Autonomous Weapons
NSPM-11 Sets 90-Day Clock on DoD Directive 3000.09 Rewrite

President Trump signed National Security Presidential Memorandum 11, "Artificial Intelligence in the National Security Enterprise," on June 5. The directive mandates the Pentagon to revise Directive 3000.09 — which governs lethal autonomous weapons and human-in-the-loop requirements — within 90 days (deadline: early September 2026), and rescinds Biden's NSM-25. (GovConFeed, Jun 15) (Morgan Lewis GovCon, Jun 16)

NSPM-11 also directs the establishment of an AI National Security Strategic Reserve — a pool of non-governmental AI talent that federal agencies can draw upon for AI-related missions. More than a dozen agencies are named participants, including DHS, DNI, CIA, FBI, State, and Treasury. Senator Ruben Gallego (D-AZ) sent a June 12 letter to SECDEF Hegseth demanding answers on civilian harm mitigation adequacy by June 26; if gaps are revealed, congressional conditions may attach to defense appropriations.

What This Means For Changeis

The 90-day Directive 3000.09 rewrite will shift contracting vehicles, program requirements, and technical standards for autonomous systems work before summer's end. Changeis should: (1) review any DoD contracts or proposals that reference the existing directive — they may require modifications; (2) monitor the Pentagon's June 26 Gallego response for signals about what changes are coming; (3) explore positioning for the AI National Security Strategic Reserve, which is a direct market signal for consulting and staffing firms with specialized AI capability; (4) begin early engagement with DHS and DNI contacts, both of which are NSPM-11 named participants, to understand implementation timelines and requirements.

EO 14409 · Frontier Models · NSA Review
EO 14409 Conditions Frontier Model Deployment on NSA Pre-Release Access

Executive Order 14409, "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security," signed June 2 and published in the Federal Register June 5, is generating a series of agency implementation deadlines through August 2026. The order is the first federal directive to condition AI model market access on prior government review — specifically, up to 30 days of NSA prerelease access before any "covered frontier model" can be publicly deployed. (JD Supra, Jun 15) (Innovation Attorney, Jun 17)

The order also directs Commerce, NSA, and CISA to establish an AI cybersecurity clearinghouse within 60 days (deadline: early August 2026), and requires a classified benchmarking process to assess advanced cyber capabilities of AI models within 60 days. The Commerce Department has separately issued the first targeted export controls on specific frontier models — Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 — under this authority, with the DC Circuit on expedited review.

What This Means For Changeis

For federal BD, EO 14409 is primarily a vendor-landscape story: any AI product Changeis proposes for a federal client that uses a "covered frontier model" may face a 30-day deployment delay for NSA review. Changeis should flag this risk in proposals where rapid AI deployment timelines are promised. The AI cybersecurity clearinghouse (due August 2026) is also a potential engagement opportunity — CISA is one of Changeis's target agencies.

Congress · Great American AI Act
Bipartisan Great American AI Act Advances; Gottheimer Introduces Parallel Vetting Bill

The bipartisan Great American AI Act of 2026 — the most substantive piece of federal AI legislation ever drafted — is in discussion-period through July, targeting "large frontier developers" (companies with $500M+ revenue that have trained frontier-class models). Requirements include transparency reports, a published frontier AI framework, critical safety incident reporting, whistleblower protections, and independent auditing. (FAQ.com, Jun 15) (Goodwin Law, Jun 15)

Separately, Rep. An anti-deepfake bill also advanced to the Senate floor on June 18. (Politico, Jun 17) (Politico, Jun 18)

The EU Parliament on June 16 adopted the Digital Omnibus on AI (423-57) — the EU AI Act update — with watermarking obligations effective December 2, 2026. (iubenda, Jun 19)

2. AI Cybersecurity — CISA / CVEs
Action Required — Federal Deadline Monday June 22

LiteLLM CVE-2026-42271 (CVSS 8.7): CISA BOD 26-04 patch deadline expires Monday June 22. Any federal contractor running LiteLLM as an AI gateway must upgrade to v1.83.7+ (and Starlette 1.0.1+) and rotate all backend LLM API keys. Affected versions: 1.74.2 through 1.83.6. Do not defer — CISA has confirmed active exploitation in the wild.

CISA · BOD 26-04 · LiteLLM Deadline Jun 22
LiteLLM RCE Chain (CVE-2026-42271 + CVE-2026-48710) Under Active Exploitation — Federal Deadline Monday

CISA added CVE-2026-42271, a command injection vulnerability in LiteLLM's MCP server preview endpoints, to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on June 8, with a June 22 remediation deadline under BOD 26-04. The flaw allows any authenticated user — including holders of low-privilege internal keys — to execute arbitrary commands on the proxy host. Chained with CVE-2026-48710 (Starlette host-header bypass, CVSS 6.5), the attack becomes unauthenticated RCE. (Latest Hacking News, Jun 16) (SecurityAgainstAI, Jun 18)

This is LiteLLM's second CISA KEV entry in 31 days (first was CVE-2026-42208, SQL injection, added May 8). The Obsidian Security full vulnerability chain — covering CVE-2026-47101, CVE-2026-40217, CVE-2026-42208, and CVE-2026-42271 — requires LiteLLM v1.83.14-stable or later for complete closure. (Penligent Security, Jun 18) (Sovereign GRC, Jun 15)

What This Means For Changeis

If Changeis or any of its clients use LiteLLM as an AI gateway or proxy (common in multi-model enterprise AI deployments), this is a same-day action item before Monday EOD. An LiteLLM gateway is the worst possible compromise target because it concentrates all downstream LLM API credentials in one place. Patch protocol: (1) upgrade to v1.83.7+ immediately; (2) rotate every provider API key (OpenAI, Anthropic, Azure OpenAI, Bedrock, Vertex); (3) rotate the LiteLLM master key and all virtual team keys; (4) block the /mcp-rest/test/ endpoints at the network perimeter; (5) audit access logs for POST requests to those endpoints. BOD 26-04's 3-day mandate for the highest-risk KEVs is now the de facto compliance baseline for all federal contractors, not just FCEB agencies.

CISA · BOD 26-04 · AI-Accelerated Threats
CISA BOD 26-04: 3-Day Patch Mandate Cites AI-Accelerated Exploitation

CISA's Binding Operational Directive 26-04, issued June 10, is the most aggressive federal patch mandate in history — replacing the 14-day and 30-day timelines of BOD 19-02 and BOD 22-01 with a four-variable risk matrix. Vulnerabilities meeting all four criteria (publicly exposed asset, KEV status, automatable exploit, full system control impact) must be remediated within three calendar days. CISA explicitly cited AI-accelerated exploitation — which compresses the time from patch release to weaponized exploit from weeks to hours — as the central driver. (RapidFort, Jun 15) (GovCon Wire, Jun 19)

The 2026 projected CVE count is tracking toward 66,000 (a record, per Help Net Security), driven in part by AI-assisted vulnerability discovery. June's Patch Tuesday addressed 200 CVEs — the largest single-month release in the program's history — including a Critical RCE in M365 Copilot (CVE-2026-45497) and a GitHub Copilot credential disclosure flaw. (Brinqa, Jun 16)

Also added to KEV on June 18: CVE-2026-20253 (Splunk Enterprise, CVSS high — first Splunk KEV ever) with a three-day deadline ending June 21. LangGraph (46M+ monthly downloads) had three chained CVEs patched in June; enterprise teams running self-hosted deployments should upgrade to langgraph-checkpoint-sqlite 3.0.1+ and langgraph 1.0.10+. (AI Weekly, Jun 19) (Cognoscere, Jun 16)
