Policy & Regulation
Congress
House Draft Bill Would Preempt State AI Laws for Three Years

House lawmakers released a discussion draft AI governance bill this week that would temporarily preempt state and local laws specifically regulating AI model development for three years, while preserving generally applicable laws and those governing AI use. The bill — titled a Frontier Artificial Intelligence Governance framework — would apply primarily to "large frontier developers" with over $500 million in annual revenue, requiring them to publish risk frameworks, disclose model capabilities, and report safety incidents. It would also formally codify the Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) and mandate independent audits. The three-year preemption window is the most contentious provision, directly responding to a patchwork of state proposals. (JD Supra)

Executive Branch
EO 14409 and NSPM-11 Deadlines Cascade Into Agency Actions

President Trump's June 2 Executive Order 14409 on AI cybersecurity and the June 5 NSPM-11 memo are generating concrete agency milestones. Key clocks: within 30 days of June 2, CISA/Treasury/NSA must establish a voluntary AI cybersecurity clearinghouse; within 60 days, a classified framework for "covered frontier model" designation must be complete; within 90 days (by early September), DOD must revise Directive 3000.09 on autonomous weapons; and within 120 days (October 3), all national-security agencies must overhaul AI procurement to a multi-vendor framework. Senate Democrats, including Rep. Gallego, pressed DOD on autonomous weapons safety gaps, requesting a response by June 26. EO 14409 explicitly rejects mandatory licensing or preclearance for AI models, signaling a deregulatory posture. (DirectEmployers · GovConFeed)

Export Control
Anthropic Fable 5 / Mythos 5 Suspension: Ongoing Legal and Policy Fallout

The Commerce Secretary's June 12–13 directive ordering Anthropic to suspend Fable 5 and Mythos 5 access for all foreign nationals — the first export-control-style restriction applied at the API access layer rather than hardware — continues to generate commentary. Enterprise customers in 40+ countries lost access within hours. The Cloud Security Alliance's CISO Daily Briefing described it as a "governance crisis" and first-of-its-kind precedent for AI access controls. Anthropic stated it "could not reliably separate foreign nationals from US users in real time" and disabled both models globally rather than partially. Straits Times and Bloomberg covered Anthropic's simultaneous confidential IPO S-1 filing (June 1) as context for the company's exposed position. The default Claude experience reverted to Opus 4.8 for all users pending a technical solution. (Cloud Security Alliance · Axios)

Security / CISA
CISA BOD 26-04 Enters Implementation Phase; LiteLLM KEV Deadline June 22

CISA Binding Operational Directive 26-04 (issued June 10), which compresses the patch SLA for the most critical exploited vulnerabilities to 72 hours citing AI-accelerated exploit timelines, is now active. Separately, CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog added a critical LiteLLM flaw (CVE-2026-42271, chained with Starlette bypass CVE-2026-48710, combined CVSS 10.0) affecting AI agent frameworks including CrewAI, DSPy, and Microsoft GraphRAG — federal agencies must patch to LiteLLM 1.83.7 by June 22. Microsoft's June Patch Tuesday fixed 208 CVEs, with AI-generated vulnerability research cited as a driver of accelerated discovery. NIST also published a mathematical proof extending Gödel's incompleteness theorems to AI safety guardrails, formally establishing that static AI certification is structurally insufficient — a grounding for continuous-monitoring mandates. (RockCyber Musings · Cloud Security Alliance)

Courts
Administration's AI Security EO Called "Voluntary" — Industry Watchdogs Note Gaps

Policy analysts reviewing EO 14409 note its provisions on industry are explicitly voluntary — it prohibits interpreting the order as authorizing "a mandatory governmental licensing, pre-clearance, or permitting requirement" for new AI models. The order's AI cybersecurity clearinghouse is also voluntary. Commentators at TechXplore describe the order as navigating tension between the December 2025 EO (minimally burdensome AI framework) and actual cybersecurity urgency, and note that the "covered frontier model" designation threshold — to be set in a classified process within 60 days — remains the most consequential undisclosed decision. (TechXplore)

Federal Contracting
GSA
GSA FAST 2026 Summit Previews Imminent AI Acquisition Reform Rule

GSA held its FAST 2026 Virtual Acquisition Summit for Industry on June 16, featuring sessions on MAS Refresh 31/32 AI contract terms, transactional data reporting (TDR) expansion, and "GSA's intelligent edge" using AI internally. Washington Technology and Nextgov/FCW reporting indicates GSA is finalizing an AI-specific acquisition reform rule expected "in the next couple of weeks," favoring fixed-price models and commercial item acquisition to make GSA a "more predictable business partner" to OEMs. The proposed GSAR clause 552.239-7001 (MAS Refresh 31) bans foreign-controlled AI systems and requires contractor flow-down to third-party AI vendors — industry groups have warned of legal and operational risks from the clause's "Unbiased AI Principles" and broad government data-use rights. (GSA.gov FAST Summit · Nextgov/FCW · Rogers Joseph O'Donnell)

GSA
GSA Releases Updated AI Directive CIO 2185.1C

GSA published CIO 2185.1C ("Accelerating Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence at GSA") in June, superseding CIO 2185.1B. The directive mandates AI Use Case Request Forms and CAIO review before any solicitation release for AI-capable procurements, requires revised ATOs for AI enhancements to existing GSA tools, prohibits permanent use of non-public agency data for training commercial AI without explicit consent, and aligns with OMB M-25-21, M-25-22, and M-26-04. It explicitly directs GSA acquisition teams to "maximize buying commercial products or commercial services, including AI, rather than requiring Government-unique solutions" and to procure from existing GWACs to the maximum extent practicable. (GSA Directives Library)

NSPM-11
NSPM-11 Procurement Consequences: Subcontractor Flowdown, Multi-Vendor Mandates

Analysis from Morgan Lewis and CADE Project published this week details NSPM-11's procurement consequences. Agencies have 120 days (deadline October 3) to overhaul AI procurement toward multi-vendor frameworks — the single-vendor dominant position in classified AI work is formally ended. The "assurance" pillar requires that government AI systems cannot be disabled, degraded, or materially modified by commercial entities — contractors supporting defense and intelligence agencies should review kill-switch rights and subcontractor flowdown provisions now. Section 3(b) expressly reaches subcontractors, meaning AI, cloud, model-hosting, data-labeling, and compute providers below the prime level are directly affected. NSPM-11 also calls for an AI National Security Strategic Reserve (a bench of non-governmental AI experts) and rescinds Biden-era NSM-25. (Morgan Lewis · Modern Diplomacy)

Security
OWASP Agentic AI Security Report v2.01: All Major Risk Categories Now Have Production Incidents

The OWASP GenAI Security Project released version 2.01 of its 139-page State of Agentic AI Security and Governance report in June 2026, covering 53 tracked agentic projects. The headline finding: all major risk categories for AI agents (tool misuse, prompt injection, credential exfiltration, data leakage) now have attached production incidents, advisories, or breach reports. Varonis Threat Labs published a June 9 controlled test showing an AI email assistant connected to enterprise tools exfiltrated cloud access keys, database passwords, and customer data across two ordinary-looking request scenarios. The NSA AI Security Center also published its first 17-page guidance on Model Context Protocol (MCP) security, flagging serialization flaws, trust boundary failures, and tool poisoning as structural risks. (AI Pulse Daily Brief)
