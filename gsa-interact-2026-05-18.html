OASIS+ Phase II May 12, 2026 Source: GSA OASIS+ Program Page (updated May 12, 2026)

OASIS+ Phase II: First Rolling Apparent Awardee Announcements Posted — NTPs Expected May 22

On May 12, 2026, GSA's Office of Acquisition Solutions Development posted the first OASIS+ Phase II rolling apparent awardee announcements on SAM.gov and the OASIS+ Solicitations (continuously open) page. Formal awards and Notices to Proceed are planned on or about May 22, 2026, pending final legal reviews. The Phase II program uses a rolling award model across 13 service domains and six contract pools (Unrestricted, Total Small Business, 8(a), HUBZone, SDVOSB, WOSB). Unlike traditional IDIQ competitions, there is no ceiling on the number of awards — proposals continue to be evaluated in order of receipt. The Management & Advisory, Human Capital, Business Administration, and Financial Services domains are now in the first award tranche.

Why it matters for Changeis: OASIS+ Phase II's first awardees will be accessible via the posted contractor list (updated weekly on gsa.gov/oasis-plus). Changeis should immediately review the list to identify potential teaming partners already awarded in the Management & Advisory and Human Capital domains — especially those who may need qualified subcontractors with Changeis's 541611 capabilities. If Changeis is evaluating a Phase II submission, the continuous on-ramp remains open and NTPs will begin flowing May 22 — meaning competitors are now able to bid on OASIS+ task orders. This creates urgency to either submit to the Phase II on-ramp or formalize teaming arrangements with Phase II awardees.
Executive Order April 30, 2026 (industry impact this week) Source: White House — Fixed-Price Contracting EO (Apr 30, 2026)

Fixed-Price Contracting EO: Agencies Must Justify T&M / Cost-Type Contracts Above $10M — FAR Amendments by August

President Trump's April 30, 2026 Executive Order "Promoting Efficiency, Accountability, and Performance in Federal Contracting" establishes fixed-price contracts as the government's default procurement method. T&M, labor-hour, and cost-reimbursement contracts now require written justification by the contracting officer — and agency head written approval when contract value exceeds $10M (for civilian agencies), $25M (DHS), $35M (NASA), or $100M (DoW). Agencies must review and seek to renegotiate their ten largest cost-type contracts by July 29, 2026. OMB must issue government-wide implementing guidance by June 14. OFPP and the FAR Council must propose FAR amendments by August 28. Solicitations issued before formal FAR amendments may already begin incorporating deviations under agency-specific authority.

Why it matters for Changeis: Professional services task orders under MAS SINs 541611 and 54151S are often structured as T&M or labor-hour to accommodate knowledge-work pricing. The EO creates an approval barrier above $10M for these contract types — contracting officers will increasingly push for FFP, Firm Fixed-Price Level of Effort (FFP-LOE), or performance-based approaches. Changeis should: (1) prepare FFP/FFP-LOE pricing structures for all active and pipeline opportunities; (2) identify which current or prospective agency customers have large T&M vehicles likely to be restructured by July 29; and (3) position management consulting deliverables as measurable outcomes rather than labor categories where possible. This is a structural BD shift with a 90-day timeline.
FedRAMP May 4, 2026 Source: FedRAMP Blog — CR26 Public Preview (May 4, 2026)

FedRAMP Consolidated Rules 2026 (CR26): Public Preview Live — New Certification Classes, 20x Timelines, Hiring Push

FedRAMP launched a public preview of its Consolidated Rules for 2026 (CR26) on May 4, with the final rules to be published by end of June 2026 and taking effect July 1. CR26 is valid through December 31, 2028, providing a fixed stability window. Key changes: FedRAMP "authorizations" are renamed to "certifications" (all authorized products are now FedRAMP Certified); impact levels transition to Certification Classes (Class A: new low baseline; B: LI-SaaS/Low; C: Moderate; D: High). FedRAMP templates are being replaced by machine-readable structured requirements and human-readable summaries. The rules include explicit timelines for Rev5 updates and will define FedRAMP 20x general availability. Simultaneously, FedRAMP opened hiring for its new Cybersecurity Service — 4 GS-15 Lead Cloud Security Engineers (USAJOBS posting 867607100), with GS-14 and GS-13 positions following in coming weeks.

Why it matters for Changeis: If Changeis advises clients on cloud service procurement or FedRAMP compliance under MAS SIN 54151S, the CR26 naming and classification changes require immediate updates to capability statements and proposals — "FedRAMP Authorized" should now be referred to as "FedRAMP Certified." The shift to machine-readable requirements and OSCAL-native artifacts is an advisory opportunity: agencies and cloud service providers will need compliance guidance as they transition from manual documentation to structured formats. The OSCAL machine-readable deadline (September 2026 for existing Rev5 authorizations) creates a near-term consulting demand spike. The CR26 public preview is open for comment via GitHub Discussions at fedramp.gov/preview/2026.
GSA AI Announced this week — Event June 3, 2026 Source: GSA AI Symposium Event Page

GSA AI Symposium — June 3, 2026: Compliant AI Acquisition, Deployment, and Security for Federal Contractors

GSA announced its AI Symposium on June 3, 2026 (virtual, free) themed "Accelerating Gen AI Adoption: Strategies for Success." The symposium features keynote addresses, panel discussions, and hands-on breakout sessions covering the full generative AI lifecycle — from foundational concepts to real-world federal implementation. Topics include responsible deployment, data usage and privacy, security considerations, and how to acquire AI capabilities in a compliant, mission-aligned way. Attendees will develop skills in prompt engineering, model selection, and AI decision-making. The event is open to federal employees, federal contractors, state/local government, military, and academia. GSA's AI positioning is reinforced by its March 2026 NIST partnership (GSA + NIST/CAISI) to develop federal AI evaluation standards and the ongoing GSAR 552.239-7001 AI clause (deferred to Refresh 32). Contact: aicop@gsa.gov.

Why it matters for Changeis: Changeis's positioning on MAS SIN 54151S (IT Services) and 541611 (Management Consulting) gives it a natural advisory role in federal AI adoption. The GSA AI Symposium is the most direct pipeline intelligence available before GSA's formal AI task orders begin flowing through the FAS Transform office. Attendance positions Changeis to understand agency AI readiness gaps — directly actionable as consulting engagements. Additionally, the GSAR 552.239-7001 AI clause (coming in Refresh 32) will impose disclosure, "American AI" usage, and government data protection requirements — contractors using AI in performance must begin preparing compliance frameworks now, before the clause is finalized.
