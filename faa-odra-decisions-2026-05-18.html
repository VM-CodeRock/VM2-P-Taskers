2. New Critical Intelligence — Protest of Cavan Solutions (2026)
NEW THIS WEEK 2026 WL 1284037 (O.D.R.A. 2026)  ·  SETIS Program  ·  Published: May 15, 2026 (via NLR/JD Supra)
Protest of Cavan Solutions, Inc.
Protester: Cavan Solutions, Inc.  |  Intervenor: Advanced Sciences & Technology LLC (AS&T)  |  Contract Vehicle: SETIS IDIQ  |  Task Order: ~$23.8M  |  Issue: OCI + Unenforceable No-Protest Clause
Precedent-Setting — Protest Moot (Corrective Action) + Clause Struck

BID PROTEST IT / SYSTEMS ENGINEERING OCI — FORMER EMPLOYEES ATEPS-II RELEVANT

Background

Cavan Solutions and Advanced Sciences & Technology, LLC (AS&T) both hold IDIQ contracts under the FAA's Systems Engineering and Technical Innovative Solutions (SETIS) program — an IDIQ vehicle structurally analogous to ATEPS-II. After the FAA product team awarded a SETIS task order valued at approximately $23.8M to AS&T, Cavan filed an initial and supplemental protest alleging organizational conflicts of interest (OCI): former FAA employees had migrated to AS&T and used their insider knowledge to unfairly influence the competition.

Core Outcome — OCI / Corrective Action

Following investigation, the contracting officer found "ample evidence" of an "unmitigable, unavoidable OCI." The FAA product team voluntarily excluded AS&T from the competition, terminated its task order, and re-awarded to Cavan — providing complete relief. ODRA dismissed the protest as moot. Cavan's request for enhanced remedies (IG referral, debarment proceedings against AS&T) was denied because the agency acted in good faith and took voluntary corrective action without being compelled.

Precedent-Setting Holding — No-Protest Clause (Clause G.8.3) Unenforceable

The SETIS contracts include Clause G.8.3, providing that contracting officer task order award decisions are "final and conclusive" and non-protestable, except where the task order value exceeds $25M or where the protest concerns the contract's scope, period, or ceiling. The product team and AS&T argued this clause stripped ODRA of jurisdiction over the sub-$25M protest. ODRA reaffirmed its earlier ruling and held the clause unenforceable on three independent grounds:

(a) 49 U.S.C. § 40110(d)(4): The FAA's organic bid protest statute mandates that bid protests "shall" be adjudicated through ODRA Dispute Resolution Officers. A contract clause stripping ODRA of jurisdiction directly violates this mandatory statutory language.

(b) FASA Inapplicability to FAA: DOD and civilian agencies may restrict task order protests under 10 U.S.C. § 3406(f) and 41 U.S.C. § 4106(f) (both FASA provisions). But Congress expressly exempted FAA from FASA via 49 U.S.C. § 40110(d)(2). The FAA cannot rely on a statutory exception that Congress declined to extend to it.

(c) 5 U.S.C. § 575(a)(3): Clauses vesting "final and conclusive" authority without judicial review function as arbitration agreements (per United States v. Wunderlich, 342 U.S. 98 (1951)). Clause G.8.3 therefore violates the federal prohibition on agencies requiring consent to arbitration as a contract condition.

2016 Memorandum — Troubling Disclosure

The product team introduced a previously non-public 2016 memorandum signed by then-Administrator Huerta purporting to endorse the no-protest clause. ODRA was sharply critical: the memorandum (1) failed to disclose that other agencies' protest bars rest on express statutory authority inapplicable to FAA; (2) omitted 14 C.F.R. §§ 17.11 and 17.45; and (3) asserted without basis that ODRA would dismiss task order protests under the clause. ODRA's conclusion: "The Government of the United States does not work in this manner." Unpublished internal memoranda cannot override public statutes or foreclose contractor rights.

CRITICAL CHANGEIS IMPLICATION — ATEPS-II Task Order Protests: Changeis holds the ATEPS-II SDB1 IDIQ (Contract 693KA9-25-D-00004, $114.7M ceiling, awarded December 2024, ordering through December 2026). SAIC holds ATEPS-II unrestricted ($629.8M ceiling) and Noblis holds another unrestricted track ($709.1M). The Cavan Solutions ruling applies directly to ATEPS-II if the base contract or task order solicitations contain language equivalent to SETIS Clause G.8.3.

Immediate Actions Required: (1) Have Contracts / Legal pull the ATEPS-II base contract and all issued task order solicitations and search for any "final and conclusive" or "non-protestable" language — within 10 business days. (3) Equally, competitors (Cavan Solutions itself, other SETIS holders, other ATEPS-II holders) have this same right against Changeis-awarded task orders. (4) Review pending task order competitions for any clauses purporting to limit protest rights. 4. Key Themes & Patterns — Updated May 18, 2026
⚠
No-Protest Clauses Are Dead at FAA

Cavan Solutions eliminates any FAA IDIQ contract clause restricting task order protests on three independent statutory grounds. This is not limited to SETIS — it applies to ATEPS-II and any other FAA IDIQ vehicle. The FAA's own 2016 internal policy memorandum authorizing such clauses was repudiated. For Changeis, this is both an offensive right and a defensive exposure.

●
OCI Vigilance — Former Government Employees

The Cavan Solutions OCI pattern — former FAA employees moving to AS&T and tainting its proposal — is a recurring risk in the small FAA contractor community. Changeis hires and teams with FAA alumni. All hiring and teaming decisions should include an OCI screen against active FAA procurement activities.

▶
Good-Faith Corrective Action Protects Agencies

ODRA in Cavan Solutions declined to recommend IG referral or debarment because the product team cooperated, investigated thoroughly, and took voluntary corrective action. The contrast with IBEX Weather Services (where the product team persisted despite a clear record) is instructive. For Changeis program delivery: document any CO/COR course corrections in writing to demonstrate good-faith contract administration.

◆
ODRA Transparency Push

ODRA rejected the FAA product team's request to suppress the public version of its denial-of-motions order, citing DOT guidance encouraging publication of formal adjudication decisions on public legal databases. This signals ODRA's intent to maintain a stronger public record — meaning more decisions will be accessible and searchable going forward, reducing the impact of the registry publication lag.

■
Publication Lag Remains — Legal Press Fills Gap

The ODRA public registry has not updated since March 2025 (15+ months). Cavan Solutions was decided in 2026 and published via National Law Review and JD Supra on May 15, 2026 — but does not yet appear on the public registry. VM2 monitoring of legal press (NLR, JD Supra, Bradley Arant, PilieroMazza) is essential to catching new decisions before they appear on the public page.

✔
ATEPS-II Now Fully Open to Protest Competition

With SAIC ($629.8M), Noblis ($709.1M), and Changeis ($114.7M) all holding ATEPS-II positions, and the Cavan Solutions ruling eliminating any contractual protest bar, every ATEPS-II task order competition is now a potential ODRA protest scenario. Changeis must invest in evaluation-defensible proposals, robust debriefing processes, and a standing ODRA protest-readiness capability.
