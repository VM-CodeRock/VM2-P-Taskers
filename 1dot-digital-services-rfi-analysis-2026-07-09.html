Strategic Context

1DOT Digital Services operationalizes the DOT 2025–2029 IT Strategic Plan, transitioning the Department from decentralized "Shadow IT" toward a unified, product-centric "1DOT IT Digital Factory" model. The PWS explicitly instructs contractors to adopt a "Builder Mentality" — engineering reusable, cross-modal solutions rather than one-off, siloed systems — and to comply with the DOT DevSecOps Playbook and U.S. Digital Service (USDS) Playbook. Strategic outcomes called out in the PWS include: eliminating redundant Shadow IT, realizing cost savings below standard GSA Schedule rates, accelerating Procurement Administrative Lead Time (PALT), applying Human-Centered Design and journey mapping, driving cloud-native (FedRAMP) transformation, mitigating technical debt, and enforcing DevSecOps-based cybersecurity.

PWS Scope Summary

The Draft PWS establishes a Department-wide vehicle for full-lifecycle software engineering and digital services delivery, centered on developing, enhancing, and maintaining a unified DOT application portfolio ("one best-in-class product for each job or use case"). Work spans three scales:

  • Enterprise-scale — foundational shared services used department-wide
  • Cross-modal — digital services fulfilling common functions across two or more Operating Administrations (e.g., registration, permitting, regulatory inspection, administrative enforcement)
  • Modal-level — maintenance/modernization unique to a single mission area

Exclusions: standalone enterprise cybersecurity operations, exclusive enterprise infrastructure engineering, and standalone commercial software/license purchases (unless incidental to an order).

Key Personnel, Clearances & Governance
  • Security clearances: Task Area 7.5 (Classified Services) requires the Contractor to recruit, deploy, and maintain personnel with active Secret, Top Secret (TS), and/or SCI eligibility as defined at the order level, with continuous eligibility and periodic reinvestigation compliance. Work may occur in SCIFs and on closed networks (JWICS/SIPRNet) requiring cross-domain/air-gapped code transfer.
  • DPAS rating: Not explicitly stated in the Draft PWS or RFI as reviewed; no DX-rated priority designation identified. Changeis should confirm this in its RFI questions if DPAS applicability is unclear for national-security-adjacent task orders.
  • Key personnel / staffing: No named labor-category key-personnel roster in the draft; instead, the PWS requires a primary point of contact (Application-Level Program Manager) per BPA engagement, "Technical Leadership and Staffing" commitments, and workforce flexibility to respond to changing order requirements with minimal downtime.
  • Governance framework: All work must align with DOT's Enterprise Program Management Review (EPMR) methodology plus DOT IT policies on cybersecurity, privacy, records management, and accessibility.
  • Transition-in requirements: Not separately specified at the BPA level in the draft (transition activities are referenced generally under Task Area 7.9 "Transition Management — System Deployment and Field Support" and are expected to be defined at the order level, especially for orders that transition work from expiring SWES task orders).
  • Delivery/capacity model: The Government may use a capacity-based ordering model — defined-size scrum teams with a stabilized labor-category mix, delivering against a cadence-based backlog rather than milestone schedules.
